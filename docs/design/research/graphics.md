## 0. Where the web build already is (read from the repo, not the web)

Read from `web/src/engine/{renderer,materials,lighting,quality}.js` and `web/tools/bake/light.mjs`:

- Five tiers Max / High / Medium / Low / Potato with dynamic resolution (Potato: 50-70 % of 720p).
- Baked light is PER VERTEX (big faces subdivided into a grid), BVH-traced in Node with soft shadows and short-ray AO,
  plus flicker groups. There is no bounce (indirect) light in the bake: `light.mjs` has no bounce/indirect pass; the
  "ambient fill" stands in for it.
- Probe grid (irradiance only) for dynamic things.
- Diffuse is Lambert; specular is an ad-hoc sheen from generated roughness / normal maps under the torch. No
  environment / reflection probes ("reflectance" exists as a material scalar).
- Tone mapping is ACES (Narkowicz fit) INSIDE every material, gamma encoded, so render targets are 8-bit and bloom
  works on display values.
- Torch: two spot cones, shadow map 2048/1024/1024/512/none, Poisson PCF; ray-marched torch volume at Max/High.
- SSAO (Max full-res, High half-res), bloom chain, FXAA, chroma, grain, vignette, grade per level, panic DOF.
- Grime/soot multiply decals ("wear") and decal atlases; textures generated by Pillow as WebP colour/normal/ORM at
  2048/1024/512/256.

So the base is already strong. The realism gaps that matter most are (I): no bounced light, vertex-resolution
lighting on box geometry, no specular reflections of the environment, 8-bit LDR pipeline, generated (not scanned)
albedo, and sharp unbevelled box edges. Sections below say what the sources show and what to do.

## 1. What makes the reference games look real, technique by technique

### 1a. Counter-Strike 2 / Source 2 (Valve developer wiki)

- [S-search] Indirect light is BAKED: into lightmaps for static surfaces and into light-probe volumes for dynamic
  objects. Direct light is either fully baked or "indexed" (its shadow/visibility baked, its shading done live so it
  keeps a specular highlight; indexed lights cannot move and are limited in number). Baked with VRAD3 (GPU ray
  tracing in CS2 Hammer).
- [S-search] Specular reflection comes from PARALLAX-CORRECTED cubemaps (`env_cubemap_box`): a cubemap captured in
  a room and projected against that room's box, so reflections in a floor line up with the walls. Each mesh takes
  ONE cubemap; overlapping volumes resolve by priority. `env_combined_light_probe_volume` = probe volume + cubemap box
  in one entity, placed per room, near eye height in simple box rooms.
- [S-search] `light_environment` has a sky-bounce multiplier (CS2 only): the sky's bounce light is a separate,
  tunable term.
- [I] What the eye reads as "CS2-real" is therefore: soft bounced light filling every corner with colour from nearby
  surfaces, contact darkening where walls meet, and glossy floors that reflect the room's lights in the right place.
  None of that is expensive at run time; all of it is pre-computed.

### 1b. Resident Evil 7 / Village (Capcom RE Engine)

- [S-search, CEDEC 2016 coverage, not GDC] RE7 used photogrammetry for a large share of assets (one report: "a little
  over half"), for speed as much as fidelity; target 1080p60 with VR.
- [S-search, CG World JP] RE7 used probe-based indirect lighting rather than lightmaps. A claim that this saved ~2/3
  memory comes from a blog [U].
- [S-search] Village adds ray-traced GI and reflections on PC as options; later RE Engine titles (Requiem, GDC 2026)
  move to full path tracing. Not reachable in a browser; the transferable lesson is the probe grid for anything that
  moves and scanned albedo for anything static.

### 1c. Metal Gear Solid V / P.T. (Fox Engine, GDC 2013 "Photorealism Through the Eyes of a FOX")

- [S-search] Linear lighting workflow; physically based shading with roughness as the central parameter; textures and
  models from photo scans (PhotoScan) so colour and shape match; deferred renderer for many lights; simulated camera
  (depth of field, bloom, motion blur).
- [S-search, reported quote] "Being correct and physically realistic does not mean a game will look good": the
  photoreal result came from artists grading and composing on top of correct physics. P.T. ran on this engine; its
  look is one small, densely dressed corridor under a few lights [I, from the game itself].

### 1d. Alien: Isolation (Creative Assembly, own engine)

- [S-search] Deferred lighting with many dynamic lights, chosen so steam and corridor lights react correctly; GPU
  particles colliding with the G-buffer; post-processing imitating late-1970s film stock: film grain, chromatic
  aberration, lens flare (all user-adjustable). Lighting matched to the practical lights of the film's sets.
- [I] Lesson: realism in a dark interior is mostly PRACTICAL LIGHTS (every light has a visible fixture that motivates
  it), haze that catches them, and a consistent camera/film model over everything.

### 1e. Outlast (Red Barrels, UE3)

- [S-search, developer retrospective excerpt] The camcorder exists so the player can see in the dark (night vision
  drains batteries). [S-search, fan analysis, not developer] The night-vision look is several post passes, not a green
  tint; eyes use an emissive that only blooms in the camcorder view.
- [I] Lesson for ROVLOC's torch: the light source the player carries is the main lighting design tool; the dark must
  be actually dark (low ambient), with the torch's hot centre, falloff and shadow doing the work.

### 1f. Unrecord / "bodycam" games (DRAMA, UE5)

- [S-search] UE5 with Lumen GI; commentators attribute the realism to "lighting and the camera"; the camera is a
  slightly warped wide lens; early demo used Unreal Marketplace assets (so not bespoke scans). No developer technical
  breakdown found [U for any specific numbers].
- [I] The camera does as much as the scene: wide FOV with barrel distortion, auto-exposure that blows out windows and
  lags, motion blur, sensor noise, compression-like softness, and a handheld body motion. These are cheap post
  effects. They also hide the flaws of simple geometry. A full "bodycam" filter is a style choice, not a requirement;
  a mild version (subtle distortion, exposure adaptation, head bob that follows the gait) transfers well.

### 1g. Texel density and materials (engine docs)

- [S-search, CRYENGINE docs] Crysis 3 / Ryse era: most assets aimed at ~512 texels per metre, checked with a debug
  view. [S-search, Maxon C4D docs] Guideline 10.24 px/cm (1024 px/m) for first-person, 5.12 px/cm for third-person.
- [I] ROVLOC is first person and the player presses against walls and lockers: aim 1024 px/m at Max for walls and
  floors the player touches (a 2048 texture tiling every 2 m), 512 px/m at High/Medium, 256 at Low, 128 at Potato.
  In studs (1 stud = 0.28 m in Roblox convention) 1024 px/m is ~287 px per stud.

### 1h. Wall design, trims, bevels, decals ("the design of walls")

- [S-search, GDC 2015, Morten Olsen, Insomniac, "The Ultimate Trim: Texturing Techniques of Sunset Overdrive"] One
  standardised trim sheet: horizontal strips of increasing height, each with a 45-degree bevel baked into the NORMAL
  MAP along its edges, so flat geometry catches a highlight along every edge. Multiple trim heights keep texel density
  consistent. Goals stated: production speed, memory, performance. [S-search, community] Without decals or secondary
  shaders trims look repetitive; the Level Design Book suggests modelling real bevels today.
- [I] Why this matters for a box-built school: a real wall is never a plain plane meeting another plane. It has a
  skirting board (kick plate) at the bottom with scuffs, a chair rail or a tile band at hip height (school corridors
  are often painted block or glazed tile below, paint above), door frames with architraves, corner guards, a ceiling
  grid of acoustic tiles, conduit and pipes running along the top, outlets, fire-alarm pulls, notice boards, a
  continuous bevel/highlight on every edge, and dirt that collects where hands, feet and water go. "Wall design" in
  the reference games is these layers plus decals, not a better single texture.
- [I] Layer recipe used across the genre (and buildable from boxes): (1) base material at correct texel density;
  (2) a trim band and skirting as separate thin boxes with their own trim material; (3) bevel highlight: either tiny
  chamfer boxes on exposed corners, or a shader edge-highlight from the box's local UV distance-to-edge (no geometry
  cost); (4) world-space grime gradient: darker and dirtier near the floor and in corners (use the baked AO term as a
  mask, which ROVLOC already has); (5) decals: stains, scuffs, posters, water damage, graffiti, cracks, all ORIGINAL;
  (6) clutter in front of the wall (bins, chairs, boxes, cables) so the wall is never seen bare from end to end.

## 2. What Roblox's best-looking horror games do inside Roblox

Developer-written breakdowns for DOORS, Pressure, The Mimic and Apeirophobia were NOT found: the results are fan
wikis and SEO pages [U for anything game-specific]. What the official docs and DevForum state:

- [S-search, Roblox DevForum, July 2025] "Unified Lighting" replaced `Lighting.Technology` (Voxel / ShadowMap /
  Future) with `LightingStyle` (Soft / Realistic) plus `PrioritizeLightingQuality`. Realistic = local lights cast
  precise shadows and specular highlights (what Future was). Developers reported Realistic reaches less light range
  than old Future; Roblox said longer light ranges and attenuation would follow.
- [S-search, Roblox Creator Docs "Enhance indoor environments"] Set LightingStyle Realistic; raise
  `EnvironmentDiffuseScale` and `EnvironmentSpecularScale` (toward 1) so smooth and metal surfaces reflect the
  environment; enclose interiors with parts at least ~2.5 studs thick so outdoor light does not leak; built-in
  materials and SurfaceAppearance (colour, normal, roughness, metalness, emissive) are PBR. Local lights affect
  SurfaceAppearance only under Future/Realistic.
- [S-search, DevForum] For darkness: set the two Environment scales to 0 and drop Ambient/OutdoorAmbient so rooms
  can be pitch black; SurfaceLights read better than PointLights for fixtures; Future/Realistic is the main
  performance cost on weak devices (one report: 20-30 fps until the graphics slider drops below level 3, then 60).
- [S-search, fan wikis] DOORS uses LIGHT AS A WARNING LANGUAGE: a short flicker before one entity, a stronger
  pattern before another, rooms in pure darkness where a dark-only threat lives. ROVLOC's teleport warning (lights
  stutter, setGroupStutter) is the same technique and already in the engine; keep its signal consistent.
- [I] What the "realistic" Roblox showcases share: Realistic lighting with very low ambient, many small motivated
  lights (SurfaceLights in fixtures, Neon emitters), SurfaceAppearance PBR on meshes, Atmosphere density for haze,
  ColorCorrection/Bloom/SunRays/DepthOfField post, and heavy dressing with meshes. All of it maps onto parts ROVLOC's
  shared builder already emits; the Roblox port gets realism mostly from LightingStyle Realistic + SurfaceAppearance
  from the same CC0 scan textures + dressing parity with the web.

### 1i. Light representation, exposure, camera

- [S-search, MJP "SG Series part 1"; Bakery manual] A plain lightmap stores one colour per texel, so normal maps
  cannot respond to baked light. Fixes, cheapest first: a dominant-direction map (one extra texture; "basically a
  normal map alongside your lightmap"; small artefacts where the dominant direction flips at shadow edges), Valve's
  3-map Radiosity Normal Mapping (Half-Life 2 era, low angular resolution), 4-map per-pixel SH (better, approximate
  specular). Doom Eternal used 2-band SH [S-search, unverified first-hand].
- [S-search, Lagarde & de Rousiers, "Moving Frostbite to PBR", SIGGRAPH 2014] Physically based camera: exposure from
  aperture / shutter / ISO (EV100), max luminance ~1.2 x 2^EV100, validated with the Sunny-16 rule. [I] Auto exposure
  with a clamped range is what makes a torch-lit room read like footage: point the torch at a near wall and the
  dark behind gets darker, step from a lit corridor into a dark room and the eye takes a second to adapt.
- [S-search, forums] Flashlights: S.T.A.L.K.E.R. uses a projected "cookie" texture; the dark rings of good horror
  torches come from the cookie plus a shadow map; a second small light at the beam's hit point fakes bounced light;
  Left 4 Dead's weapon light is reportedly near-zero spill so things jump out. Free cookie packs exist on
  OpenGameArt, but ROVLOC should generate its own (licence of each pack varies, and it is a 256 px texture).

### 1j. Characters (skin, eyes, hair, animation)

- [S-search, Penner, SIGGRAPH 2011 Advances course] Pre-integrated skin: scattering baked into a lookup texture
  indexed by N.L and curvature; one texture fetch, no blur pass. Cheaper still: wrap lighting (GPU Gems ch. 16).
  Separable SSS (Jimenez) is two screen-space 1D blurs: Max tier only.
- [S-search, Unity HDRP eye docs and artist material] Eyes = two layers: wet cornea (sharp specular, refraction) over
  iris and sclera; games fake the refraction with parallax on one mesh. The bright catch-light and a wet line on the
  lower lid do most of the work [I].
- [S-search] MakeHuman exports from the official, unmodified build are CC0 (base mesh, targets, proxies since 2020);
  separately downloaded community assets are not. CMU mocap: described as free for any use and redistributable,
  NOT a CC0 deed; confirm terms at mocap.cs.cmu.edu before shipping [U]. Bandai Namco's mocap set and the SFU set are
  non-commercial; Mixamo's terms are unclear for this use: avoid all three.
- [S-search] ambientCG: every asset CC0 1.0 (official docs). Poly Haven: CC0 per its licence page (seen via a
  translated copy and third-party guides; English wording not seen first-hand) [U, low risk].

## 3. What three.js on WebGL2 can do today, with costs

Three.js facts below were checked in the installed three 0.186.1 [S-code]: `AgXToneMapping` and `NeutralToneMapping`
constants exist (AgX after Filament / iolite); `GTAOPass`, `SAOPass`, `SSAOPass`, `SSRPass`, `SMAAPass`,
`TAARenderPass`, `BokehPass`, `LUTPass`, `UnrealBloomPass` exist; `KTX2Loader` with the Basis transcoder
(`basis_transcoder.wasm` 527 KB + `.js` 57 KB, uncompressed on disk); `LightProbeGenerator`, `ProgressiveLightMap`
and `potpack` (rectangle packer) ship in examples; materials have `lightMap` / `aoMap` on a second UV channel
(`texture.channel = 1`, r151+). There is NO built-in box-projected (parallax-corrected) env map in the WebGL
renderer: it is a ~15-line GLSL patch (community gists, drei `useBoxProjectedEnv`, after Lagarde SIGGRAPH 2012).
ROVLOC uses its own ShaderMaterials, so every item below is a change to `materials.js` / `renderer.js`, not a pass
dropped in. Millisecond figures are my estimates [I] for a 720p internal frame on a Chromebook-class Intel UHD /
Mali iGPU and 1440p on a mid desktop GPU; no benchmark source was found, so measure before committing.

Tiers are Max / High / Medium / Low / Potato as in `quality.js`. "Chromebook" = Potato (and Low).

### 3.1 Bounced light in the bake (indirect / GI)  -- biggest single win
- Payoff: the "CS2 look": soft fill, colour bleeding (warm floor tint on walls, green from a green locker),
  corners that are dark because light cannot reach them, not because of a fill constant.
- Run time: zero on every tier (it lands in the same per-vertex RGB the shader already reads).
- Build: extend `tools/bake/light.mjs`: after direct light, gather 1-2 diffuse bounces with cosine-weighted BVH rays
  (64-256 per point) reading the previous pass's irradiance x albedo at the hit (an irradiance cache over the vertices
  or a coarse voxel grid). Same for probes. Bake time grows by roughly the bounce ray count / today's AO ray count
  (minutes, not hours, with the worker pool) [I].
- Pitfalls: flicker groups. Bounce must be split per group the same way direct light is (a power cut must also kill
  its bounce), or the dark rooms glow. Albedo must be the texture's mean colour x part colour, else bounce is
  over-bright. Clamp energy (albedo <= 0.8).

### 3.2 Texel lightmaps instead of vertex light (Max / High / Medium)
- Payoff: sharp-but-soft shadow edges from door frames, shelves and window bars, round light pools on floors,
  contact shadows under lockers: vertex light on subdivided boxes cannot hold these.
- Unwrap: boxes need no xatlas: each visible face is already a rectangle; give it width x height texels at the
  density, pad 2 px, pack with `potpack` per chunk [I]. xatlas (or Blender Smart UV) only for imported meshes.
- Density / size estimate [I]: a 400 x 288-stud level has ~115k stud^2 of floor, ~350k stud^2 of total visible
  surface. At 2 texels per stud (~14 cm per texel, coarser than CS2, fine with SSAO on top) that is ~1.4M texels
  + padding = one 2048^2 atlas per level; at 4 texels/stud, four. GPU memory per 2048^2 atlas: 16 MB as RGBA8,
  4 MB as BC7/ASTC via KTX2 UASTC. File: ~1-4 MB per atlas as RGBM in WebP/KTX2 [I].
- Encoding: RGBM or RGBE in 8-bit, or KTX2 UASTC HDR; keep the flicker group id + share in a second, nearest-
  filtered texture (or in the alpha of an RGBM-free half-float target on Max) so the flicker table still works.
- Chromebook: keep today's vertex light on Low / Potato (zero texture memory). The same bake produces both.
- Blender Cycles route: export the level (glTF), bake "Diffuse: direct + indirect" (or Combined) into a UV2 atlas,
  re-import. Pitfalls reported on the three.js forum: glTF has no lightmap slot (re-attach by name / use emissive),
  UV2 must not overlap, winding/flip mismatches. [I] It also loses the flicker-group split and the Roblox parity the
  in-house baker keeps; use it only for the opening's outdoor set if at all.

### 3.3 Reflection probes, box projected (wet floors, polished tile, lockers, glass)
- Payoff: polished school floors reflecting the strip lights in the right place is the single strongest "real" cue
  in a corridor after GI [I, consistent with Source 2 using parallax-corrected cubemaps everywhere].
- Build: at bake time, in the Playwright step that already exists, render a 128 px cube at the centre of each room /
  corridor segment with baked light only (no torch, no monster), prefilter roughness mips (PMREM-style), store
  RGBM WebP or KTX2. Per probe ~30-80 KB [I]; 30-60 probes per level = 1-4 MB.
- Shader: pick the probe for the chunk (nearest box containing the pixel), box-project R, sample mip by roughness,
  multiply by the baked occlusion and a Fresnel term. One cube fetch per pixel.
- Tiers: Max/High/Medium per-room probes; Low one probe per level; Potato none (today's sheen stays).
- Wet floors / puddles: a roughness mask decal (puddle = roughness 0.05, darker albedo) on the same probes; planar
  `Reflector` only for one hero surface in a cutscene (it renders the scene twice); `SSRPass` Max only if ever.
- Pitfalls: the torch is not in the probe, so a wet floor does not reflect the torch itself: add the analytic torch
  specular (already there) and a dynamic reflection of the torch's hot spot is not needed. The monster is also not in
  the probe: acceptable; it shows up in the analytic specular of fixtures if added.

### 3.4 The torch: cookie, physical falloff, bounce
- Payoff: the torch is what the player looks at for the whole game; an evenly lit disc reads "game", a beam with a
  hot core, a faint ring, lens dirt and soft spill reads "footage".
- Build: one generated 256^2 greyscale cookie (centre hot spot, one or two rings, dirty-lens blotches, all ORIGINAL),
  sampled in the spot cone's projected UV. Inverse-square falloff with a short range window. A small warm point light
  at the beam's hit point (one raycast per frame against the BVH the game already has) with ~5-10 % of the beam's
  energy tinted by the hit surface's albedo fakes torch bounce.
- Cost: one texture fetch in the torch term, every tier including Potato; one extra point-light slot (Potato has 2;
  use the second).
- Pitfalls: cookie must match the shadow map's projection; keep the hit-point light out of the shadow map.

### 3.5 HDR target, tone mapping (AgX option) and auto exposure
- Today: ACES inside each material, 8-bit targets. Payoff of moving to an RGBA16F scene target with tone mapping in
  the post pass: bloom from true HDR (fixtures glow without the whole wall blooming), AgX available, exposure
  adaptation possible.
- [S-search] AgX desaturates toward white instead of skewing hue (ACES pushes saturated reds/blues to orange/purple);
  AgX is less saturated by design and a scene graded for ACES will not look better by swapping alone. [I] For sodium,
  emergency-red and blue moonlight scenes AgX is the more realistic choice; regrade the level films after switching.
- Auto exposure: downsample luminance to 1x1 through the bloom chain's existing mips, log-average, ease toward a target
  with a clamp of about +/-1.5 EV and a 1-3 s time constant, all on the GPU (no readback).
- Cost: half-float target doubles main-pass bandwidth; ~0.3-1 ms desktop [I]. Max / High / Medium only. Low/Potato keep
  in-material ACES and get exposure "adaptation" from a CPU estimate (probe brightness at the camera + torch hit
  distance), costing nothing on the GPU.
- Pitfalls: WebGL2 needs EXT_color_buffer_float to render to half-float (check and fall back); fog colour and the
  clear colour must be tone mapped consistently (lighting.js already computes the fog's tone-mapped colour).

### 3.6 Ambient occlusion (already have SSAO)
- [S-search] N8AO (MIT) half-res mode claims 2-4x faster than full; GTAOPass in three is better than SSAOPass but
  costlier. [I] With bounce light and lightmap AO baked, screen-space AO's job shrinks to contact shadows of moving
  things (props knocked over, the monster's feet). Keep the existing pass; on Medium consider a half-res 8-sample
  version (~1 ms on iGPU [I]); never on Potato.

### 3.7 Texture compression (KTX2 / Basis)
- Payoff: ~4x less GPU memory than RGBA8 (BC7 / ASTC 4x4 from UASTC; ETC1S smaller files, lower quality); lets
  1024 textures fit on 4 GB Chromebooks; faster uploads (no main-thread decode of WebP).
- Today: 2048 set = 43 MB WebP on disk; on the GPU that is 16 MB + mips per 2048 map, x3 maps per material [I].
- Build: `ktx create` / `toktx` from KTX-Software in tools/textures.py's output step; UASTC + zstd for normal and ORM
  maps (ETC1S blocks smear normals), ETC1S acceptable for colour at 512/256 (Potato/Low). Transcoder adds ~584 KB
  uncompressed (less gzipped) to the download once [S-code size]; one shared KTX2Loader instance only.
- Pitfall: the lite single-file build embeds assets as base64; KTX2 there still saves GPU memory but the wasm must be
  inlined too.

### 3.8 Decals (already have wear + decal atlases)
- Add: stains that read in the roughness channel too (a water stain is glossier or duller, not just darker), scuffs
  along skirting and door handles, posters/notices, cracked plaster, rust runs under pipes. Generated or from CC0
  scans, never lifted from a game. All tiers that draw `wear` (Max..Medium); Low/Potato can bake the darkest decals
  into the vertex colour for free.

### 3.9 Volumetric light (already have a ray-marched torch cone and haze ribbons)
- Keep. Add for fixtures: static light shafts as baked cone meshes with soft depth fade (`shafts` already) aligned
  to window light on the outdoor-facing floors. Froxel volumetrics (UE/Frostbite style) are too heavy for WebGL2 iGPUs.

### 3.10 Edge bevels without geometry
- Payoff: box worlds look like boxes because every edge is a perfect 90-degree crease with no highlight.
- Build: in the world shader, compute distance to the nearest face edge from the part's local UV / size (the bake
  already knows each face's extent), bend the normal over the last ~1-2 cm, add an edge-wear mask (paint chips, lighter
  edge, darker grime in the inner corner using baked AO). One-time shader work; ~0.1 ms; all tiers but Potato
  (Potato: brighten edge in the albedo only).
- Pitfall: only on exposed convex edges; the bake must mark which edges touch another part (otherwise seams between
  stacked boxes show highlights).

### 3.11 Lens and camera
- Already: grain, chroma, vignette, panic DOF. Add (Max/High): subtle barrel distortion (2-4 %), camera motion blur from
  the depth buffer and the previous view matrix (camera only, ~1 ms), DOF with bokeh in cutscenes only
  (`BokehPass`-like; the cutscene camera is scripted so focus distances can be authored), gait-synced head bob and a
  slow handheld drift. Potato: none of the passes; head bob is free.
- Pitfall: grain and chroma above a light touch make it look cheaper, not more real [I]; keep chroma to the edges.

### 3.12 Skinned characters (task #44 in flight)
- MakeHuman CC0 export -> Blender (decimate to ~8-15k tris Max, ~3k Potato; 4 influences Max, 2 Potato) -> glTF with
  KTX2 textures. Skin: pre-integrated LUT (one fetch) Max..Medium, wrap lighting Low/Potato. Eyes: single mesh,
  parallax iris, wet-lid strip, a catch-light that follows the main light. Hair: alpha-tested cards with dithered /
  alpha-to-coverage edges and a Kajiya-Kay-style anisotropic highlight; Potato: solid hair mesh.
- Lighting: characters sample the probe grid (already) plus the torch and the reflection probe for eyes/lips.
- Faces: ARKit-style blendshapes (blink, brows, jaw, ~10 visemes) driven by phoneme timings exported by the offline
  speech model (ties to task #49). Potato: jaw + blink only.
- Animation: CMU mocap retargeted in Blender for walk / idle / crouch / reach; hand-keyed cleanup. Not Mixamo.

### 3.13 The outdoor late-afternoon cutscene
- Sun low (5-15 degrees), warm (around 3500-4500 K), long shadows; sky from a CC0 Poly Haven HDRI (late-afternoon /
  overcast-sunset) used for IBL and an SH probe (`LightProbeGenerator`), a sun shadow map focused on the scripted
  framing (the camera is known, so one tight 2048 map beats cascades), bake the static Back Lot with that sun and sky
  (it never moves during the cutscene), AgX, atmospheric perspective (fog tinted toward the sky colour with distance),
  bloom on the sun, authored DOF on the bin and Danny, slight lens vignette. Potato: lightmapped set + sun without
  shadow on characters except a blob/contact shadow, no DOF.

## 4. The ordered plan for ROVLOC (most realism per hour first)

All estimates are mine [I]. "All tiers" includes Potato, at zero or near-zero run-time cost.

1. **Bounce light in the existing bake** (3.1). 1-2 bounces, split per flicker group, probes too. Run time: free on
   every tier. ~2-4 days. The biggest step toward the CS2 / RE look, because both are built on baked indirect light.
2. **CC0 scanned materials for the 12 surfaces the player sees most** (painted block wall, glazed tile band, vinyl
   composite / terrazzo floor, acoustic ceiling tile, steel locker, wooden door, metal door frame, basement concrete,
   rusty pipe, auditorium carpet and seat fabric, roof membrane / gravel), from ambientCG / Poly Haven, delit, at

### How close to "CS2" can a browser game from boxes and free assets get?

Honest answer [I]: in a still frame of a dark corridor lit by fixtures and the torch, at Max on a desktop GPU, close
enough that a teenager would call it realistic: CS2's own interiors are baked GI + PBR scans + parallax-corrected
cubemaps + good tone mapping, and every one of those is doable here. At Potato on a Chromebook it will look like a
well-lit older game (good light, soft, low-res textures), not like CS2. What would still give it away:

- **Silhouettes and geometric detail.** Everything is a box; real buildings have mouldings, rounded edges, cables,
  sagging ceiling tiles. Bevel shading hides edges, not silhouettes. Imported meshes for hero props (radiators, fire
  extinguishers, chairs) close part of the gap.
- **People and the monster up close.** Faces, hair and hand-made animation are where browser games show most; CS2
  has scanned heads and mocap teams. Keep faces at a distance, in shadow, in motion.
- **Moving light.** The torch and flickering fixtures do not bounce dynamically (faked by the hit-point light);
  CS2 mostly avoids moving lights too.
- **Texture resolution and repetition on lower tiers**; filtering at grazing angles on Potato (anisotropy 1).
- **Aliasing.** FXAA on a 50-70 % of 720p frame shimmers on thin geometry; CS2 uses MSAA at native resolution.
- **Light leaks at box seams** in lightmaps (fixable with the bake marking touching faces).

## Avoid

- Copying or closely imitating any game's content: no textures, decals, posters, models, monster designs, maps or
  colour grades lifted from CS2, RE, Outlast, P.T., Alien: Isolation, DOORS, Pressure, The Mimic, Apeirophobia or
  any other game, and no ripped game assets "for reference" in the repo. Techniques only.
- "Realistic" packs of unclear licence (Sketchfab "free" models, Unreal/Unity marketplace content, texture sites
  without a CC0 deed), Bandai Namco and SFU mocap (non-commercial), Mixamo (unclear terms); scans with baked shadows.
- Real-time GI, froxel volumetrics, screen-space reflections or planar reflections on Low/Potato, and full-res SSAO
  below Max: they break 60 FPS on integrated GPUs.
- ETC1S compression on normal maps; multiple KTX2Loader instances.
- Heavy grain, strong chromatic aberration and constant DOF in gameplay: they read as cheap filters, not as realism.
- Swapping ACES for AgX without regrading the level films.
- Automatic tier switching (the project's rule: hint only).
