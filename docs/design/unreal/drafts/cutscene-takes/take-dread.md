# Stay Behind: the twelve cutscenes (the horror director's take: dread)

The map is the canon; this file only stages it. Twelve scenes, about 17 minutes in all (1,015 seconds), in story order. Then the six moments that stay in first person, then the three scenes I would fight for.

## How every scene here is shot

- **The camera almost shows.** The fear sits at the edge of the frame, in the background behind a face, in the dark between two flashes of light. We never cut to the hunter with a bang. When it is in a shot, it was already there.
- **Hold a beat too long.** Every scene has one shot that stays after it should have cut away. That is where the player's skin crawls.
- **Sound before shape.** You hear a thing before you see it, and sometimes you only hear it.
- **No music while the hunter is near.** The songs sit only where the map puts them.
- **Lines are clear over everything.** Rotors, rain and alarms are turned down under every spoken line (the sound people call this "ducking").
- **Words used below.** *Eye-line*: exactly where Danny is looking. *Rack focus*: the sharp part of the picture moves from near to far, so a background thing "arrives" without any cut. *Cut on action*: the cut happens at the start of a movement and picks up at its end, so the hard middle is never animated. *Matched cut*: the last cutscene frame is Danny's own view at the first-person lens, and the game takes over on the very next frame.
- **The hand-back** is always that matched cut, with the sound carrying over it. This is safer than blending the camera, which a UE 5.8 bug report says is broken. The player has control on the first frame and can move within two seconds.
- **Faces that speak on camera:** Danny, Kayla, Marcus, Gus, Kessler, Ray, Mari, Priya, Owen. That is nine of the ten allowed. The helicopter crew, the soldiers and the taken never show a speaking face.

## 1. "We'll Be at the Gate"

**Where:** Prologue. The player has just walked the trophy corridor: Kayla's flash photo, the cookie, Danny's face in the trophy glass. **Length:** 100 s.
**On screen:** Danny, Kayla and Marcus, and all three speak. The announcer is a voice only.

**The shots**
1. Black. Through the wall come the buzzer and the crowd, then the PA: "That's the half, folks. Please remain in your seats." The camera pulls back out of Danny's head to just behind his shoulder.
2. A low wide shot from the far end of the corridor. The two walk toward us, small against the gym's light, and the camera backs away at their pace.
3. Side-on, close. Kayla does not look at him: "My mom took the job. We move in June."
4. Danny's face, held a beat too long before the joke comes, late: "So I'm the one who stays behind. Cool. Love that for me."
5. Kayla's face behind the trophy glass's reflection. She does not laugh.
6. The gym doors bang behind them. Marcus jogs up, spinning a drumstick: "You guys are leaving? It's halftime."
7. Marcus walks backwards toward the bright gym doors, still spinning the stick: "You guys'll wait, right?"
8. The reverse shot: Danny is already turning to the push bar. "We'll be at the gate."
9. Over Kayla's shoulder: Marcus slips into the light. The gym door swings shut on its slow closer, and the bar of light on the floor narrows to a line and goes.
10. The push bar. Cold air, breath fog. Then we hold on the corridor they left: the trophy glass and the vending machine's hum. Nothing else, a beat longer than it should be.

**The image:** Marcus walking backwards into the light, framed by the closing door. It is the last time anyone sees him awake.
**The sound:** "Please remain in your seats," clean and booming in the PA's echo. The player hears it again in Chapter 1 as the hunter's first whisper. Then the door closer's long hiss. No score: only the pep band through the wall, which stops when the door shuts.
**The trick:** The walk is a slow walk from Epic's Game Animation Sample. Faces get close-ups only when the people stand still (shots 3 to 5 and 7 to 8). Marcus's backwards walk is a library backpedal, with the drumstick spin keyed by hand as one loop. Nobody touches anybody. The four key lines are faces filmed on a phone; the rest is driven from the audio.
**The hand-back:** Danny's eye-line through the opening door into the dark lot, cut on the door. The player is standing in the parking lot with Kayla on his left, and the walk and banter begin.
**The risk:** Three teenage MetaHumans close up under flat fluorescent light, the least forgiving light in the game.
**The fallback:** Half the tubes are dead ("after hours"), so the faces sit in the trophy cases' warm light. If the faces still fail, it plays in first person: Kayla says her lines to the player, and the only cut is the door closing on Marcus.
*If Marcus were not taken at halftime (decision 5), shots 6 to 9 go, and the scene loses its spine.*

## 2. "Lights Out"

**Where:** Prologue. The player has walked the lot, got the laugh out of Kayla, and lived through the pulse: phones hot and dead, streetlights dying street by street. He looks up. **Length:** 75 s.
**On screen:** Danny and Kayla, only from behind and in silhouette. No face speaks.

**The shots**
1. Low, behind the two. The clouds over the city light up from above, not from the ground.
2. The cloud sags. Something flat and endless pushes down through it without a sound, its underside crawling with slow lights like a city hung upside down.
3. Their two small faces, lit only from above, looking up.
4. The last car alarm in the lot chokes off mid-whoop. Silence, then a pressure in the ears.
5. Beyond the fence, a small ship drops onto the football field like a stone, with no flame. The turf jumps and steam rolls out.
6. A long lens through the chain-link. The steam is lit from inside by the ship's landing light: on, off, on.
7. On: steam. Off. On: a hand, far too long, flat on the turf at the steam's edge, fingers spread. Off.
8. On: the hand is gone. Off. On: something stands in the thinning steam, three metres tall, hooded, its back to us. Kayla, hardly a sound: "Danny."
9. Off. On: it faces the school now. The long pale face is not turned to them but to the lit windows of the gym.
10. Danny drops behind a car. The cut comes as he starts to drop.

**The image:** One strobe frame: the tall shape standing in the steam behind the chain-link, lit from below.
**The sound:** The absence of sound: the alarms dying, the mothership's ear pressure, the landing light ticking like a relay. Every "on" carries a soft electric thunk. The frame where it stands has none. No music.
**The trick:** The unfolding is never shown. The blackouts between flashes hide every change, and each "on" is a held pose (hand flat, standing back, face turned), so nothing needs to be animated. The two humans are backs against a lit sky, so there are no faces and no lip-sync. The mothership is volumetric cloud and slow lights.
**The hand-back:** Danny's eye-line between two bumpers at the field. The player is crouched in the same place, with Kayla crouched beside him: "Band-room door. Go." The sneak begins, and in play the hunter is already standing up and turning its face.
**The risk:** The mothership reading as a model; the hunter's three poses each have to read in a single flash.
**The fallback:** Show only the mothership's lights in the cloud and the stars going out. Keep only the hand and then the empty steam.

## 3. "The Skateboard"

**Where:** End of the prologue. The player got back in, Kayla fell on the stairs, and Gus said "You two. Down. Now." **Length:** 60 s.
**On screen:** Gus, Danny and Kayla. Gus speaks once.

**The shots**
1. Inside the steel door, in Gus's torchlight. The bar drops with a clank. Gus, quiet: "That'll hold."
2. Gus's hands on the bar. His key ring is trembling, and nothing else of him moves.
3. Danny's face at the small wired window, his breath fogging it.
4. Through the window, from just behind his head: the corridor, with one fluorescent tube still alive and flickering.
5. The tube dims. A sound comes before any shape: skateboard wheels on terrazzo, a long, slow roll.
6. Into the last pool of light a skateboard rolls from the left on its own. It slows and stops in the middle, one wheel still turning.
7. Kayla's face beside his on the glass. She knows the sticker. Nobody speaks.
8. The tube dies. The glass is black, with their reflections in Gus's torchlight.
9. Hold on the black. Very far off, one heavy step. Title: STAY BEHIND.

**The image:** The skateboard stopped in the last of the light, one wheel turning.
**The sound:** The wheels slowing on the stone, recorded for real; the tube's buzz; then the single heavy step after the light has gone. The title comes up in silence.
**The trick:** Almost nothing has to be animated: a board rolling under physics, a flicker, three still heads at the glass, and hands on a bar keyed only in close-up.
**The hand-back:** The title fades into first person in the boiler room. The player is at the same window, now black, with Gus's torch on the left.
**The risk:** The board looking pushed by a script.
**The fallback:** It is nearly first person already. Play it from Danny's eyes.

## 4. "The Hand on the Glass"

**Where:** Chapter 1. The player has let in Kessler, Owen, Mari and Priya. Ray is the last to arrive. **Length:** 95 s.
**On screen:** Gus, Ray and Kessler speak; Danny is seen; the others are hands and shoes.

**The shots**
1. Hammering on the steel door. Gus's face, lit from below by his torch, deciding.
2. Through the wired glass, Ray's face pressed close, muffled: "Open it! Open the damn door!"
3. Gus's key turns and the bar lifts (hands only).
4. The door opens a hand's width and Ray falls through. The cut comes as he falls; we pick him up already on the floor.
5. Close on Gus shouldering the door shut. Behind him, through the glass and out of focus, something pale and flat.
6. Rack focus to the glass: a hand flat on it from outside. The palm is chalk white, the fingers longer than Gus's forearm, the knuckles webbed with dark veins. Gus does not see it.
7. Danny's face sees it. He says nothing. Hold.
8. The bar drops. The hand slides slowly down the glass and out of frame, with a squeak of skin on glass.
9. Cut to later, by candle and torch. Kessler with her roll book, counting people we see only as hands, shoes and a red beanie: "...five. Six. Seven." She touches her own chest: "Eight."
10. Kessler: "Nobody goes up. We stay behind this door until help comes."
11. Danny looks at the door's window. It is black.

**The image:** The hand on the wired glass behind Gus's head, which has not turned.
**The sound:** The hammering stops dead. Then the one wet squeak of the palm on glass, and Kessler's count in a hushed, dripping room. No music: the hunter was just there.
**The trick:** No hunter body, only a hand mesh posed on glass and one slide down it, keyed by hand. Ray's fall happens across a cut. The count shows no faces except Kessler's.
**The hand-back:** Danny's eye-line on the window. The player stands at the door, with Gus beside him: "Generator don't care who's scared." The generator puzzle begins.
**The risk:** The hand's size beside Gus, and skin on glass looking like a sticker.
**The fallback:** Only the shadow of the fingers through the wired glass, cast by the corridor light. Or play it first person: the player sees the hand behind Gus and cannot tell him.
*The map has Ray arrive "early on"; I make him last so the hand ends the arrivals. Either works.*

## 5. "The Camp Song"

**Where:** Chapter 1, the first night. The player has run Flare Road and restarted the generator. **Length:** 110 s.
**On screen:** Mari (sings and plays), Danny (the promise), with Owen, Kayla and Gus silent.

**The shots**
1. The generator's hum and one work lamp. The group sits on cots, seen as a row of knees and hands.
2. Mari's hands on a cheap guitar, close. She tunes one string: out, then in.
3. Mari's face, lit from the side. She sings the camp song softly, half humming.
4. Owen in a blanket, watching her hands, not her face.
5. Kayla's strapped ankle on a crate. Her fingers keep time on her knee, then stop.
6. Gus leans in the generator-room door, watching the generator, not the song.
7. The song ends. Quiet. Danny, before he can stop himself: "I'm getting everyone out. All of you." A beat. "Which, historically, from me, means nothing. But."
8. Kayla looks at him and hides a small smile.
9. The camera leaves them, drifting slowly up the wall to an air grille near the ceiling, and holds there far too long. Dust shivers in its slats. Nothing more.
10. Back down: Danny is looking up at the grille.

**The image:** The grille, held, while the promise still hangs in the room.
**The sound:** The song, live and close, the guitar slightly out of tune, over the generator's hum. On the grille, under the hum, only the tick of metal in the duct. The player will understand this shot in the last ten minutes of the game, when the hunter says the promise back.
**The trick:** Everyone sits. Mari's fingers are one hand-keyed loop of three chords, shown only in shots where her face is not. Her singing face is filmed on a phone. The grille shot needs no one.
**The hand-back:** Danny's eye-line on the grille. The player sits on his cot looking up at it, and the night begins (Owen asks to be walked past the dark).
**The risk:** A singing face, and fingers that drift away from the audio.
**The fallback:** Mari plays with her back to the camera, lit by the lamp. The song is heard from Danny's cot in first person, and the grille shot stays.

## 6. "They're Breathing"

**Where:** The turn at the end of Chapter 2. The player is back from the gym and the nurse's office. **Length:** 75 s.
**On screen:** Priya, Ray and Kayla speak. Danny is seen.

**The shots**
1. The monitor wall in the dark: six grey feeds, three of them dead. Faces lit blue from below.
2. Feed four, the loading dock. Soldiers with lamps on their armour slide frosted cradles into a small ship. The picture is grainy and jerks at eight frames a second.
3. Priya, flat and certain: "They're bodies. They're just moving the bodies."
4. Kayla zooms in, and the picture breaks into blocks.
5. On the feed: a cradle lid, frosted white. A soft oval clears in the frost, fades, and clears again.
6. Priya's face changes. Her mouth opens before the words come: "They're breathing."
7. Ray, from the dark behind them: "Say that again." Priya: "They're *breathing*."
8. Kayla's face: hope first, then what it means.
9. On the feed, one soldier stops and turns its lamp straight up into the lens. The picture whites out and feed four dies.
10. Four faces lit only by static.

**The image:** The security-camera frame with breath blooming on a frosted lid.
**The sound:** The monitors' whine, and the soldiers' scanner tones coming faintly through the camera's cheap microphone. Priya's line is dry and clear. Music: one bowed-metal note rises on the breath and is cut dead when the feed whites out.
**The trick:** The soldiers are only ever seen at security-camera quality, low frame rate, grain and smear, which hides library animation completely. The faces in front barely move; they react, lit from below.
**The hand-back:** Danny's static-lit face. The player stands at the monitor wall with Kayla's radio desk on his right, and the safe-room night begins.
**The risk:** Four faces reacting without moving can look wooden.
**The fallback:** Shoot over their shoulders: the monitors and the backs of their heads, voices only. Priya's is the one face kept.

## 7. "Frost"

**Where:** Chapter 3, straight after the Mark (played in first person) and the blackout in which Mari is taken. **Length:** 55 s.
**On screen:** Danny alone, who speaks no line. The Still Ones.

**The shots**
1. High and wide from the fly gallery: the stage, and Danny lying on the boards in a work light's circle, the dark house beyond.
2. Close: his eyes open. Breath fog.
3. His forearm in the light. Black lines spread from one point like frost on a window, and grow a few millimetres while we watch.
4. He touches them and flinches. Cold.
5. Low, behind him, the wings: three Still Ones standing with their faces turned away.
6. Danny's face as he understands where he is.
7. The same wing shot, the same framing, except that one figure is a step nearer and the light now stops at its shoulders.
8. At the edge of the light, Mari's hair clip on the boards where she stood.
9. Danny gets to one knee. The cut comes as he starts to rise.

**The image:** The black frost-lines on a forearm under one work light.
**The sound:** The work light's buzz and his breath. On the cut back to the wings, a dry creak where one moved: heard, never seen. No music.
**The trick:** Danny lies still, and he gets up across a cut. The Mark is a material that grows across the arm, which is the easiest kind of acting there is. The Still Ones never have to move: the cut moves them.
**The hand-back:** Danny's eye-line on the wings. The player kneels with the torch up, facing the three, and must cross the live floor home in failing light.
**The risk:** Danny's waking face in hard light.
**The fallback:** No face: the arm, the breath, the two wing shots, all in first person.
*If the owner drops the Mark (decision 4), this scene loses its arm and becomes only the wings and the hair clip.*

## 8. "Where's Mari"

**Where:** Chapter 3, the safe room. The player has just got home hurt. **Length:** 60 s.
**On screen:** Danny and Owen speak. Kessler, Kayla and Gus are seen.

**The shots**
1. The steel door from inside. It opens on Danny, backlit by the tunnel's red exit sign, not stepping in yet.
2. The group turns: the backs of heads, one after another.
3. Danny, the old grin arriving late: "So. Good news and bad—"
4. He stops. Hold on the grin that cannot finish.
5. Owen, from a cot: "Where's Mari?"
6. Danny: "...Yeah."
7. He sits on the nearest water drum, just outside the lamp's light.
8. Mari's cot, her blanket folded her way, the guitar on its nail.
9. Danny's pack at his feet, the paper packet of guitar strings sticking out.
10. Danny pulls his sleeve down over his wrist. Under the cuff, the edge of the black lines. Then the generator's hum stutters once and recovers. Nobody but the player notices.

**The image:** Mari's empty cot with the guitar above it, and Danny's sleeve pulled over the black lines in the foreground.
**The sound:** Owen's three words, clear. The generator's one stutter: it plants the breach two chapters early. Music: the camp song's hook on a detuned piano, very soft, on the cot shot. The hunter is far away tonight; the music may play.
**The trick:** One library walk in silhouette against the red sign, then everyone sits. Three short lines. Objects carry the grief.
**The hand-back:** Danny's eye-line on his own sleeve. The player sits on the drum with the sleeve in view, and the night's choices begin: show Kayla the Mark, or hide it.
**The risk:** The failing grin is the hardest face acting in the game.
**The fallback:** Danny stays a silhouette in the doorway the whole time, his face never shown. Or play it in first person, where Owen's question is asked to the player.

## 9. "Knock"

**Where:** Chapter 4, the safe room after dark. Ray went out alone for Caleb's letterman jacket. **Length:** 90 s.
**On screen:** Kessler, Danny and Kayla speak. Gus is seen. Ray is a voice only.

**The shots**
1. A wide shot of the steel door from the far end of the shelter. Three knocks.
2. Everyone still. Gus's hand on the bar. Kessler steps between him and it: "Don't."
3. Ray's voice through the steel: "It's Ray. It's me. I got his jacket. Open the door."
4. Kessler, close, in torchlight: "The things up there sound like people."
5. Danny: "That's Coach. That's his voice." Kessler: "That's what I'm saying, Mr. Reyes."
6. The same wide shot. Three knocks, in the same rhythm and the same spacing. The camera has not moved.
7. Kayla, by the dead monitors: "Those were the same."
8. Silence. Then one knock, tired and uneven and lower on the door, as if a man had sat down against it. Very low: "...Please."
9. Kessler turns her back on the door, sits on her cot and opens her roll book.
10. Danny looks down at the tunnel hatch in the floor: the way round.

**The image:** The steel door, wide and still, with everyone frozen in front of it while the same knock arrives twice.
**The sound:** Real knuckles on real steel. The doubled rhythm is the scare, because the player learned on this floor that Mimics repeat with the same timing. The tired knock that breaks the pattern is the hope. No music.
**The trick:** Nobody moves. The door does the acting. Ray is never on screen.
**The hand-back:** Danny's eye-line on the hatch. The player stands over it with the choice open: go round and test him, or leave it shut.
**The risk:** Very little. Kessler's face in close-up.
**The fallback:** First person throughout, with voices only.

## 10. "Somebody Keeps the Lights On"

**Where:** Chapter 5. The hunter's chase across the roof ends in the helicopter's searchlight. **Length:** 105 s.
**On screen:** Gus and Danny speak. Kessler is seen. The survivors are silhouettes, and the crew chief is faceless.

**The shots**
1. From above, the searchlight swings onto the roof. Rain blows sideways through the beam in the rotor wash.
2. Silhouettes run into the light toward the cabin door. We cut before anyone climbs and pick them up already seated.
3. Kessler at the door with her list. The rotors swallow it; a few names come through. (If the seats were shared, there is no list: she hands her place to the nearest kid and steps back beside Gus.)
4. Danny at the cabin door, turning back. Gus stands at the edge of the light, rain on his cap, keys in his hand.
5. Gus: "I don't fly. Never have." Danny: "Gus." Gus: "Somebody keeps the lights on, son. Go on."
6. Gus takes one step back out of the beam and is only a shape.
7. From the cabin, the roof drops away and the searchlight slides off it.
8. **If he was helped (his hearing aid):** before the light leaves him, Gus tips his head. He has heard something. He clicks off his torch and is gone into the stair hut, and a door shuts. The roof is empty.
   **If he was not:** the light slides off Gus while he waves his torch up at them. Behind him, at the roof door, at the very edge of the beam, a long pale face, lower than a doorway because it is stooping. It is already there. Gus does not turn. The beam leaves him. Dark.
9. Kayla beside Danny in the cabin, looking down.

**The image:** The second version: Gus waving his torch up at the helicopter while the long face waits at the stair door behind him, half in the beam.

**The hand-back:** Danny's eye-line down at the dark roof. The player sits in the cabin, free to look around, and the first song begins.
**The risk:** The heaviest scene in the set: rain on MetaHuman hair, a hovering aircraft, many bodies.
**The fallback:** Play it in first person from the cabin door: Gus says his lines to the player, the survivors are only the shapes passing him, and shot 8 is seen from the door as the helicopter lifts.

## 11. "What Time?"

**Where:** Chapter 8, the nest. The player has put the drumstick in Marcus's hand and solved the waking symbols on Kayla's cradle. **Length:** 90 s.
**On screen:** Kayla and Danny speak. Marcus and Mari are still figures out of focus.

**The shots**
1. The nest: a low, grown chamber, frost on everything, cradles like seed pods in dim blue. Danny kneels at one, seen from behind.
2. Kayla's face beneath a frosted lid, only its shape.
3. Danny's palm wipes a patch of frost clear.
4. Her face, still. Then her breath fogs the inside of the glass.
5. Out of focus behind them, Marcus and Mari in their cradles, their lips moving together with a murmur whose words never quite land.
6. The lid opens by itself. Kayla's eyes open and find him. She does not trust what she sees.
7. Kayla, hoarse: "What time?"
8. Danny. The joke does not come: "Four."
9. She breathes out: "...Danny." Her eyes go to his arm, where the black lines now climb past the elbow. (If he showed her the Mark in Chapter 3, she only looks. If not, this is when she learns it.)
10. Danny: "If I get in one, they follow it." A beat. "They follow you."
11. Behind them, Marcus's and Mari's lips stop at the same moment. Silence.

**The hand-back:** Danny's eye-line on the nest's mouth. The player kneels with Kayla behind him and the dark opening ahead, and has to wake the others while it comes.

**The fallback:** The lid stays frosted, and we see her only through the cleared hand print.

## The six story moments that stay in first person

These are where the fear has to stay in the player's hands. None of them takes control away for more than a few seconds.

1. **The Mark (Chapter 3).** A whisper right behind him, in Marcus's broken voice, and the player turns, which is the mistake this floor punishes. The view rises two metres as his feet leave the boards. The long face hangs upside down into the frame from above, as in the favourite picture: the black eyes with one point of light each, the seam down the long face, a veined hand gripping the proscenium the way it grips the lockers. Then the player's own forearm is lifted into view, one long finger is pressed to it, and frost crackles. He is put down. Mari calls "Danny" once, far off, and everything goes black. No body is ever shown, only the face, the hand and the view rising.
2. **Two Kaylas (Chapter 4).** The yellow jacket stands at the end of a library aisle with its back to him: "Danny, over here." He asks "What time?" and it says "What time?" back, in her voice and with her timing. Then the real voice comes from behind, with breath in it: "Four. Why are you asking me... oh." To look at her, the player must turn his back on the thing. When he looks back, it is a step closer, in the same pose. "Is it a good look?" lands because the player is still shaking.
3. **The breach (Chapter 5).** It is heard, never seen. Over the radio, under Kayla's "It's in. It's inside, Danny," the generator's hum winds down to nothing. The player has known that hum as home for five chapters. Its silence is the scene.
4. **The parachute and the fall (Chapter 5).** The cabin spins. The player looks down at Kayla's hands clipping the harness to his chest, and her face is in frame for one second: "Go. I'm right behind you." The shove takes the ground from under him. Then wind, the canopy's crack, and silence. Three blocks away, lit by the fire, something taller than the soldiers lifts a small figure in a yellow jacket. It is tiny in the frame, and the player cannot do anything about it.
5. **The nest (Chapter 8).** Marcus's lips are moving with the hunter's murmur. The player's own hand puts the drumstick into Marcus's hand, and the fingers do not close around it. The player does it himself because no cutscene could make him feel it.
6. **The hold and the white (Chapter 8).** One button: his hand on the core. Pods launch one by one, a face at each window. The hunter folds in over the console from above, its face upside down in his view, the favourite picture at its full size, and whispers "getting... everyone... out." Danny: "Yeah. That one I'm keeping." His free hand spends the flare, the cell and the extinguisher. The hand closes over his mouth, but the Kessler voice is already out: "Miss Brooks. Is something funny?" Kayla laughs on the radio, crying. Then the true thing, in his own voice. White. *My one addition of staging, not plot:* the white thins into the view from Kayla's pod window at dawn, played as her for twenty seconds. The ship comes apart in total silence above the city while the radio hisses, then the game cuts to June. This replaces the story draft's twelfth cutscene ("the ship comes apart"). If unwanted, cut straight from white to June.

The rooftop whisper of "stay... on the... line" in Chapter 6 also stays first person and needs no help. The radio on his chest stays dead while she is whispered from above.

## The three I would fight for

1. **4, "The Hand on the Glass."** It is the cheapest scene in the set (one hand mesh, one slide) and the most frightening. It shows the hunter's size before its face. It tells the player that the steel door is all there is. And its last shot of the black window teaches him to dread that window for the rest of the game.
2. **10, "Somebody Keeps the Lights On," the version where Gus was not helped.** This is my whole lens in one frame. The camera almost shows it: the face at the edge of the beam, there before we looked, and a good man who never turns around. It also makes a quiet choice from Chapter 2 (a packet of hearing-aid batteries) pay off with no meter and no message.
3. **11, "What Time?"** The code word that protected him from the Mimics is turned around: the person he crossed the sky for tests *him*, because she has been listening to the hunter's voices in her sleep. It is two still faces and a frosted lid, so it is almost safe to stage. The murmur stopping behind them is the best silence in the game.
