STAY BEHIND: THE CUTSCENES, JUDGED AS THE OWNER WOULD
(judge: "would he say that was cool?"; files judged: take-dread.md, take-heart.md, take-spectacle.md)

OVERALL
No one director wins the set. Dread wins the scares (the scenes where a horror player's skin crawls),
Heart wins the people (the scenes that make the ending hurt the way Red Dead 2 does), and Spectacle wins
the big pictures and has the best idea for the ending. Tally in the final twelve: heart 5, spectacle 4,
dread 3. The three agree on the right things: every catch, the Mark, the breach, the parachute, the nest
and the hold stay in first person; the hunter's unfolding is never shown; every scene ends on Danny's own
view so control is back in under two seconds. The set below runs about 1,030 seconds (17 minutes).

Recommended twelve, in story order, about 17 min in all:
 1  Halftime ................ heart      100 s   score 7
 2  Last Lights ............. spectacle  110 s   score 9
 3  The Board (title) ....... spectacle   50 s   score 9
 4  First Night ............. spectacle  120 s   score 7
 5  Where's Mari ............ heart       70 s   score 8
 6  Knock ................... dread       85 s   score 8
 7  Somebody Keeps the Lights On  dread  110 s   score 9
 8  It's Me. Again. (Ch 6) .. heart       65 s   score 8
 9  What Time? (Kayla wakes)  dread       95 s   score 9
10  Right Behind You (pod) .. heart      100 s   score 9
11  Seeds (dawn, after white) spectacle   45 s   score 8
12  We Waited (the gate) .... heart       80 s   score 8

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. HALFTIME ("We move in June" / "We'll be at the gate")   WINNER: heart   SCORE: 7
Why: Heart's version is built on objects that pay off nine hours later: the film camera's flash (whose
last frame Kayla develops in June), the thumb stopping on the wind-on lever, "Swear on the hat" / "On the
hat", and Marcus seen running back through the wired glass of the closing door. It is the right opening,
but it is a talk scene with three teen faces close up, so a bored player rates it a 7, not a 9.
Graft: from dread, the PA line "That's the half, folks. Please remain in your seats" over the opening
(the hunter whispers it in scene 3, so the player must hear it clean here). From spectacle, the shot of
the two from behind walking to the glass doors with the stadium's four light banks burning in the cold:
those four lights die in scene 2. Put Marcus's skateboard (with one loud sticker) leaning on the trophy
case in the over-the-shoulder shot so scene 3's board is recognised at once.
Fix: keep faces in the trophy cases' warm light with half the tubes dead (dread's fallback), never flat
fluorescent light. Cut dread's long hold on the empty corridor; it drags in minute six of the game.

2. LAST LIGHTS (the mothership and the small ship)   WINNER: spectacle   SCORE: 9
Why: The stadium's four light banks dying one at a time with a heavy clunk under a ship the size of a
district is the poster of the game, and the joints opening "like a ladder pulled to full height", heard
over two faces, hides the hardest animation in the game.
Graft: from dread, the landing light strobing in the steam (on: a hand flat on the turf; off; on: the
hand gone; off; on: it stands, hooded, back to us). It is the scariest way to reveal a monster without
animating it. From heart, the crowd inside the gym going silent all at once behind the wall, Kayla's
fingers closing on Danny's sleeve, and Danny's one line, "Okay. That's... a lot of ceiling." (the one
quotable line; Kayla's whispered "Danny." stays).
Fix: trim to about 110 s. End with it standing, back turned: the map gives the face turning toward the
school to the playable sneak, so do not use dread's last strobe frame of the face turned.

3. THE BOARD (the steel door, the title)   WINNER: spectacle   SCORE: 9
Why: Tubes dying from the far end of the corridor toward the door, a skateboard rolling into the last
light on its own and clicking to a stop, then, from right behind the steel door, the announcer's words
broken: "...remain... in your... seats." It teaches the whisper rule (a whisper means it is right here)
in the first ten minutes, with no faces and almost no animation.
Graft: from dread, one wheel still turning after the board stops; Gus's key ring trembling on the bar;
Kayla's face beside Danny's at the glass as she recognises the sticker (no words).
Fix: the whisper must sound like the PA voice torn up, not a monster voice; let a beat of black pass
between the last tube and the whisper so the player has time to think the scene is over.

4. FIRST NIGHT (the count, the camp song, the promise)   WINNER: spectacle   SCORE: 7
Why: Heart wrote the better material in two scenes (95 s and 100 s), but two seated safe-room cutscenes in
Chapter 1 is where this owner gets bored. Spectacle's merge does the count, the Kessler impression ("Mr.
Reyes. Is something funny?" / "Is something funny?" / "...The chin's too low."), the song and the
promise in one 120 s scene, and opens on the best picture of home: eight small people in one caged
bulb's light under the 1962 signs.
Graft: from heart, Danny humming one bar along wrong and Mari laughing without stopping (it is why her
loss hurts in Chapter 3), and Kayla looking down at "Which, historically, from me, means nothing", the
only one who knows it is about his father. From dread, the ending: after "But.", the camera drifts up to
an air grille and holds while dust shivers in the slats (three seconds, not "far too long"). The player
understands it when the hunter says the promise back at the hold.
Fix: cut spectacle's slow move along faces (shot 8) and the "later" cut; the promise is said at the
generator straight after the song. Owen's laugh must be a real recorded laugh. Hold it to 120 s.

5. WHERE'S MARI (home after the Mark)   WINNER: heart   SCORE: 8
Why: A joke that dies halfway ("So. Good news and bad—"), Owen's "Where's Mari?", "...Yeah.", Danny
sitting down on her cot, and the guitar on the nail ringing an out-of-tune chord by itself when the steel
door slams. Nobody says a sad word and it hurts; the guitar ring is the sound he will remember.
Graft: from dread, Danny's entrance as a silhouette in the doorway against the tunnel's red exit sign,
and the generator's hum stuttering once (plants the breach). Heart's Kayla noticing the pulled-down
sleeve stays, because it sets up the Mark choice.
Fix: the map says Mari's strings are "still in your pack": do not decide that in the cutscene. He takes
the packet out, looks, and the choice to leave or keep it is the player's in first person. "...Yeah." must
be flat, not sad (heart's own warning).

6. KNOCK (Ray at the steel door)   WINNER: dread   SCORE: 8
Why: The same three knocks arrive twice with the same spacing while the camera does not move, and the
player has just learned on the Mimic floor that the same timing means a Mimic; then one tired, uneven
knock low on the door, "...Please." It is horror that uses the game's own rules, and Ray is never shown.
Graft: from heart, Kessler's line, very quietly, to the door: "I count my kids, Mr. Reyes. I can't count
him." (keeps her frightened, not a villain), and her thumb on DOBBS on the sign-in sheet. From spectacle,
Gus saying nothing and tilting his torch so the beam lands on the tunnel hatch, then looking at Danny.
Fix: do NOT use spectacle's Ray face at the window: seeing his face would answer the question the player
is supposed to test through the hatch. Cut dread's "Kessler sits and opens her roll book" to make room
for the grafted line.

7. SOMEBODY KEEPS THE LIGHTS ON (the roof, Gus)   WINNER: dread   SCORE: 9
Why: It makes a packet of hearing-aid batteries from Chapter 2 pay off with no meter: helped, Gus tips
his head, hears it, clicks off his torch and is gone into the stair hut; not helped, he waves his torch
up at them while the long pale face waits stooped at the stair door behind him, and he never turns.
That second frame is what a horror player screenshots.
Graft: from spectacle, the opening: the hunter standing at full height inside the searchlight's rain
column at the end of the chase, the beam flaring white into the lens, the parapet empty, one heavy step
on metal below the edge. From heart, the rotors dropping under Gus's lines so his keys jingle, Gus
striking a red flare before he goes (helped version), and the twelve frames of the long face turning
into the light with two pins in its black eyes, the only cutscene look at the favourite face.
Fix: Kessler's list is one shot, not three; boarding is silhouettes stepping into white between cuts.
Hold 110 s. Song 1 starts only once they are in the air, in first person.

8. IT'S ME. AGAIN. (the wreck, Chapter 6)   WINNER: heart   SCORE: 8
Why: After twenty minutes of talking into a dead radio, Danny presses talk at the wreck and his own voice
comes back out of the cabin: Kayla's walkie, blinking red in rainwater beside her film camera. It is
exactly the map's wreck, honest to "nothing fakes Kayla", and it is the city's one look at his face.
Graft: none needed; spectacle and dread have no Chapter 6 scene.
Fix: "...Cool. So I've been talking to a seat." must be thrown away, not pitied. He switches her walkie
off and keeps it: then the pod scene must show it clipped in her harness already (see scene 10), since
nothing can be handed over on camera.

9. WHAT TIME? (Kayla wakes in the nest)   WINNER: dread   SCORE: 9
Why: The code word that tested Mimics is turned round: Kayla, who has slept among the hunter's voices,
wakes and tests HIM. "What time?" "Four." Her breath fogging under his hand print on the lid, then Marcus's
and Mari's moving lips stopping at the same moment behind them: when the whispering stops, it has noticed
you. Two still faces and a lid, so it is nearly safe to stage.
Graft: from spectacle's "The Talk", Kayla's answer to "They follow you": "You're not your dad, Danny."
It is the only place the father (decision 6) is said on camera. From heart, the frost in Marcus's
eyebrows and his headphones still round his neck: calm and whole, never dead-looking.
Fix: the map places "If I get in one, they follow it" after the others are woken; keep it here only if
the waking of the others is played straight after (it is). Under the Harvest instead of the Voice
Collectors, the moving lips and their stopping go. The drumstick stays first person, before this scene.

10. RIGHT BEHIND YOU (Kayla's pod)   WINNER: heart   SCORE: 9
Why: The four lines ("You're lying. You do the thing with your mouth." / "I don't have a thing." / "You
have a thing, Danny."), then the lid seals and her voice is cut off mid-breath, and a click later the
same voice is clear in his ear on the radio: "Stay on the line, okay?" That move from glass to radio is
the best sound idea in all three takes, and the owner asked first for good talking.
Graft: from dread, the two palms on either side of the glass not lined up, frost closing in from the edges
until her face is gone, and one heavy step through the floor, then another nearer. From spectacle, the
reverse from inside the pod: his hand flat on the frost, the black lines on his wrist, his print filling in.
Fix: give her a radio: her walkie (from scene 8) already clipped to her harness. The hand-back goes to
the pod release for the hold (heart is right; the map puts the core's final exam before the pods, so
dread's and spectacle's turn "toward the core" is out of order). Four ending variants, as heart lists.

11. SEEDS (after the white)   WINNER: spectacle   SCORE: 8
Why: The ending turns a number into a picture: pods leaving the dying ship like seeds at dawn, as many as
the people Danny freed, then the silent break along its spine, the thunder arriving seconds late, and
the walkie in Kayla's hand going from hiss to nothing. No actor needed.
Graft: from dread, start it as the white thinning into the view from Kayla's pod window (dread's
"twenty seconds as her"), so the shot of her face through frost is the anchor.
Fix: cut from 70 s to about 45 s: straight after the hold, the player is spent and must reach June fast.
No music; Song 3 waits for the gate.

12. WE WAITED (the gate, June)   WINNER: heart   SCORE: 8
Why: "You guys'll wait, right?" is the guilt the whole game runs on, and here, at the same gate, whoever
lived says "We waited." Kayla laughs crying and lifts the film camera: the same click and flash that
opened the game. Song 3 starts on one chord of Mari's old guitar. In "Only her" she photographs the empty
gate anyway.
Graft: none; spectacle hands back to play at the fence, and dread ends at the white.
Fix: "We waited" said flat and tired, never acted. Up to seven people standing still in daylight is the
most exposed shot in the game: shoot the group from far off, faces small, and give the close-up only to
the speaker and Kayla.

SHOULD NOT BE CUTSCENES (play them in first person instead)
- They're Breathing (Chapter 2). All three wrote it well and all three fallbacks say it works at the
  monitor desk. Play it first person with dread's beats: Priya's "They're bodies", the frost clearing on
  the lid, "They're breathing" / "Say that again" / "They're breathing", and the soldier turning its lamp
  straight into the lens until the feed whites out: in first person it looks at YOU. Add heart's Ray
  hand flat on the monitor and spectacle's cradles floating on a ribbon of light (nobody carries).
- The hand on the glass (Chapter 1). Dread made it a cutscene; heart and spectacle are right that it is
  scarier in first person: aim the one bulb at the door, the hand arrives behind Ray as the door opens,
  nobody turns the player's head. Keep dread's squeak of the palm sliding down the glass.
- Frost (dread, waking with the Mark). The Still Ones only move when unwatched, so waking must be the
  player's own look round. Keep dread's pictures in first person: the frost-lines growing, the wing
  figure a step nearer, Mari's hair clip at the edge of the light.
- The nest with the drumstick (heart). The player must place it himself; nothing happens; that is the scene.
- The Lift (spectacle). Straight after the 110 s roof scene it makes over three minutes without control.
  Play it first person in the cabin; keep spectacle's lit basement window (Gus keeping the lights on) as
  something the player can look down at, and Danny's "Kayla—" cut off by the missile.
- The Belly (spectacle, Chapter 7). The flight's own view out of the cloud can show the ship filling the
  sky; keep its murmur of thousands of sleeping voices. A 25 s outside shot only if time allows.

DRAGS
- Back-to-back cutscenes: the roof plus the lift (190 s) and dread's two Chapter 1 scenes; heart's two
  Chapter 1 safe-room scenes (195 s). Fixed above by merging the first night and playing the lift.
- Every "hold a beat too long" in dread: allow one per scene, three seconds at most.
- Last Lights at 120 s and Seeds at 70 s: trim to 110 and 45.

MISSING
- The father is never on camera in any take: fixed by grafting "You're not your dad, Danny" into scene 9.
  His mother's note in Chapter 6 stays first person; consider an old photo of his dad on the fridge there.
- Kayla's radio in the pod: no take shows how she has one. Fixed by the walkie from scene 8, shown clipped.
- The favourite face is never held in a cutscene, which is right (it lives in the first-person Mark and
  hold, both staged from the favourite picture's angle: face coming down out of the dark into one light,
  the big veined hand gripping beside it). Scene 7's twelve frames are the one cutscene glimpse.
- Nothing else in the map needs a cutscene it does not have.
