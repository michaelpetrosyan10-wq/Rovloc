# Stay Behind: the twelve cutscenes, staged for the people ("Take Heart")

The actors' director's pass. The game map is the canon; where this differs from the story draft, the map won. Its recommended answers are assumed: the Voice Collectors, the Mark, Marcus taken at halftime, the hunter taking Kayla from the wreck, Danny's father as what he walked away from, the basketball game.

**What this pass believes.** A cutscene is cool when the player suddenly cares about someone. So these twelve are staged for faces, hands and small objects, and they keep coming back to the same five things: Kayla's film camera, Marcus's drumstick, Mari's guitar strings, Danny's red beanie, and **a hand flat on glass**. The hunter's hand on the wired glass in Chapter 1 is answered by Ray's hand on a monitor in Chapter 2 and Kayla's hand on the pod glass in Chapter 8. Danny jokes hardest when he is most afraid, and the scenes give him room to fail at it.

**Words used here.** A *close-up* fills the frame with a face or hand; a *wide* shows the whole place; a *push-in* is the camera creeping closer; *cut on action* cuts mid-movement so the eye never sees the hard part; *eye-line* is where Danny is looking. Every scene ends on Danny's eye-line at the first-person field of view, so play picks up from it without a jump.

**Rules every scene keeps:** 45 to 120 seconds (the twelve add up to **17 min 20 s**). At most three people in a shot, apart from one still, half-dark wide in the shelter. No fights, lifting, carrying or long touching: a touch is one hand, in close-up, under three seconds. The hunter only stands, stoops, turns its face, reaches and waits. Nothing graphic happens to anyone taken; nothing fakes Kayla on the radio. Every line sits clear over everything. No score while the hunter is near; only Song 3 starts inside a cutscene, the last one. Control returns within two seconds.

**Faces that speak on camera (nine of ten):** Danny, Kayla, Marcus, Kessler, Gus, Mari, Priya, Ray, Owen. The crew wear visors; Caleb, the laundromat boy and the taken never speak on camera. One slot spare.

**The hunter, from the favourite picture (B3).** A long pale face tapering to a blade, a seam down its middle, two black almond eyes with one pin of light in each, dark streaks running down from them like old tear-lines, a mouth that is only a slit at the tip, a grey hood hanging like a wet blanket, and a huge old hand with a black net of veins over the knuckles, spread on the lockers. In the picture it comes *down* out of the dark into one hard ceiling light. That is how it is shown here: from below, emerging, one light on the face, the rest black. Its hands do most of the acting.

## 1. "Love That For Me"

**Where:** Prologue, after the player has walked the trophy corridor at halftime. **Length:** 100 s.

**On screen:** Danny, Kayla, Marcus. **Speaking faces:** all three.

**The shots:**
1. Close on Kayla behind her grandfather's film camera, one eye shut. *Click,* the flash whites out the frame. We never see the face Danny pulled: it is the last frame on her roll in June.
2. Over Kayla's shoulder: Danny's back, red beanie, the trophy case glowing; Marcus leaning on it, spinning a drumstick.
3. Kayla winds the film on, eyes down on the camera, not on him: "My mom took the job. We move in June."
4. Close on Danny, half in trophy-case light. A beat too long. Then the grin arrives: "So I'm the one who stays behind. Cool. Love that for me."
5. Close on Kayla's thumb stopping on the wind-on lever.
6. Wide down the corridor from the back doors: Danny walks toward us and hits the push bar. Cold blue air. He does not look back.
7. Outside, looking in: Marcus holds the door with his shoulder. "You guys are leaving? It's halftime." Already turning: "Gotta get my board. You guys'll wait, right?"
8. Danny, on the step, breath fogging: "We'll be at the gate." Marcus: "Swear on the hat." Danny taps the red beanie: "On the hat."
9. Through the wired glass of the closing door: Marcus runs back toward the gym light, small and blurred by the wire mesh.
10. Kayla steps out and falls in beside Danny; the camera drifts into his eye-line: the dark lot, rows of cars.

**The image:** Marcus through the wired glass of the closing door, running back toward the light. Every player who finishes the game will want that frame back.

**The sound:** the film camera's real mechanical shutter and wind-on ratchet; then the push bar clanking shut on the gym's roar. No score.

**The trick:** all talk is standing still, one face at a time; the four key lines get phone-filmed faces, the rest is voice-driven. Danny's walk is far off, then from behind. Marcus's library run is broken up by the wire mesh. Hidden: the walk-and-talk, the run, the crowd (sound through a wall).

**The hand-back:** first person starts on shot 10's view, Kayla at his right shoulder; the parking-lot banter is play.

**The risk:** three teen faces in the first two minutes; if they read as adults, the game is judged by it.

**The fallback:** first person (the camera aimed at you, the flash, Marcus in the doorway), keeping shots 7 and 9. Danny's close-up returns as his reflection in the door glass.

## 2. "A Lot of Ceiling"

**Where:** Prologue, straight after the playable pulse. **Length:** 80 s.

**On screen:** Danny and Kayla, mostly from behind; the hunter's hands. **Speaking face:** Danny, one line.

**The shots:**
1. Very wide, low, behind the two among dead cars: the clouds light up from inside.
2. The mothership's underside comes down through the cloud without a sound, crawling with slow lights, until it is the whole sky.
3. Close on Danny, chin up, lit cold from above: "Okay. That's... a lot of ceiling."
4. Close on a hand: Kayla's fingers find his hoodie sleeve and close on it. They stay closed.
5. Behind them, the dark gym. The crowd noise inside drops, all at once, to nothing.
6. On the football field, between them and the gate, a small ship falls out of the dark onto the turf. Steam across the yard lines.
7. Close and low by the hatch: one hand comes out and spreads its fingers on the wet turf, long, pale, black veins over the knuckles. A second hand.
8. The two faces in the ship's glow. For once Danny has nothing to say.
9. The hatch again. Empty. Steam. Whatever came out is not there.
10. Kayla pulls him down by the sleeve, behind a car. Cut on the drop.

**The image:** two small figures from behind, a dead streetlight between them, under a sky that is all ship.

**The sound:** the gym going silent: a thousand people stopping at once behind a wall. Then the small ship's thump, felt more than heard. No score from shot 6 on, because the hunter is here.

**The trick:** nobody moves except one hand on a sleeve (Control Rig) and one crouch, hidden by the cut. The expensive picture, ship and sky, holds no people. The hunter is two hands; it unfolds in the cut between shots 8 and 9, and the player first sees it standing, turning its long face, through the bleacher slats in the sneak. Hidden: the crowd, the unfolding, the crouch.

**The hand-back:** the cut on the drop lands in first person, crouched behind the car, the field to your left; the sneak begins at once.

**The risk:** a mothership that reads as a painting; hands that read as rubber.

**The fallback:** shots 1 to 5 need no faces. If the hands fail, shot 7 becomes the hatch light flickering as something crosses it.

## 3. "The Chin's Too Low"

**Where:** Chapter 1, the first night in the shelter, once everyone is in. **Length:** 100 s.

**On screen:** all eight; three at most per shot after shot 1. **Speaking faces:** Kessler, Danny, Owen (a laugh).

**The shots:**
1. The one wide: eight people around a camping lantern on a water drum, the yellow-and-black fallout sign above, most of them in silhouette. Kessler stands with the sign-in sheet.
2. Close on her pen ticking names. Her voice, flat: "Six. Seven. Eight." Then, lower: "Eight."
3. Owen, fourteen, knees up, shaking. Ray beside him, looking at the door, not at anyone.
4. Danny, on a cot, cannot stand the silence: "Eight. Great number. Very... round."
5. Kessler turns her head, slowly: "Mr. Reyes. Is something funny?"
6. Danny, perfectly, chin in, flat: "Is something funny?"
7. Owen laughs: a snort, then a real laugh behind his hand. Mari grins at the floor.
8. Kessler, after a beat, almost a smile: "...The chin's too low."
9. Then she folds the sheet into her cardigan pocket and the smile is gone: "Nobody goes up. We stay behind this door until help comes."
10. Behind her, Gus looks at the steel door, then the generator. He does not nod.
11. Push-in on Danny, the laugh going out of his face, his eyes on the door.

**The image:** eight faces in pools of lantern light around a 1962 water drum, the shelter's yellow-and-black sign above them.

**The sound:** Owen's laugh, the first laugh anyone has heard since halftime, under the generator's hum. No score.

**The trick:** everyone sits or stands, carried by library idles and breathing; the impression exchange is phone-filmed. Darkness keeps all but the speaker half in shadow, which also keeps eight MetaHumans affordable. Hidden: anyone moving, any full body in light.

**The hand-back:** first person on his cot, facing the door, free to stand.

**The risk:** eight people in one room is the heaviest scene in the game. Owen's laugh must be a real recorded laugh.

**The fallback:** shot 1 becomes a pan across hands (pen, knees, fists). Or first person from the cot: the impression still lands, coming out of the player's "own" mouth.

**Later:** "Is something funny?" returns as Danny's last joke at the hold.

## 4. "Which, Historically"

**Where:** Chapter 1, later that night, after Owen's walk past the dark tunnel. **Length:** 95 s.

**On screen:** Mari, Danny, Kayla, Owen asleep. **Speaking face:** Danny; Mari hums, side-on in the dark.

**The shots:**
1. Close on a strumming hand: a cheap guitar, one bad string, Mari's thumb picking out the first notes of the camp song.
2. Wide from the floor: Mari on an upturned bucket, Owen asleep under a coat, Kayla's strapped ankle up on a crate.
3. Mari's face, side-on, eyes shut, humming more than singing.
4. Kayla, watching Danny, not Mari.
5. Danny, back against the generator, feeling it shake. He hums one bar along, wrong, and Mari laughs without stopping.
6. The song ends. Mari damps the strings with her palm. Silence apart from the diesel.
7. Close on Danny, not looking at anyone: "I'm getting everyone out. All of you." A breath. "Which, historically, from me, means nothing." Another. "But."
8. Hold on Danny. He does not finish the sentence.
9. Kayla's face. She is the only one who knows what "historically" means. She looks down.
10. Mari plays the last chord again, softly, a full stop.
11. Push-in on his eye-line: the sleeping Owen, the dark tunnel beyond the door.

**The image:** Mari on a bucket with the guitar, the generator's pilot light the only warm colour in the room, Owen asleep under a coat behind her.

**The sound:** the camp song, live, one string slightly flat, over the diesel hum: the game's first music comes from a person in the room. Then "But." and nothing after it.

**The trick:** nobody stands. Only the strumming hand is seen (a real player filmed on a phone, keyed with Control Rig); the fretting hand stays in shadow. Mari's face is side-on and dark. The promise is one phone-filmed face, with pauses only a real face can hold. Hidden: fingers on frets, anyone walking.

**The hand-back:** first person, sitting against the generator.

**The risk:** a fake strum looks cheap fastest; the promise could be overplayed.

**The fallback:** the guitar out of focus, strum as sound only; or the promise said in first person, looking at the sleeping Owen.

**Later:** the hunter whispers these words back at the hold, and "Yeah. That one I'm keeping" finishes the "But." If the owner chose the basketball team (6b), Kayla's look stays; only its meaning changes.

## 5. "Say That Again"

**Where:** Chapter 2, the turn, as the player comes back from the gym. **Length:** 70 s.

**On screen:** Priya, Ray, Kayla, Danny; a grainy feed on a monitor. **Speaking faces:** Priya, Ray.

**The shots:**
1. Over Priya's shoulder, a grainy feed: tiny soldiers loading frosted cradles into a small ship.
2. Priya's face lit grey by the screen, two rectangles of light in the glasses Danny brought her.
3. The feed, zoomed and blocky: one frosted lid. A pale patch fogs on the inside of it, fades, fogs again.
4. Priya, very quietly: "They're breathing."
5. Ray, behind her, out of the dark: "Say that again."
6. Priya, turning to him, louder, almost angry: "They're *breathing*."
7. Close on Ray's hand, big and scraped, pressing flat against the monitor glass over the cradle.
8. Ray's face, lit by the screen from below, and one breath that is not angry for the first time.
9. Kayla on her crate, looking at Danny. Danny looking at the screen.
10. Push-in on the feed over Danny's shoulder: the small ship's hatch closing on the cradles.

**The image:** Ray's hand flat on the monitor glass over a fogged cradle lid. It answers the hunter's hand flat on the wired glass in Chapter 1.

**The sound:** generator hum, monitor whine, then Ray's one ragged breath. One low piano note enters under shot 8 and holds: the score's first hope. The hunter is far upstairs.

**The trick:** the action is on a rough security feed, which costs almost nothing; the people barely move; the touch is a hand on a screen. Two short phone-filmed faces. Hidden: soldiers and cradles up close, anyone moving.

**The hand-back:** first person at the monitor, the feed still playing; Ray's hand slides off the glass as you take control.

**The risk:** the fogging lid must read on a grainy feed in two seconds.

**The fallback:** first person behind Priya; Ray's hand enters your view.

## 6. "Good News and Bad"

**Where:** Chapter 3, home after the Mark and the live floor. **Length:** 75 s.

**On screen:** Danny, Owen, Kayla, Kessler and Ray in the background. **Speaking faces:** Danny, Owen.

**The shots:**
1. Behind the steel door, inside: Gus drags the bar. Danny comes through it hurt, sleeve pulled down over his left hand.
2. A slow pan across faces in lamp light, ending on Mari's made cot, her guitar on its nail above it.
3. Danny, a grin already on, already too fast: "So. Good news and bad—"
4. He stops. Close on his face. The grin stays for a second after the joke has gone.
5. Owen, half up from his cot: "Where's Mari?"
6. Danny: "...Yeah."
7. Wide from the floor: he sits down on the nearest cot, which is hers. Nobody moves.
8. Close on his hand taking the packet of guitar strings out of the hoodie pocket and setting it on her blanket.
9. Gus pushes the steel door closed. On the nail, the guitar's strings ring faintly with the slam, a soft, out-of-tune chord nobody played.
10. Kayla, across the room, sees the sleeve pulled over his hand. She does not ask yet.
11. Push-in on his eye-line: the strings on the blanket.

**The image:** Danny sitting on Mari's empty cot, the strings packet on the blanket beside him, the guitar on the nail above his head.

**The sound:** the guitar on the wall ringing by itself when the steel door slams. No score.

**The trick:** one walk in, from behind; then everybody is still. The sit-down is a floor-level wide, cut on the bend. Two phone-filmed lines; the rest is faces doing nothing. The Mark stays under a sleeve, saved for the player's own first-person look. Hidden: the injured walk, the Mark, any big reaction.

**The hand-back:** first person on Mari's cot. Leave the strings (Owen restrings the guitar by the next return) or take them. Kayla's Mark talk comes later, in play.

**The risk:** "...Yeah." carries the chapter. Played sad, it dies; it must be flat.

**The fallback:** first person; Owen asks the camera; the guitar's ring survives any version.

## 7. "Ruth, It's Me"

**Where:** Chapter 4, night, back from the Mimic floor; Ray went out alone for Caleb's jacket. **Length:** 75 s.

**On screen:** Kessler and Danny; Ray only as a voice and knocks. **Speaking faces:** Kessler, Danny.

**The shots:**
1. Black. Three knocks on steel, heavy, then a fourth, weaker.
2. The steel door from inside, wide, the bar across it, its small window dark. Kessler stands in front of it with the sign-in sheet pressed to her chest.
3. Ray's voice through the door, muffled and real: "It's Ray. Ruth. It's Ray. I got his jacket."
4. Danny steps into frame beside her. Close on both, her face lit from below by a lamp on the floor.
5. Danny, low: "That's Ray."
6. Kessler, not looking at him: "The things up there sound like people."
7. Knocks again, a different rhythm. Ray: "Ruth. Please. Open the door." (A Mimic repeats itself exactly. He just didn't, and a player who learned the floor can hear it.)
8. Close on Kessler's thumb on the sign-in sheet, over a name: Dobbs. She does not cross it out. She does not let go either.
9. Danny looks at the tunnel hatch. A long beat: the old Danny would walk away from this.
10. Kessler, very quietly, to the door, not to Danny: "I count my kids, Mr. Reyes. I can't count him."
11. Push-in on Danny's eye-line: the tunnel hatch.

**The image:** Kessler's back against the steel door, the sheet held to her chest, Danny beside her in half-light, the door's window black.

**The sound:** the knocking, and the difference between the first knocks and the second: an alive man changes his rhythm. No score.

**The trick:** two people standing in near-dark, phone-filmed; knocks recorded on a real steel door. Hidden: Ray entirely, which is the point: the player decides without seeing him.

**The hand-back:** first person facing the hatch: go round and test Ray, or stay.

**The risk:** Kessler becoming a villain. Shot 8 and line 10 are there to stop that; she has to sound frightened, not cold.

**The fallback:** first person at the door, Kessler's face in front of you. It loses almost nothing and keeps the choice in the player's hands; if faces test badly, do this first.

## 8. "Somebody Keeps the Lights On"

**Where:** Chapter 5, the roof, the moment the chase ends in the helicopter's light. **Length:** 105 s.

**On screen:** Gus, Danny, Kayla, Kessler, the rest in silhouette, the hunter far off. **Speaking faces:** Gus, Kessler, Danny.

**The shots:**
1. Wide, rain sideways in the searchlight cone. The helicopter is a blinding light, a skid and the edge of an open door; crew in helmets and dark visors wave people in.
2. Kessler, shouting over the rotors, reading from her list (or, if the group knows about the seats, folding the list away unread and stepping back beside Gus).
3. Figures run out of frame into the light, one at a time, each lost in the glare.
4. Danny at the door's edge, turning back: Gus has not moved.
5. Close on Gus, rain on his glasses, the keys on his belt: "I don't fly. Never have."
6. Danny: "Gus."
7. Gus: "Somebody keeps the lights on, son. Go on." (If Danny brought his hearing-aid batteries, Gus touches his ear first: he can hear it coming.)
8. Over Gus's shoulder, at the far edge of the light by the stair hut: something tall, stooped, still. The searchlight swings across it; for twelve frames its long pale face turns into the light, two pins in two black eyes. Then the light swings on.
9. Gus strikes a flare. Red fire. He walks away from the helicopter toward the stair hut, his back to us, keys swinging.
10. Kayla's hand comes into frame and closes on Danny's hood (the second sleeve-grab). He lets himself be pulled.
11. Inside the door, looking back out: Gus's flare, small and red, at the stair hut, and the dark beyond it.

**The image:** Gus walking away into the rain toward the dark with a red flare held up, his keys swinging, the helicopter's glare behind him.

**The sound:** Gus's keys. The mix drops the rotors for his lines and the keys jingle under them; the flare strikes and the rotors return like a wave. No score: the hunter is on the roof.

**The trick:** the helicopter is only light, rain, noise and a door edge. Boarding is people running out of frame into glare, from behind. The hunter only stands and turns its face. Gus walks from behind; Kayla is a hand. Hidden: the helicopter, the boarding, any lifting, the hunter moving.

**The hand-back:** first person in the cabin as it lifts, free to look; Song 1 begins.

**The risk:** wind and rain on MetaHuman hair; the hunter's face looks like a costume if held a frame too long.

**The fallback:** first person at the cabin door: Gus's lines to camera, the flare walking away, the hunter in one searchlight sweep.

**Ledger variants:** who runs into the light (shot 3); Kessler reading or folding the list (shot 2); Gus's ear touch (shot 7). Whether Gus lives is only told in June.

## 9. "It's Me. Again."

**Where:** Chapter 6, the wreck, after twenty minutes of talking into a dead radio. **Length:** 65 s.

**On screen:** Danny alone. **Speaking face:** Danny.

**The shots:**
1. Wide, rain, a dark street: the helicopter on its side across two cars, its rotor bent into a shopfront.
2. Danny from behind, torch beam on the open cabin. Empty seats.
3. Close on his thumb on the talk button. Quiet: "Kayla. ...Hey. It's me. Again."
4. From inside the wreck, small and tinny, his own voice: "...Hey. It's me. Again."
5. Danny freezes. Close on his face in the torch spill.
6. Torch on a seat: Kayla's walkie lying in rainwater, its little red light blinking. Next to it, her grandfather's film camera, strap tangled in a seatbelt.
7. His hand picks up the camera, then the walkie. He turns hers off. The red light goes out.
8. Close on Danny. The joke tries to come and does not: "...Cool. So I've been talking to a seat."
9. Push-in on his eye-line: the film camera in his hands, the counter on the top showing frames left.

**The image:** a walkie blinking red on a wet seat in a dead helicopter, Danny's own voice coming out of it.

**The sound:** his own voice coming back at him out of the wreck, half a second late. No score; rain on metal.

**The trick:** one man crouched in rain and torchlight; one pick-up, close on hands; a static wreck. Hidden: the crash, anyone else.

**The hand-back:** first person, looking down at the camera in his hands. Talking still works afterwards; still nobody answers.

**The risk:** the line in shot 8 must be throwaway, not sad, or it turns into self-pity.

**The fallback:** first person: press talk and hear yourself in the seat. It is a cutscene only because it is the city's one look at Danny's face.

**Owner choice:** unchanged if soldiers take Kayla (3c). Her walkie at the wreck is in the map; the echo is staging, not plot.

## 10. "Got Your Stick"

**Where:** Chapter 8, the nest, after the Spine. **Length:** 85 s.

**On screen:** Danny; Marcus and Mari asleep in its cradles; the hunter heard, never seen. **Speaking face:** Danny.

**The shots:**
1. Wide, dim: an alcove of wet membrane and frost, a few cradles in a curve. No light but Danny's torch and a slow pulse in the walls.
2. Marcus in a cradle, eyes shut, frost in his eyebrows, headphones still round his neck. Calm. Whole.
3. Mari beyond him, the same. Danny's torch does not stay on her long.
4. From somewhere deep in the nest, the hunter whispers in Marcus's cracked voice: "you'll... wait..."
5. Close on Marcus's lips: they move with the words. He is not saying them. They are being said through him.
6. Danny takes the drumstick from his back pocket.
7. Close on hands: Danny lays the stick in Marcus's open palm. The fingers do not close. Danny folds them shut over it with his own hand, and lets go.
8. Danny, very quiet, crouched: "Hey. Got your stick."
9. A beat. He touches the brim of the red beanie, the old promise, and does not say it.
10. Far off, heavy steps. Coming home.
11. Push-in on his eye-line: Marcus's hand closed round the drumstick.

**The image:** two hands, one frost-pale, closed together round a drumstick with worn tape on the grip.

**The sound:** Marcus's own voice whispered from the dark, while his lips move with it. No score, only the nest's murmur and then the steps.

**The trick:** the taken lie still; breathing and a lip shape keyed to the whisper is all they need. The crouch hides in the cut to the hands; the touch is Control Rig, under three seconds. The hunter is sound only. Hidden: the hunter, any movement of the taken, anything graphic.

**The hand-back:** first person, crouched at Marcus's hand, the steps already coming: the first thing you do is leave.

**The risk:** Marcus and Mari must not look dead or hurt. Frost and calm faces, nothing wet on them.

**The fallback:** first person, as the draft had it: the player places the stick with a held button. Keep shot 5.

**Owner choice:** with the Voice Collectors, the lips moving with the whisper is literally what the ship does. With the Harvest (1a) or the Return (1c), drop shot 5 and keep the rest. If Marcus were a survivor (5b), this scene does not exist.

## 11. "You Have a Thing"

**Where:** Chapter 8, the pod bay, after the self-destruct and the run. **Length:** 100 s.

**On screen:** Danny outside the pod, Kayla inside it, a curved glass between them. **Speaking faces:** Danny, Kayla.

**The shots:**
1. Wide: the pod bay coming apart in slow pieces, sparks falling, a row of pods with a face at each small window.
2. Through the pod glass: Kayla strapped in, Danny outside, both faces close, the glass between them catching sparks.
3. Danny: "Go. I'm right behind you."
4. Kayla, a long look: "You're lying. You do the thing with your mouth."
5. Danny, the grin almost making it: "I don't have a thing."
6. Kayla: "You have a thing, Danny."
7. Close: her hand flat on the inside of the glass. His goes flat on the outside, over it. (The third hand on glass. The first was the hunter's.)
8. The pod's seal closes with a hiss; her voice is cut off mid-breath, muffled.
9. A click on the radio on Danny's chest, and her voice, clear in his ear: "Stay on the line, okay?"
10. Danny takes his hand off the glass. "Yeah."
11. Push-in on his eye-line, turning away: the release panel, and one glyph lit on it. **Hold.**

**The image:** two hands flat on either side of a pod window, sparks running down the glass between them.

**The sound:** Kayla's voice moving from muffled behind glass to clear on his radio. No score.

**The trick:** two still faces with glass between them: reflections, sparks and condensation soften every edge, and the bodies never touch. Spend the best phone-filmed takes here. Hidden: any embrace, the pod's mechanism, the ship's destruction (only sparks at the edges).

**The hand-back:** shot 11 ends at the release panel; the first-person hold begins on the same frame, the player's hand going to it.

**Ledger variants:** best ("Everyone he could"): as written. "The line": she says "Okay," then "Stay on the line," and they talk to the white. "The recording": she believes him; shot 9 is her saying "Right behind me," dazed. "Only her": she is asleep in the pod; shots 3 to 6 are said to her sleeping face, and shot 9 is silence.

**The risk:** the two most important faces, held close for a minute; if they are not ready, it shows here first.

**The fallback:** first person through the glass, Kayla's face close in the window, hands on the glass from Danny's side only. The scene loses Danny's face and nothing else.

## 12. "We Waited"

**Where:** Epilogue, June, as Kayla, after the wall, the chores, the last frame of her film (Danny pulling a face) and his tape. **Length:** 90 s.

**On screen:** Kayla; whoever lived, standing at the gate. **Speaking face:** whoever is first at the gate (two words); Kayla only laughs.

**The shots:**
1. Wide, June evening: the chain-link gate from the field where the small ship landed. New grass.
2. Kayla from behind, walking to it with her film camera round her neck.
3. Reverse: on the other side of the gate, the people who lived, standing still. (With "Everyone he could": Gus, Owen with the guitar, Priya, Ray and Caleb, Kessler. With fewer, fewer. With "Only her": no one.)
4. Close on Kayla's face. She stops.
5. Whoever is first (Owen, if he lived): "We waited."
6. Kayla's face. A laugh she did not want, crying. The same laugh as at the hold.
7. She lifts the film camera, one eye shut. *Click,* the flash. The frame whites out, like the first shot of the game.
8. White fades back to the gate. One chord on Mari's old guitar, if Owen is there; Song 3 begins.
9. Push-in on her eye-line: the gate, open.

**"Only her" version:** shot 3 is the empty gate; shot 5 is silence; shot 6 is Kayla photographing the empty gate anyway; Song 3 begins on one voice.

**The image:** a group of tired people standing still at a chain-link gate in June light, and Kayla's flash going off at them.

**The sound:** the shutter, the same click as in scene 1. Then Song 3.

**The trick:** everyone at the gate stands still; Kayla walks from behind; one face says two words. Hidden: hugs; the photo replaces them.

**The hand-back:** first person as Kayla at the open gate. The credits roll over play: she walks through and down the street, with them or alone, and the game ends with the song.

**The risk:** a crowd of up to seven standing in daylight is the most exposed shot in the game. The line "We waited" tips into melodrama if anyone acts it.

**The fallback:** shoot shot 3 from far off, faces too small to read, and play shots 4 to 9 from Kayla's eyes. The flash and the chord survive any version.

## The six story moments that stay in first person

Fear belongs in the player's hands, but each of these is still directed.

1. **The skateboard (end of the prologue).** Movement locked, look free. The corridor lights die fixture by fixture toward the door; the board rolls in only once the player is looking at the window, slows, stops, one wheel still turning. Two seconds. The last light dies. Title.
2. **The hand on the glass (Chapter 1).** The player is looking at Ray in the doorway; the hunter's hand is flat on the far door's wired glass behind him, in frame, never pointed at. Some see it first; everyone sees Gus see it, because his face drops and his torch swings. The camera is never moved for the player.
3. **The Mark (Chapter 3).** The whisper behind; the player turns, which is the trap. The floor drops away; the view is turned down to Danny's forearm and the long finger enters from above, the favourite picture's angle, the face stooping out of the dark into one stage light. Black. Mari, once, far: "Danny." He wakes looking at his own arm as the frost-lines spread.
4. **The two Kaylas (Chapter 4).** The yellow jacket, facing away, "Danny, over here" three times with the same timing. The player holds a key to whisper "What time?"; it asks it back in her voice. Her real voice from right behind; the player turns and her face is close in the torch. The best joke-under-fear in the game is played face to face.
5. **The parachute (Chapter 5).** Song 1, the city small below, her hand beside his; the missile cuts the song mid-line. Then the mix drops everything: her face fills the view and only her hands are seen, clipping the chute to his chest (the two-body problem vanishes in first person). "Go. I'm right behind you." The shove, the canopy, and below, on the wreck, the tall shape lifting a yellow jacket. The player can do nothing; that is the point.
6. **The hold (Chapter 8).** One button: his hand on the release. A face at each pod window; the Mark climbing the arm in view; the hunter crouched close, whispering the promise in Danny's own voice; "Yeah. That one I'm keeping." The other hand spends the last flare, cell and extinguisher. Over the radio, "Miss Brooks. Is something funny?", her laugh, crying, the true thing, then its hand, then white. No music.

**Also first person:** the pulse, the breach, the lift, the landing searchlight, his note under his mother's (his own hand writing it), the Takedown, the climb monologue, waking Kayla and "If I get in one, they follow it" (she wakes looking straight at the player), and every catch and death.

## The three I would fight for

1. **"Good News and Bad" (6).** The cheapest scene in the game and the one that decides whether the player cares: a joke that dies halfway, one question, a guitar ringing by itself when a door slams. Nobody says a sad word, and it hurts.
2. **"It's Me. Again." (9).** Twenty minutes of talking into a dead radio, paid off by his own voice coming out of a seat. One man, one wreck, rain; it keeps every rule, and it is the city's only look at his face.
3. **"You Have a Thing" (11).** The whole friendship in four lines through a pane of glass, the third hand on glass after the hunter's and Ray's, and her voice moving from the glass to his radio, where it stays to the white.
