# Stay Behind: the twelve cutscenes (the spectacle take)

**The lens: scale and image.** A cutscene here is cool when the player sees something he has never seen: a ship the size of a district putting out a town's last lights, a long hand on wet turf, a searchlight standing in the rain on a school roof, pods leaving a dying ship like seeds at dawn. Scale is cheap when it is far, dark and still, so in this take the big things are always far, dark and slow, and the money goes where it shows: two faces held close, one hand, one object.

**Rules every scene keeps**
- **Far things are big, near things are small.** Ships, skies and cities are wide, slow shots in darkness and weather. People are close, short shots, half in shadow. Almost nothing in between, because a whole body moving in full light is where a game without motion capture looks fake.
- **The camera does the acting.** When a body would have to do something hard (run, lift, fight, unfold), the camera looks at a face, a hand, a light or a doorway, and the sound tells you what happened.
- **One image, one sound, at most one line** per scene, chosen before anything else.
- **In and out through Danny's eyes.** Each scene opens by pulling back out of his head and closes by pushing into his eye-line. The last frame lines up with the first-person view the player gets back (a "matched cut": the two frames match, so the cut is invisible), sound runs on across the cut, and control returns within two seconds. Every scene is skippable and subtitled.
- **Talk is clear over everything.** Rain, rotors and engines dip under every line.
- **Music obeys the game:** no score while the hunter is near; the three songs where the map puts them; no music in the hold.

## Part 1: the twelve

### 1. Flash
**Where:** Prologue, about 6 minutes in. In first person the player has walked the trophy corridor; Kayla raises her grandfather's film camera, and the flash starts the scene. **100 s.**
**On screen:** Danny, Kayla, Marcus; all three faces speak.

1. White: the flash and the film shutter's click; out of the white, behind Danny's shoulder, the silly face he pulled melting off. (The photo is not shown until June.)
2. From behind, the two walk a dark corridor toward glass doors; across the black lot the stadium's four light banks burn white in the cold. The crowd roars through the wall; the buzzer.
3. Kayla, close, walking, not looking at him: "My mom took the job. We move in June."
4. Danny, close, half a second too long before the grin: "So I'm the one who stays behind. Cool. Love that for me."
5. From outside the glass: he shoves the bar and pushes out, breath showing; she waits a beat behind the glass, then follows.
6. The doors bang open: Marcus, drumstick spinning, gym light behind him: "You guys are leaving? It's halftime."
7. Over his shoulder, already backing away: "I left my board. You guys'll wait, right?"
8. Danny: "We'll be at the gate." Marcus: "Swear on the hat." Danny touches his red beanie: "On the hat."
9. Through the door glass Marcus runs back down the lit corridor and round the corner; the door swings shut and the roar goes muffled.
10. Low behind the two: the dark lot, the far white lights. Push to Danny's eye height.

**Image:** shot 2, two kids from behind walking toward glass doors, the stadium lights hanging in the cold beyond: the last picture of the normal world. **Sound:** the roar through cinder block, cut to a murmur by the closing door: everyone the hunter is about to take. **Line:** "So I'm the one who stays behind."
**Trick:** library walks (Epic's Game Animation Sample) seen from behind, where a walk always looks right; faces only when people stop, acted on a phone and solved from the video. Marcus runs small, from behind, through glass. The crowd is sound and yellow light round a door.
**Hand-back:** cut to first person on the same view across the lot, Kayla at his left; the walk is played.
**Risk:** teen faces reading as twenty-five; Marcus's run. **Fallback:** the walk in first person, with shots 3, 4 and 8 as close faces in the doorway's dim; if faces fail, everything is first person and shot 2 is a still frame over the sound.

### 2. Last Lights
**Where:** Prologue, about 12 minutes in. The player lived the pulse in first person: phones hot and dead, the streetlights dying street by street toward him. The scene starts when the dark reaches the school. **120 s.**
**On screen:** Danny and Kayla (backs, then faces in the dark), the mothership, the small ship, one long hand. Only Kayla speaks.

1. Very wide over the town: every street dark; the stadium's four light banks the only light for miles; two tiny figures among the cars.
2. The clouds light from above, as if dawn were coming from the wrong side.
3. Low behind the two, looking up: an endless dark underside slides through the cloud, crawling with slow lights like traffic seen from a plane. No sound.
4. Wide on the stadium: the ship's shadow crosses the field and the light banks die one at a time, each with a heavy clunk. The fourth holds, buzzing.
5. Kayla's face in the last white light, whispering: "Danny."
6. The fourth bank dies. Black, one full second.
7. A point of light falls from the belly and becomes a small ship slamming onto the 50-yard line in a burst of steam lit from inside.
8. A hatch slides open. Nothing for three seconds.
9. Close and low on the wet turf: one long pale hand comes down flat, fingers spreading, veins dark.
10. Cut to the two faces behind a car in the steam's glow; off screen, a long run of joints opening one after another, like a ladder pulled to full height.
11. Wide: it is standing, three metres tall in the steam, stooped, hooded, still. It has not turned.
12. Push into Danny's eyes between two cars.

**Image:** shot 4, a ship the size of a district putting out a football field's lights one bank at a time. **Sound:** the four clunks, then total silence, because the mothership makes none. No score: the hunter is coming.
**Trick:** the mothership is one big low-detail model with glowing lights above volumetric cloud, moving very little; the stadium is four lights switching off; the small ship drops inside steam that hides its edges. The crawl and the unfolding, the hardest animation in the game, are never shown: the hand is one hand-posed close-up, the unfolding is a sound over two faces, and the next wide shot is a still pose.
**Hand-back:** first person crouched in the same gap, Kayla's breath beside him. The sneak begins, and in play the hunter turns its long face toward the school: its first act is in the player's hands.
**Risk:** the ship like a painting; the hand like a rubber glove. **Fallback:** keep the ship inside cloud, seen only as lights moving across it (cheaper and scarier); replace the hand with a wet slap on turf, heard over black.

### 3. The Board
**Where:** Prologue end. The player crept back in, Kayla fell on the basement stairs, Gus said "You two. Down. Now." Gus swings the steel door shut. **50 s, then the title.**
**On screen:** Gus's hands and back, a skateboard. No faces; one whisper.

1. Close on Gus's hands dropping the bar into its brackets. Clang.
2. Through the door's small wired window: a long corridor, its tubes still lit.
3. The tubes die from the far end toward the door, one by one, a soft tick each: the pulse again, indoors.
4. In the last two tubes' light, a skateboard rolls into frame from a side hall on its own, its wheels clicking over the tile joints.
5. It slows, clicks, slows, and stops in the middle of the corridor.
6. The last tube goes out. Black window.
7. Black. From just behind the door, close, the announcer's words broken: "...remain... in your... seats."
8. STAY BEHIND, white on black, in silence.

**Image:** a skateboard alone under the last tube, through wired glass; everyone knows whose it is. **Sound:** the wheels' clicks over the joints, further and further apart, until they stop. **Line:** "...remain... in your... seats."
**Trick:** no body to animate: two hands, one physics object on a guided path, a timed row of lights, a whisper. Almost free, and one of the strongest scenes in the game.
**Hand-back:** after the title, first person facing the black window; Gus's torch clicks on behind: "Away from the glass." Chapter 1 begins.
**Risk:** the roll looking pushed by a script. **Fallback:** hear it roll in the dark and show it already stopped in the last light.
*If the owner had chosen otherwise:* with Marcus a survivor (decision 5b) the board means nothing and the scene goes.

### 4. Count
**Where:** Chapter 1, the first night. The player has started the generator with Gus, fetched food through the tunnels and lived through the first outage. **120 s.**
**On screen:** all eight, never more than three lit at once. Faces that speak: Kessler, Danny; Mari plays; Owen laughs.

1. High and wide from the shelter stairs: eight people on cots and crates in the pool of one caged bulb, the yellow-and-black 1962 signs, black to every wall, the steel door at the light's edge. The generator hums.
2. Kessler standing, finger moving down the room: "Seven. Eight." A pause. She counts again.
3. Danny murmurs a joke to Kayla. Kessler, without turning: "Mr. Reyes. Is something funny?"
4. Danny, in her voice, perfectly: "Is something funny?"
5. Owen laughs for the first time since the field, and covers his mouth.
6. Kessler, after a beat: "...The chin's too low." Then, to the room: "We stay behind this door until help comes."
7. Over Mari's shoulder: she takes her guitar and starts the camp song; her hands in shadow, the strings catching the bulb.
8. A slow move along faces in the light: Kayla's leg up on a crate, Priya hugging her knees, Ray staring at the door, Gus in the dark mouthing the words.
9. Later: Danny on the floor of the generator room, lit by its red indicator lamp, the others silhouettes in the doorway.
10. Danny: "I'm getting everyone out. All of you. Which, historically, from me, means nothing. But."
11. Push into his eye-line on the generator's gauge.

**Image:** shot 1, eight small people in one pool of light in a 1962 shelter: this game's picture of home. **Sound:** the hum under everything, and three chords on a cheap guitar; the song is the only music. **Line:** the promise, which the hunter says back at the end; the Kessler impression sets up Danny's last words.
**Trick:** seated and standing people with small moves (a turn, a laugh, a hand to a mouth) from phone-filmed one-camera capture. Three faces lit at a time; the other five sit at low detail in shadow, which keeps the heaviest people scene inside the PC's budget. Guitar hands over the shoulder, two short hand-posed close-ups.
**Hand-back:** first person sitting at the generator, the gauge ahead. Owen's request follows in play.
**Risk:** eight MetaHumans in one room; the guitar hands. **Fallback:** shot 1 as a wide over the heard count, the talk in first person; if the hands fail, the song is heard while the camera stays on faces listening.

### 5. Breath on the Glass
**Where:** Chapter 2, the turn. Back in the shelter after Halftime Draw, Priya calls him to the monitors. **75 s.**
**On screen:** Priya, Ray, Kayla, Danny; soldiers and cradles on a feed. Faces that speak: Priya, Ray.

1. Close on the old monitor: a grainy, jerky security feed of the rear lot in rain, the small ship lit, a line of soldier lamps.
2. Priya's face, blue from the screen, the glasses he found reflecting it.
3. The feed: frosted cradles float out of the school's back doors on their own along a ribbon of light, a soldier walking beside each. Nobody carries anything.
4. Priya turns the zoom knob; the picture jumps closer in blocky steps.
5. One cradle lid, white with frost. Inside, a round patch of fog blooms on the glass and fades. Again.
6. Priya, very quietly: "They're breathing."
7. Ray, getting up from the dark behind her: "Say that again."
8. Priya, turning to him: "They're *breathing*."
9. Kayla at the radio desk, hand over her mouth; the next cradle passes behind her on the screen.
10. Danny's view as the small ship's hatch closes in the rain.

**Image:** a breath fogging a frosted lid on a grainy security camera: hope and horror in one dot of fog. **Sound:** rain on the feed's tinny speaker and the old monitor's whine; the line lands in silence. **Line:** "They're breathing."
**Trick:** soldiers and cradles exist only on a low-frame-rate, grainy feed, which hides the animation, and the cradles float, so no one lifts or carries. Faces are lit by one screen.
**Hand-back:** first person at the monitor desk; the evening is played.
**Risk:** the feed too clean. **Fallback:** all of it in first person at the desk: the feed is a screen in the world and the lines are spoken around the player.

### 6. The Window
**Where:** Chapter 4, the turn. The player came down from the third floor with Kayla. After dark, knocking: Ray went out alone for Caleb's letterman jacket. **75 s.**
**On screen:** Kessler, Danny, Gus; Ray through the window. Faces that speak: Kessler, Danny, Ray (muffled).

1. Wide from the cots: the steel door, its small wired window a grey square, and knocking: three, three, three.
2. Through the window: Ray's face pressed close, lit by his own dying torch, breath fogging the glass.
3. He lifts Caleb's jacket to the glass, the letter flat against the wires: "Ruth. It's me. I've got his jacket."
4. Kessler, back flat to the door, hand on the bar: "We don't know that's him."
5. Danny: "It's Ray. Ms. Kessler, it's Ray."
6. Kessler, not looking at him: "Nobody opens this door. Not after dark."
7. Ray's torch flickers; for half a second the corridor behind him shows, empty, or nearly.
8. Gus by the tunnel hatch says nothing; he tilts his torch so its beam lands on the hatch, and looks at Danny.
9. Danny's eye-line: the hatch in Gus's beam.

**Image:** a man's face and his son's jacket pressed to a small wired window: the frame where Chapter 1 showed the hunter's hand, now a person. **Sound:** the knock, always exactly three and three, until you wonder whether a person knocks that evenly. **Line:** "Nobody opens this door. Not after dark."
**Trick:** Ray is a face behind wired glass and his own fog, which hides the lip-sync; his voice comes through steel. Kessler stands still. Gus's whole performance is one tilt of a torch.
**Hand-back:** first person on the hatch in Gus's beam; the choice (test him through the tunnel, or leave the door shut) is played.
**Risk:** Ray's face through glass looking like a texture. **Fallback:** the window stays black except for the jacket pressed to it; Ray is a voice.

### 7. Five Seats
**Where:** Chapter 5. The player has just survived the hunter's long chase over the roof and run into the helicopter's searchlight. **110 s.**
**On screen:** the hunter (far, silhouette), the survivors left, the crew chief (helmet, no face), Gus. Faces that speak: Kessler, Danny, Gus.

1. Very wide from the next roof: the school black in rain; the helicopter a dark mass behind a searchlight whose beam is a solid white column of rain.
2. Long lens (one that flattens distance) across the roof: at the beam's far edge the hunter stands to its full height, still, its long face to the light.
3. The beam swings onto it and flares white straight into the lens.
4. The flare clears: the parapet is empty. Below the edge, one heavy step on metal.
5. The crew chief's gloved hand in the beam, five fingers spread.
6. Kessler in the rain, the soaked roll book open, reading names. (If the group knows about the seats, no list: they look at each other and say names.)
7. Silhouettes duck one at a time into the white and are gone; each cut lands on the next.
8. Gus, rain on his face, stepping back out of the light: "I don't fly. Never have."
9. Danny: "Gus."
10. Gus: "Somebody keeps the lights on, son. Go on." He walks into the dark toward the roof hatch, his torch a small yellow dot.
11. Danny's eye-line into the white; a hand reaches out of it for him.

**Image:** shot 2, a three-metre silhouette standing inside a searchlight beam on a school roof in the rain. **Sound:** the rotors, dipping for each line, and the single heavy step on metal after it has gone. No score while it is near; the music is held back so Song 1 starts the moment they rise away from it. **Line:** "Somebody keeps the lights on, son."
**Trick:** the helicopter is a light, rotor wind on puddles and a dark shape above the glare, never a detailed hovering model. The hunter is a still pose that leaves behind a lens flare, so its fast exit is never seen. Boarding, which would be a moving group, is silhouettes stepping into white, one per cut. Gus walks away from camera. Rain on close faces hides a lot.
**Hand-back:** first person in the cabin door, the crew chief's glove pulling him in; scene 8 follows.
**Risk:** rain and wind on faces and hair; several versions of shots 6 and 7 for who boards. **Fallback:** board in first person in the beam; keep shots 1, 2 and 8 to 10; if faces fail, Gus speaks as a silhouette against the light.

### 8. The Lift
**Where:** Chapter 5, straight after boarding. **80 s.**
**On screen:** Danny, Kayla, whoever boarded, the crew chief. Faces that speak: Danny, Kayla (barely).

1. Very wide from below: the helicopter lifts off the roof; in the black town the school shows one lit basement window. Gus is keeping the lights on.
2. Kayla at the cabin window, the city sliding below her face. Song 1 begins: the score's voice, or Owen humming if he boarded.
3. Her hand on the bench beside Danny's, a finger's width apart.
4. Exterior, wide: the helicopter a small light climbing between the dead city and the lit belly that fills the top of the frame.
5. Danny's face, looking at her for a long moment.
6. Far below, a small light lifts off a roof and draws a thin line upward.
7. Danny: "Kayla—" She turns.
8. The song mid-word. White.

**Image:** shot 4, a tiny helicopter between a dead city and a ship's underside that covers the sky: the game's one picture of hope. **Sound:** Song 1 cut off mid-word, a quarter-second of silent white, then first person screaming. **Line:** "Kayla—", the thing he never gets to say.
**Trick:** a helicopter in flight is a rigid model on a path; nothing bends. Inside, two seated faces and two still hands. The missile is a light on a line. The hit is a cut to white: no crash, no fire, no bodies.
**Hand-back:** out of the white, first person in the spinning cabin; control is his in the white itself.
**Risk:** cabin and window view not matching in scale. **Fallback:** the lift in first person, looking round only, with shots 1 and 4 as the two exterior views.
*If the owner had chosen otherwise:* if soldiers took Kayla (decision 3c) the lift is unchanged; only what Danny sees falling changes.

### 9. The Belly
**Where:** Chapter 7, end of the flight. The player has steered through cloud while Song 2 played and, if he held the talk button, said what he never said. The cloud thins. **60 s.**
**On screen:** the stolen ship and the mothership; no faces.

1. Pull back out of the cockpit through the canopy: the small ship alone in white cloud.
2. Wide: it breaks out of the cloud top, a dart on a grey sea, the first hint of dawn in the east.
3. Tilt up: the mothership's underside covers the whole sky, ribbed, crawling with slow lights, lit hangar mouths in rows like windows in a tower lying on its side.
4. Very wide from far off: the ship is a spark against the belly; you have to look for it.
5. Close past the hull: frosted windows, rows and rows of them, each a cradle. Thousands.
6. One hangar mouth ahead, lit; the ship heads in too fast.
7. Sparks on the hangar floor; black on contact.
8. In the black: metal folding, then silence and a slow drip.

**Image:** shot 3, a ship so big it is the sky. **Sound:** Song 2's last phrase ends as the cloud clears, and in the gap comes the ship: a deep hum and, under it, too far to make out words, the murmur of thousands of sleeping voices. The ship is listening.
**Trick:** no characters at all. The same far model as the prologue, the detail spent only on the strip of hull in shot 5. The crash is a cut to black; the sound does the damage.
**Hand-back:** first person in the wrecked cockpit, canopy cracked, the player's hand on the release.
**Risk:** the belly reading as a repeated texture. **Fallback:** keep it in the dark before dawn, only its lights showing.
*If the owner had chosen otherwise:* under the Harvest or the Return (decision 1a or 1c) the voices go and the hum stays.

### 10. The Talk
**Where:** Chapter 8, about 20 minutes into the last hour. Kayla and whoever he could reach are awake; the pods are locked to the core. **90 s, one version per ending shape.**
**On screen:** Danny, Kayla; both faces speak.

1. Very wide, low: two kids sitting on the floor between rows of frosted cradles that run away into blue haze for what looks like a kilometre.
2. Danny pushes up his sleeve: the black frost-lines have climbed past the elbow.
3. Kayla's torch finds the arm. If she has seen it before: "It's higher." If not, a long silence, then: "How long?"
4. Danny, half in the torchlight: "If I get in one, they follow it. They follow you."
5. Kayla understands before he finishes: "No."
6. The two from behind, the cradles murmuring; a nearby lid fogs and clears.
7. Danny starts a joke and lets it go unfinished.
8. Kayla, the only time her face fills the screen: "You're not your dad, Danny."
9. Wide again, two small figures among thousands; he stands. Push into his eye-line toward the core.

**Image:** shot 1, two teenagers on the floor of a hall of thousands of sleepers fading into haze. **Sound:** the sleepers' murmur, so close you can almost make out words. Three notes of the Danny-and-Kayla melody on the detuned piano, once (the hunter is far). **Line:** "If I get in one, they follow it. They follow you."
**Trick:** two people sitting on a floor, the easiest staging there is. The scale is one cradle model repeated into fog. Kayla's torch is the only key light, so faces are half dark. The ending shapes change only shots 3, 5 and 8.
**Hand-back:** first person standing, looking down the hall toward the Spine; the self-destruct begins.
**Risk:** the most face-heavy scene in the game. **Fallback:** first person sitting on the floor beside her in the torchlight, with shot 1 kept as the opening frame.
*If the owner had chosen otherwise:* with no Mark (4b), shot 4 becomes "Someone has to hold it"; if what Danny walked away from was the team (6b), shot 8 changes.

### 11. Right Behind You
**Where:** Chapter 8, about 50 minutes in. The player ran to the pods as the ship came apart and caught Kayla's hand as a deck tilted; the freed are in their pods. **75 s.**
**On screen:** Danny, Kayla; both faces speak.

1. Wide on the pod bay: a curved honeycomb of pods, faces at some frosted windows, red light pulsing, the deck tilted.
2. Kayla sits on the rim of her pod and swings her bad leg in (cut on the action).
3. Danny, grinning: "Go. I'm right behind you."
4. Kayla, looking at his mouth: "You're lying. You do the thing with your mouth."
5. Danny: "I don't have a thing." Kayla: "You have a thing, Danny."
6. By the ending: she knows and says "Stay on the line, okay?"; or believes him and smiles; or her eyes are already closing.
7. The lid comes down between them, frost racing over the glass.
8. From inside the pod: Danny's hand flat on the frosted glass, the black lines on his wrist, his face a blur behind.
9. He takes the hand away; its print in the frost stays a moment, then fills in.
10. He turns. Push into his eye-line down the shaking corridor to the core.

**Image:** shot 8, his hand flat on the glass seen from inside: the hunter's hand on the wired glass in Chapter 1, answered. **Sound:** the lid's seal hissing shut and taking her voice mid-word; then the ship groaning. No music. **Line:** "You have a thing, Danny."
**Trick:** she sits and swings in behind a cut; the lid is a machine; the goodbye is two faces and a hand, no hug. The hand on glass is hand-posed, through frost, with the face out of focus.
**Hand-back:** first person facing down the corridor; the run to the core is played.
**Risk:** three versions of Kayla's answer triple the face work. **Fallback:** keep shots 7 to 9 (no faces needed) and play the talk in first person at the open lid.
*If the owner had chosen otherwise:* with no Mark the black lines go; the hand on the glass still works.

### 12. Seeds
**Where:** after the hold: the Kessler voice, the true thing, white. **70 s, then June.**
**On screen:** the mothership, the pods, Kayla in her pod. No one speaks.

1. The white thins to dawn blue: very wide, the city from far above, the mothership small now against the size of the sky.
2. From its flank, pods drift out and fall in a long slow arc, each with a small light, like seeds blown off a dandelion. There are as many as the people Danny freed.
3. Closer on the arc: frosted windows turning in the light.
4. Inside Kayla's pod: her face lit gold through frost, the walkie in her hand, hissing. (In "Only her" she is asleep.)
5. Far wide: the mothership breaks along its spine without a sound, light running through it.
6. Seconds later the sound arrives, a long low roll like thunder a long way off.
7. The walkie in her hand: the hiss stops.
8. The pods settle toward the city as the sun comes up; the camera rises past them into empty sky.
9. The sky stays and turns to June. "June." The camera comes down to a chain-link fence and the school behind it, at Kayla's eye height.

**Image:** shot 2, pods leaving a dying ship like seeds at dawn, their number the ending you earned. **Sound:** the gap between the silent break and the thunder reaching us, then the walkie's hiss stopping. No music: Song 3 waits for the gate. **Line:** none; Danny has said everything.
**Trick:** no people move. Pods are small lit objects on paths; the break-up is far and small, so it never needs close detail; Kayla's face is still and half-lit through frost. Endings change only the number of pods and whether her eyes are open.
**Hand-back:** first person as Kayla at the fence on the same view; the epilogue is played.
**Risk:** the break-up looking like a cheap explosion. **Fallback:** no break-up on screen, only a flare of light where the ship was, then the sound.

## Part 2: six story moments that stay in first person

1. **The hand on the glass (Chapter 1).** Ray hammering, Gus at the bar, the player two metres behind. Aim the one bulb at the door so the wired window is the grey square the eye goes to. As it opens, the hunter's hand comes flat onto the glass behind Ray, fingers spread, its face above the light. Two seconds, the door shuts, the hand slides off with a squeak of skin on glass. Nobody turns the player's head; he sees it because he was already looking.
2. **The Mark, and waking (Chapter 3).** The whisper behind him makes the player turn, which is the trap, since the Still Ones move while he looks away. The lift is the view rising a metre and a half, the long face hanging down into it from above, close, as in the favourite picture; one long finger on his forearm, frost spreading with a cold crackle; Mari's call, once, far off; black. He wakes on the stage boards under one work light, and the first thing he is given is his arm with the black lines. Then he must look up: the Still Ones stand round the stage, faces away, closer than before. No cutscene can make that look as frightening as doing it yourself.
3. **The two Kaylas (Chapter 4).** Light the yellow rain jacket from the side so it is the only colour on the floor. Its "Danny, over here" has no breath before it. The player whispers "What time?"; it asks it back in her voice with the same timing. The real "Four" comes from behind, with a breath first and the room's real echo. Let him turn to her while the Mimic stays facing away.
4. **The parachute and the fall (Chapter 5).** Spinning cabin, red light, the crew chief down. Kayla's face very close, the buckle in her hands at the bottom of the view, "Go. I'm right behind you.", the shove. The view tumbles, the canopy cracks open and the city swings into place: black streets, the lit belly above, the helicopter falling three blocks away. Then steady the descent for five seconds over the wreck: tall among the Patrol lamps, the hunter lifts someone in a yellow jacket. He can look away; it is still happening.
5. **The nest (Chapter 8).** Marcus and Mari still in their cradles, faces calm and clean, lips moving with the whispers; nothing done to them that can be seen. The player lays the drumstick in Marcus's open hand himself, with one button. Nothing happens. That is the scene.
6. **The hold (Chapter 8).** One button held for the pod release, the other hand on the pockets. Pods launch on a strip of lights, a face at each window. No music: breath, pods, the hunter, the radio. The hunter crouches folded beside him and whispers the promise back; Danny answers. When the pockets are empty its hand closes over his mouth; the last words are already out. White, into Seeds.

## Part 3: the three I would fight for

1. **Last Lights (2).** The game's poster and its first promise of scale: a ship the size of a district putting out a town's last four lights, then one hand on wet turf. It is also the cheapest big scene in the game, because everything in it is far, dark, slow or a single close-up, and the hardest animation of all (the unfolding) is a sound over two faces.
2. **The Board (3).** Fifty seconds, no faces, no bodies, one skateboard, and it carries the guilt the whole game runs on: "You guys'll wait, right?" It proves a cutscene does not need people to hit hard, the best news a team without motion capture can get.
3. **Seeds (12).** The ending turns a number into a picture: the player sees how many he saved as lights falling at dawn, the walkie goes quiet, and the sky becomes June. It closes the loop Last Lights opened, and like it, needs no actor at all.
