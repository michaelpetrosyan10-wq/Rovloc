STAY BEHIND: THE TWELVE CUTSCENES, JUDGED BY THE PRODUCER WHO HAS TO GET THEM MADE

The test for every scene: will it look real on one gaming PC with 16 GB of memory, with no motion-capture
stage and no animator? People are MetaHumans (teenage faces are the known risk), bodies come from library
animation and phone acting, faces are driven by the recorded voice or a phone video, no more than ten
speaking faces, and the hunter's hard movement stays off camera.

OVERALL
- The three directors did not pick the same twelve moments. Eleven moments are in at least two takes:
  the halftime walk, the sky, the skateboard, the first count, the camp song, "they're breathing",
  "where's Mari", Ray at the door, Gus on the roof, the talk with Kayla in the ship, and Kayla's pod.
  The twelfth is the dawn after the hold. The spectacle take calls it "Seeds". The dread take plays it
  as twenty seconds in first person. It is also the story draft's twelfth scene. Those are the twelve
  judged here.
- Dread is the most buildable take. It hides the most: held poses between blackouts, nobody moving, the
  door doing the acting. Spectacle is best wherever there are no people or the people are far away:
  the skateboard, the floating cradles, the helicopter as light and rain, the pods at dawn. Heart is best
  where a face has to carry the scene and needs objects and glass to protect it: the camp song, the pod,
  and keeping every line spoken while standing still.
- Final set: dread wins 5 scenes, spectacle 4, heart 3. That comes to about 17 minutes (1,030 seconds), inside
  the 14 to 18 minute target.
- Speaking faces in this set: Danny, Kayla, Marcus, Gus, Kessler, Priya, Owen and Ray, with Mari humming
  side-on. That is nine of the ten allowed. Six of them are teenagers. Only Danny and Kayla get long,
  well-lit close-ups. Marcus, Priya, Owen and Mari are always half-lit, side-on or brief.
- Moments the three takes made cutscenes that this set plays in first person instead:
    Waking with the Mark ("Frost", dread): first person, as heart and spectacle both say. The player looks
      at his own arm, then looks up at the Still Ones, which are closer. Keep dread's hair clip on the boards.
    The lift ("The Lift", spectacle): first person in the cabin, looking around only, with Song 1. Out the
      window, the one lit basement window is Gus keeping the lights on (a graft that costs nothing).
      The missile cuts the song mid-line.
    The ship's belly ("The Belly", spectacle): the cloud clearing is played in the flight. The crash is a
      cut to black with sound.
    The wreck ("It's Me. Again.", heart): first person. Press talk and hear your own voice come out of
      Kayla's walkie on the wet seat. This is the best idea in the city chapter, and it costs nothing in
      first person.
    The nest and the drumstick ("Got Your Stick", heart): first person, as the map has it. The player's own
      hand puts the stick in Marcus's hand.
    The gate in June ("We Waited", heart): first person as Kayla. Up to seven people standing in daylight is
      the most exposed shot anyone proposed. Keep "We waited" and her camera flash as played moments.
      Song 3 starts at the gate as the map says.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE HALFTIME WALK ("We move in June", prologue, about 100 s)
Winner: HEART. Score 6/10.
Why: every line is spoken standing still, one face at a time. Danny's walk is seen far off or from behind.
  Marcus runs back toward the gym seen through wired glass, which hides a library run. Dread has the pair
  walk toward the camera and Marcus walk backwards spinning a drumstick. Spectacle has Kayla in close-up
  while walking. A walking close-up is where a library walk shows. It still only scores 6, because three
  teenage faces in the first two minutes is the game's biggest art risk, and the player judges everything
  that follows by it.
Graft: dread's half-dead tubes, so the faces sit in the warm light of the trophy cases instead of flat
  fluorescent light. Dread's gym door closing on its slow closer, so the bar of light on the floor narrows
  to a line. Spectacle's four stadium light banks seen far off through the back doors as the first wide
  shot, which is cheap and sets up scene 2.
Fix: no drumstick spin in close-up. In close shots the stick is held still; it spins only in the wide or
  through the wire mesh. Kayla's film-camera hands are one hand-keyed close-up each, at most three seconds.
  Marcus speaks only while standing in the doorway. If the teen-face test fails, use heart's fallback:
  first person, with the camera pointed at the player, keeping only the shot of Marcus through the glass.
If the owner chose otherwise: with Marcus as a survivor (5b), shots 7 to 9 go.

2. THE SKY ("Lights Out": the mothership and the small ship, prologue, about 85 s)
Winner: DREAD. Score 8/10.
Why: the hunter appears only as held poses lit by a landing light flashing on and off: a hand flat on the
  turf, then standing with its back to us, then its face turned to the gym. Every change happens in the
  dark between flashes, so the crawl and the unfolding are never animated. The humans are backs and
  silhouettes against the sky, so there is no lip-sync. The strobe makes three still poses look frightening
  instead of stiff. Spectacle's version is good too, but it runs 120 s and adds a speaking face in white
  light. Heart's version leaves the hunter standing up and turning for live gameplay to show.
Graft: spectacle's stadium light banks dying one at a time with a heavy clunk. It is the best picture of
  scale in any take, it is four lights switching off, and the fourth one holding and buzzing should be
  kept. Add no more than 10 s for it.
Fix: dread's hand-back says that in play the hunter "is already standing up". It must already be standing
  when play starts, and in play it only turns its long face (allowed movement). Keep the mothership inside
  the cloud as slow lights; never a clear, sharp silhouette. Before lighting, check each held pose against
  the favourite picture: the stoop, the hood, the big veined hand.

3. THE SKATEBOARD (end of the prologue and the title, about 50 s)
Winner: SPECTACLE. Score 9/10.
Why: no faces at all. Gus's hands drop the bar, the corridor tubes die one by one from the far end, and a
  skateboard rolls into the last light and stops. The wheels click over the tile joints, further and
  further apart. Then the broken whisper "...remain... in your... seats" from just behind the door, and
  the title. One physics object, a timed row of lights and two hands. Dread's version adds Danny's and
  Kayla's teenage faces pressed to the window, which costs risk and adds nothing.
Graft: dread's shot of Gus's key ring trembling while nothing else of him moves (one close-up of a hand).
Fix: make the roll slow down like a real board on a real floor (record a real board's wheels) so it does
  not look pushed by a script. If it still looks scripted, use spectacle's fallback: hear it roll in the
  dark and show it already stopped. Pick one sound to remember: the wheel clicks. The whisper is the line,
  so leave out dread's extra heavy step.
If the owner chose otherwise: with Marcus a survivor (5b), the scene goes.

4. THE HAND ON THE GLASS AND THE FIRST COUNT (Chapter 1, about 110 s)
Winner: DREAD. Score 8/10.
Why: the hunter is only a hand mesh pressed flat on the wired glass behind Gus's head, with one slow slide
  down. Gus is an adult face, the safest kind we have. Ray falls through the door across a cut. In the
  count afterwards, only Kessler's face is shown; everyone else is hands, shoes and a red beanie. That
  keeps the heaviest people scene in the game cheap on 16 GB and hides five teenage faces. Heart's and
  spectacle's counts both open on a wide shot of all eight people in light.
Graft: the Kessler impression from heart ("Mr. Reyes. Is something funny?" / Danny in her voice / Owen's
  real laugh / "...The chin's too low."). It sets up Danny's last words at the hold, so the player must
  have heard it. Stage it as two close faces only (Kessler, then Danny). Owen's laugh is heard, or he is
  seen from behind with a hand to his mouth.
Fix: shoot the hand close to the glass and slightly soft, lit from the corridor, so it does not look like a
  sticker. Check its scale next to Gus's forearm in the first slice. If it fails in close-up, play shots 1
  to 8 in first person (heart's and spectacle's way: the bulb aimed so the window is where the eye goes)
  and keep the count as the cutscene.

5. THE CAMP SONG AND THE PROMISE (Chapter 1, first night, about 95 s)
Winner: HEART. Score 6/10.
Why: nobody stands. Only Mari's strumming hand is seen, played by a real player filmed on a phone; her
  fretting hand stays in shadow. She is side-on and dark, humming more than singing, so there is no
  singing lip-sync to fake. The promise is one phone-filmed close-up with real pauses. Dread shows Mari's
  singing face and a hand-keyed loop of chords. Spectacle folds the song and the promise into a 120 s
  scene with all eight people. Fingers drifting out of time with the audio look fake fastest of
  anything here, so this still only scores 6.
Graft: dread's last shot. The camera drifts up to an air grille and holds on it while the promise hangs in
  the room: dust shivering in the slats, nothing else. It needs no person and pays off at the hold.
Fix: the guitar audio comes from the same phone take as the hand, never matched by hand afterwards. If the
  strum does not sync, put the guitar out of focus and let the song be heard over faces listening. Hold
  Danny's promise still; do not cut away during "But."
If the owner chose otherwise: with the basketball team (6b) as what Danny walked away from, Kayla's look
  means something else; nothing has to be reshot.

6. "THEY'RE BREATHING" (Chapter 2, the turn, about 75 s)
Winner: SPECTACLE. Score 8/10.
Why: the soldiers and cradles exist only on a grainy, jerky security feed, and the frosted cradles float
  out on a ribbon of light, so nobody lifts or carries anything. That fixes the one thing in the map's
  line ("loading frosted cradles") that a library could not animate. The faces in front are lit only by
  the screen. Dread and heart have soldiers sliding or loading the cradles.
Graft: dread's ending. A soldier on the feed turns its lamp straight into the lens, the picture whites out
  and feed four dies, leaving faces lit only by static. It costs nothing and is the scene's best sound cue.
  Heart's insert of Ray's big scraped hand flat on the monitor glass is also safe (a hand on an object,
  posed by hand).
Fix: Ray does not stand up. Spectacle has him "getting up from the dark"; instead his voice comes from the
  dark behind them, as in dread. The fogging lid has to read within two seconds even on the grainy feed,
  so test the feed's grain level first. If the feed looks too clean, lower its frame rate.

7. "WHERE'S MARI" (Chapter 3, the safe room, about 60 s)
Winner: DREAD. Score 7/10.
Why: Danny is a silhouette against the red exit sign, sits on a water drum outside the lamp light, and
  objects carry the grief: her cot, the guitar on its nail, the strings sticking out of his pack, his
  sleeve pulled over the black lines. It follows the map (the strings are still in your pack). It also
  has a fallback that keeps the scene working with no face at all: Danny stays a silhouette in the
  doorway. Heart has Danny set the strings on her blanket, which changes the map's beat into a small new
  choice.
Graft: heart's guitar on the nail ringing faintly by itself when the steel door slams. It is a free sound
  and the one the player will remember. Heart's floor-level wide shot for the sit-down, with the cut
  coming as he bends.
Fix: the failing grin is the hardest bit of face acting in the game. Film it on a phone, keep it to about
  two seconds, and half-light it. If it reads as acting, use the silhouette fallback. Owen's "Where's
  Mari?" is spoken from a dim cot, half his face lit.

8. THE KNOCK (Chapter 4, Ray at the door, about 90 s)
Winner: DREAD. Score 9/10.
Why: Ray is never on screen. Nobody moves. A still wide shot of the steel door does the acting: the same
  three knocks twice with exactly the same timing (which is what a Mimic does), then one tired, uneven
  knock low on the door and "...Please." Real knuckles on real steel. Heart is almost as safe. Spectacle
  puts Ray's face behind wired glass, which is one more face and one more risk.
Graft: from heart, a close-up of Kessler's thumb on the roll book over the name Dobbs (a hand, cheap), and
  her last line to the door: "I count my kids, Mr. Reyes. I can't count him." It stops her from playing as
  a villain.
Fix: none needed beyond a phone-filmed Kessler close-up in torchlight. Kessler "steps between" Gus and the
  door across a cut, not on camera.

9. "SOMEBODY KEEPS THE LIGHTS ON" (Chapter 5, the roof and the helicopter, about 105 s)
Winner: SPECTACLE. Score 5/10.
Why: it hides the most of the hardest scene. The helicopter is a searchlight, rotor wind on puddles and a
  dark shape above the glare, never a detailed hovering model. Boarding is silhouettes stepping into the
  white, one per cut, so there is no moving group. The hunter is one still pose at the edge of the beam,
  and its fast exit is hidden by the beam flaring into the lens, then "one heavy step on metal". Dread
  and heart stage the same goodbye but with more close-ups in rain and, in heart's case, Kayla pulling
  Danny by the hood (two bodies touching with force). It is still the riskiest scene in the set: rain
  and wind on MetaHuman hair, a hovering aircraft, several bodies and variants for who boards.
Graft: heart's sound. The rotors drop out for Gus's lines and his keys jingle under them, then the
  flare strikes and the rotors come back like a wave. Heart's picture of Gus walking away from camera
  toward the stair hut with a red flare held up, which is a library walk from behind. One small ear touch
  if Danny brought the hearing-aid batteries (from heart), instead of dread's second alternate ending shot.
Fix: Gus wears his cap and Kessler her hood, so there is no loose wet hair in close-up. Kessler reading
  the list becomes an insert of her hands and the soaked roll book, with her voice. Gus's two close-ups are
  the only faces in rain. Cut Danny "reaching" for the hand out of the light: end on his eye-line into the
  white, then hard cut to first person. If rain on faces fails, Gus speaks as a silhouette against the
  searchlight (spectacle's fallback). Whether Gus lives is told only in June, so the hunter is never shown
  reaching him.

10. "WHAT TIME?" (Chapter 8, waking Kayla in the nest, about 90 s)
Winner: DREAD. Score 7/10.
Why: Kayla lies still. Her eyes open, her breath fogs the inside of the lid, and she says four words. The
  lid opens by itself. Danny kneels the whole time, and the only touch is his palm on the glass. Marcus
  and Mari are out-of-focus held poses whose lips move with the murmur, so the taken show nothing
  graphic. The murmur stopping all at once is a free sound. Spectacle's "The Talk" has two kids sitting
  on the floor, which is also easy, but it is 90 s of face-heavy dialogue with three versions of three
  shots, and a hall of thousands of cradles to fill on 16 GB.
Graft: spectacle's variant line for the Mark. If she has seen it: "It's higher." If not, a silence, then
  "How long?" This replaces dread's silent look.
Fix: Kayla's face is very close, the second most important face in the game. Keep it behind the frosted
  lid until her eyes open, and keep the frost on the glass's edges for the whole scene (dread's fallback,
  half used from the start). One phone-filmed take for "What time?" and "...Danny." The map puts "If I get
  in one" after the others are woken. It works here, but if the owner wants the map's order, move that one
  line into play.
If the owner chose otherwise: under the Harvest (1a), the lips do not move and the stopping murmur is lost.
  With no Mark (4b), the line becomes "Someone has to hold it."

11. KAYLA'S POD ("You Have a Thing", Chapter 8, about 100 s)
Winner: HEART. Score 7/10.
Why: Kayla is already strapped in when the scene opens, so nobody climbs in. Spectacle has her swing her
  bad leg in, which is a body move. Two still faces with curved glass between them, so reflections,
  condensation and sparks soften every edge. Her hand flat on the inside and his on the outside is a
  touch through glass, not a touch. Her voice moves from muffled behind the sealed glass to clear on
  the radio on his chest. That is a sound beat that costs no animation. It ends on the release panel
  with the glyph HOLD, which leads straight into the first-person hold where the map puts it.
Graft: spectacle's shot from inside the pod, of Danny's hand flat on the frosted glass and the handprint
  staying a moment, then filling in. No face is needed, and it is the safety shot if the faces fail.
Fix: she must already be holding a walkie when the scene opens (it got to her in play, off camera),
  because nobody hands anything over on camera. Spend the best phone-filmed takes of the whole game here.
  The four ending versions change mostly the audio: record all four lines, but animate only two face
  versions (awake and asleep). Keep the ship's destruction to sparks at the edges of the frame. If the
  faces are not ready, play it in first person through the glass, with Kayla's face in the window.
If the owner chose otherwise: if the soldiers took Kayla (3c), nothing here changes.

12. SEEDS (after the hold, the dawn, about 70 s, then June)
Winner: SPECTACLE. Score 8/10.
Why: no person moves. Pods are small lit objects on set paths, falling in a slow arc like seeds, and how
  many there are is the ending you earned. The mothership breaks far away and small, then the sound
  arrives late like distant thunder. Kayla's face is still and half-lit through frost, and the walkie's
  hiss stops. It completes the scale that scene 2 opened. It is also the story draft's twelfth scene,
  and dread wants the same moment in first person.
Graft: dread's idea as the cheap fallback. Twenty seconds from Kayla's pod window in first person, with
  the ship coming apart in silence and the radio hissing.
Fix: no physics simulation of the break-up. Pre-bake a few large pieces with light running through the
  hull, seen only from far off (spectacle's own fallback is a flare of light where the ship was). Reuse the
  prologue's far mothership model, and load the city only as lights under cloud, to stay inside 16 GB.
  For the four endings only the pod count and whether her eyes are open change.
If the owner chose otherwise: under the Harvest (1a), nothing in this scene changes.

THE THREE SCENES MOST LIKELY TO LOOK FAKE, AND WHAT EACH SHOULD BECOME

1. Scene 9, the roof. Rain and wind on MetaHuman hair, a hovering helicopter, a group boarding, the hunter
   and several ending versions, all in one scene. It should become two cutscene pieces with first person
   between them. First, a far wide shot: the searchlight column in the rain and the hunter's still
   silhouette in the beam, flaring away. Then the player boards in first person into the white. Then a
   short cutscene of Gus's goodbye only: two close-ups under his cap, then his back walking away with
   the flare. The helicopter is never seen as more than light and a dark shape.
2. Scene 1, the halftime walk. Three teenage faces in the first two minutes, under the least forgiving
   light, plus a running Marcus and a spinning drumstick. It should become: every line spoken standing
   still in trophy-case light, Marcus through wired glass, no walking close-ups. If Danny and Kayla read
   as over twenty in the first-slice test, play it in first person with the camera aimed at the player.
3. Scene 5, the camp song. Fingers on strings out of time with the music. It should become: one
   phone-filmed strumming hand, the fretting hand in shadow, Mari side-on in the dark, and the guitar out
   of focus if the sync fails. The scene is really about Danny's promise, which is one still close-up.
(Next after these: scene 11, the pod. It is less likely to look fake because of the glass, but it matters
 most, so it is tested early.)

BUILD ORDER: RISKY ONES TESTED FIRST

0. First day, before any scene: the hand-back. A matched cut from Danny's eye-line to first person, with
   control back within two seconds and sound carrying over the cut. The camera blend is reported broken
   in UE 5.8, so do not rely on it.
1. Scene 5, the camp song. It sits inside the first slice's safe room and tests at once a phone-filmed
   Danny face, the guitar hand, three or four MetaHumans in lamp light on 16 GB, and the hand-back.
2. Scene 11, the pod. It tests Danny's and Kayla's faces held close for a minute, which is the teen-face
   question the first slice must answer, plus the glass and frost material. A grey-box pod is enough.
3. Scene 9, the roof, in grey. It tests rain on hair, the searchlight in volumetric rain, a hovering
   light and the hunter's still pose. Decide early whether boarding goes to first person.
4. Scene 1, the walk. Three teenage faces, Marcus included, and the corridor lighting.
5. Scene 2, the sky. The mothership in volumetric cloud and the hunter's held poses under the strobe; a
   memory test on 16 GB.
6. Scene 12, Seeds. The far break-up, the pods on their paths and the reused mothership; memory again.
7. Scene 10, "What Time?". Kayla's face waking close behind frost.
8. Scene 4, the hand and the count. Hand scale beside Gus, and eight people at low detail in the shelter.
9. Scene 7, "Where's Mari". The failing grin; the silhouette fallback is ready if it fails.
10. Scene 6, "They're breathing". The security-feed look.
11. Scene 8, the knock. Almost no risk: a door, sound and two faces in torchlight.
12. Scene 3, the skateboard. Almost free; build it whenever the corridor exists.
