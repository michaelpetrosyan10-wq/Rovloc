## Review findings the editor did not adopt, or left open

- Rejected (as-the-player, city): cutting Chapter 6 to 60-65 minutes. In section 8 the owner settled 'about an hour and a half'. The city is kept at about 85 minutes, and every other fix for it is adopted (the opening chase, Patrol scenes that frighten, a leashed stray, the gun at the midpoint, talking to the dead radio).
- Partly adopted (as-the-player, ending): 'the run with Kayla and the woken to the pod bay'. The woken walk to the pod bay on their own, because the feasibility review rules out escorting people and moving crowds. Only Kayla runs the last stretch with Danny, as a scripted set piece.
- Put to the owner, not settled (consistency): the retry checkpoints widen his 'save by hand in safe rooms plus a chapter autosave', so they are his decision 2, not a designer change. Reading the lantern rooms as his 'safe rooms' is a designer reading he can reject.
- Not done before approval (feasibility): the per-chapter asset census naming a checked source for every hero prop. It needs his PC and his Fab login. It is scheduled as the first job of the grey pass, and any puzzle whose props have no source gets simplified then.
- Not done before approval (feasibility and consistency): the separate canon sheet file with one owner per number. Its content is in changedFromDrafts; the lead should write it out before any data goes into the project.
- Estimate only (feasibility): the schedule in builder-days per effort unit can only be measured in the first slice. The 19 to 32 months is a range with a 25-35% usage-limit loss built in, to be rewritten after the slice.
- Designer-settled without asking (consistency): Ray, a parent outside the owner's 'students, a teacher, the janitor', is made the assistant basketball coach rather than asked as a yes/no. The owner may object.
- Answered by design without asking (owner checklist items marked 'to confirm'): the radar wish is met by the scanner, which costs a recharge and can be heard. The second-chance or syringe wish is met by the struggle and the restart points. Neither was put to him as a question.

## The owner's decisions (21 November: "use recomended")

- **Why did the aliens come?** -> The Voice Collectors. (It ties the stolen whispers, the symbol tones, the Mark and the hand-held self-destruct into one idea.)
- **When Danny dies, where does he start again?** -> Close by, progress kept. (Losing 20 minutes to one death is what makes horror too hard, and these restart points are not saves.)
- **Who takes Kayla, and who may use her voice?** -> Hunter takes, voice twice. (It makes the hunter personal and gives the game its saddest sound while the radio is always really her.)
- **Does the hunter mark Danny in Chapter 3?** -> Yes, the Mark. (The ending gets a reason the player understands and still stays Danny's choice.)
- **What happens to Marcus?** -> Taken at halftime. ('You guys'll wait, right?' is the guilt the whole game runs on.)
- **What did Danny walk away from before?** -> His father. (Staying behind answers the question his whole life asks, with no extra scenes.)
- **What is the evening event?** -> Basketball game. (The announcer's halftime words become the first whispers and the floors are already dressed for halftime.)
- **Who voices and acts the people?** -> Real recordings with friends. (Real voices make the sad scenes land, and the old voices keep work moving in the meantime.)
- **How strong is the language?** -> Real but rationed. (It sounds real and the strong words still hit.)
- **Will the game ever be sold, and may anything be spent?** -> Maybe sold later. (Every tool and asset is then picked with terms that allow a sale, so nothing has to be redone.)
- **Upgrade the PC's memory and add a backup drive?** -> 32 GB plus drive. (16 GB is half what the editor wants, so it is the one change that most shortens the schedule, and the drive protects the work.)
- **How does co-op come later?** -> Single-player, co-op later. (It protects the story he asked for first and keeps the door to co-op open.)
