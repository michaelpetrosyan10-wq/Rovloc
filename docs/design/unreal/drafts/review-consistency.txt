STAY BEHIND: consistency, owner's answers and originality review of the six drafts (21 Nov 2026)
Worst first. Each finding: severity, where, the problem, the fix.

1. [BLOCKER] Two different survivor casts
WHERE: 3-systems.md 7.1 (and 7.3, 7.4); 5-audio.md 7.2; against 1-story.md section 2.
PROBLEM: The story's six are Kessler (52, chemistry), Gus (58), Priya (17), Mari (15), Owen (14) and Ray (44). Systems builds a different six. It has Marcus as a living survivor who "goes out alone, and that is how he is lost", but in the story Marcus is taken at halftime and is the hunter's first take (story D4). Systems also gives the teacher heart pills, Gus reading glasses (the story gives him hearing-aid batteries), the inhaler to the youngest (in the story it is Mari's) and has no Ray. Audio still budgets "Marcus, if one of the six". Ray, a parent, is also outside the owner's own list ("students, a teacher, the janitor").
FIX: Rewrite systems 7.1 from the story's table: each person's need and payback, with the Ray role ("goes out alone") staying with Ray. Delete Marcus from systems 7.1 and from audio 7.2. Make the reading-glasses and heart-pill needs the story's items (hearing-aid cells, inhaler, glasses, painkillers, a light, proof of Caleb). Either make Ray a teacher or coach who came for his son, or put "one parent among the survivors" to the owner as a one-line yes or no.

2. [BLOCKER] Three different safe-room breaches
WHERE: 3-systems.md 7.3; 2-creatures.md 1.9 (Chapter 5 row); 1-story.md Ch5.2.
PROBLEM: Story: in Chapter 5 the hunter follows the Mark into the shelter and the group flees to the roof. Creatures: in Chapter 5 something drains the generator so the light fails first (to keep hunter rule 2). Systems: after the Chapter 3 return the hunter breaks in through the boiler-room wall, the ledger picks who is lost, and the steel door is then welded shut. A welded door makes the story's Chapter 4 scene (Ray knocking, Kessler refusing to open) impossible. It also clashes with the story's fixed losses (Mari in Chapter 3; Owen, Ray and Gus through LEFT moments).
FIX: One breach, in Chapter 5, as the story has it. The hunter follows the Mark, cuts the generator so the room goes dark (creatures' mechanism, rule 2 kept), and the group escapes up the service stair. Delete the systems 7.3 timing, the boiler-wall entry and the welding. Who is lost at that point comes only from the story's ledger table (Owen on the stairs and the rest), not from a new "least helped" rule.

3. [MAJOR] The hunter's door counts make most of the taken unsaveable, against his answer
WHERE: 4-puzzles-world.md 1.3 (puzzle 1.3), section 4 pages 1 and 2, decision D4 (gym door "618"), 8.4 (the Nest wall); against 1-story.md section 3 and its decision 8.
PROBLEM: The world draft has the hunter scratch a count of its takes on every room, 618 on the gym door. The story (its decision 8, recommended) says the hunter keeps whatever it takes with its own hands, in its nest, where they are lost within a day; only the Patrol's catches can be woken. Together the two drafts mean 618 or more people are beyond saving. That turns the owner's "most are TAKEN alive (not killed)" into the opposite. It also clashes with the story's own line that Marcus is the hunter's first take.
FIX: Make the room counts the Patrol's collection tally (sprayed, as the world draft already has the Patrol do in the city, 6.2). Pair each count with the glyphs for "taken" and "alive", which keeps them as number lessons. The hunter scratches counts only on its Nest wall, where they are few (Marcus, Mari, whoever the LEFT list adds, Kayla last). Change "the hunter has scratched" to "the Patrol has marked" in world 1.3, page 1, page 2 and D4.

5. [MAJOR] Three different rules for why phones fail, and devices that break each one
WHERE: 1-story.md section 3 "Phones (P4)" (a pulse that cooks lithium batteries); 3-systems.md 3.3 and decision 1 ("if it could call someone, it's dead"); 5-audio.md section 6 "Phones" (phones get no signal and buzz), 4 (voicemails on dropped phones as whisper sources) and 9 (Song 1 plays from a survivor's phone); 4-puzzles-world.md "Depends on other drafts".
PROBLEM: Systems' rule kills two-way radios, yet the walkie-talkies and the shortwave are the spine of the game. The story's rule also kills the coach's digital stopwatch (a lithium coin cell), car-alarm fobs (coin cells), modern camcorders and thermal cameras (lithium packs), and the story's own prologue has "car alarms choke off". Yet the city uses car alarms and a fob as lures (systems 12, world 6.1 and 6.3, creatures 2.6). Audio needs live phones for voicemails and for Song 1.
FIX: Use the story's lithium pulse as the one rule; it is the designers' call, so drop systems decision 1. Then make the devices fit it:
- a wind-up mechanical stopwatch;
- an AA-powered tape camcorder;
- an AA flash camera;
- a thermal camera with a NiMH pack charged by the crank (already in world 5.3);
- older cars whose lead-acid alarms still work, with keyed alarm switches instead of fobs.
Delete audio's phone section and the voicemail whisper source, and take Song 1 from the story (the score, or Owen humming).

8. [MAJOR] Three creatures are other works' content under a new coat
WHERE: 2-creatures.md 2.3 (Still Ones' look), 2.2 (Listeners' head and catch), 2.7 (Brute's body and charge); 5-audio.md section 1 item 5 and section 4 (Listeners).
PROBLEM:
- Still Ones: "faces hidden in their own long hands; each time Danny looks back the hands are a little more open", together with "move only when not looked at". That is Doctor Who's Weeping Angels ("Blink", 2007) in look as well as rule.
- Listeners: a head made of folded membranes that spread open toward a sound is A Quiet Place's creature, and "the fan closes over Danny's head" adds the Stranger Things Demogorgon's petal head.
- Audio gives the Listeners a sweeping tongue click and a double click. That is The Last of Us's Clickers, which the creature draft itself rules out.
- Brute: armour plating grown into its flesh, a straight-line charge and slow turning (the counter is to loop it) is the Dead Space Brute. The name was the owner's pick; the body and mechanic are not.
FIX:
- Still Ones: show the faces but turned to the wall or ceiling; the tell when one has moved is which way it faces now.
- Listeners: a smooth eyeless head, with hearing through skin that ripples toward a sound; the catch is a pin to the floor and a held breath on Danny's neck. Audio uses creatures' hum and hum-stop, with no clicks anywhere.
- Brute: drop the straight-line charge and slow turn. It is too big for school-size doorways, so loops go through narrow doors; keep "tears soft hiding open" and its tap-and-sniff window.
