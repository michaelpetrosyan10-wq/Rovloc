STAY BEHIND: THE VOICE PLAN, SCEPTICAL LISTENER'S REVIEW            21 November 2026
Reviewed: VOICES.md, all of it. Nothing was changed. Searches used: 19 of 25.
Marks: [checked: url] = read today through search results (huggingface.co itself could not be opened from here);
[from memory] = not re-checked today.

VERDICT
The plan is honest about what machines cannot do yet (part 1's table is right), its rules against the old causes of
"robotic" are sound, and it sends nothing to a paid route without his yes. It is NOT ready to run. Three things would
let robotic or mismatched voices through while every check says "pass": (1) one character made by two or three
routes or engines with nothing checking they sound like one person; (2) a ranking score that rewards the flat,
over-clear read he already rejected; (3) a blind listening test that is not blind. The first proof is also too big,
and it puts the deciding question last.

FINDINGS, WORST FIRST

1. BLOCKER. One character, two or three different-sounding people.
Where: part 2 "The recommended mix"; 4.2 (8 takes from 2 engines); 4.4 rungs 4 to 6; part 9 "change route".
Problem: Danny's ordinary lines come from text-to-speech, his crying and shouting lines later from a real person
(route A) or a converted performance (route B), and lines that fail on one engine are made on "another engine". A
listener hears the change of person at once; it is the most obvious machine tell there is. The only guard is a
speaker-match score of 0.75 against one calm anchor clip, and two voices from different engines can pass that and
still sound like different people to a person. Nothing in part 8 asks the owner whether two lines are the same
person.
Fix: Add rule 5 to part 5: "In the finished game every character's lines come from ONE route and ONE engine. A
character moves route all at once (e.g. Danny goes fully real), never line by line." Backup engine and rung 4 are
allowed only in the working build, and only if the take is then converted to the character's anchor voice and passes
the identity gate. Add to part 8 a "same person?" check: 6 pairs per character (one calm line beside one hard line,
one main-engine beside one backup), buttons "same person / different person"; pass 6 of 6. Mixed-route Danny is
allowed only if this passes.

2. BLOCKER. The ranking score picks the robotic take.
Where: 4.3, the score line ("30 % recogniser confidence ... 15 % detector 'human' score ... 10 % UTMOSv2"), the
identity gate, the pitch gates; 9 step 2.
Problem: (a) A speech recogniser is most confident on clear, fully pronounced, evenly paced words: the "reading
voice" the plan itself lists as a machine tell. Crying, whispering, swallowed "gonna" and a thrown-away "...Yeah."
all lower its confidence. Giving it 30 % of the score selects the flattest take. (b) The identity gate compares every
take with ONE calm anchor; emotional speech is known to move speaker-match scores away from the same person's calm
speech [checked: isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/goebiowska25_interspeech.pdf], so good frightened and crying
takes fail and calm-sounding ones pass. (c) Pitch-movement and pitch-wobble gates mean nothing on a whisper (no pitch)
and were set from Danny/Kayla/Marcus only; a calm, slow 58-year-old (Gus "quieter, not louder") may fail them for
being in character. The talk gate (4.5 semitones) sits inside the measured flat range (3.6 to 4.7), so some flat
reads pass. (d) The synthetic-speech detectors were built on older machine voices; 2026 papers report they miss
modern voice engines, Maya1 and Dia2 included [checked: arxiv.org/html/2601.20510; arxiv.org/pdf/2607.11706].
UTMOSv2 was built to score clean read speech [checked: huggingface.co/sarulab-speech/UTMOSv2, arxiv.org/abs/2409.09305].
(e) "Calibrate on round 1 vs round 2/3" is circular: the gates were made from exactly those files
(out2/A1_causes.txt), so they will separate them and prove nothing about a new engine's faults.
Fix: Use the recognisers only as a pass/fail gate (right words), never in the ranking. Make one anchor clip per
character PER KIND (calm, frightened, whisper, shout, crying) and compare each take with the anchor of its own kind.
Skip pitch gates on whisper lines; set every character's pitch thresholds from that character's own acted anchors;
raise the starting talk gate to 5.0. Remove the detector from the ranking entirely (keep it only as an experiment
logged beside the owner's answers). New ranking: 35 % pitch movement and wobble, 25 % loudness movement and pause
pattern against the direction, 25 % emotion fit, 15 % UTMOSv2. Calibrate every gate on the owner's answers from the
new blind test (finding 3), not on the old rounds: a gate stays only if the takes he called "machine" fail it more
often than the ones he called "real".

3. BLOCKER. The blind test is not blind and cannot fail clearly.
Where: part 8 item 1; 9 step 8.
Problem: The 4 real clips are "lines he and anyone who agrees record on a phone": he will know his own voice and
whoever he asked, so he is not guessing. Phone clips in a bedroom sound different from clean machine files (room,
noise, phone microphone), so he can tell them apart by the recording, not the voice. 4 real against 12 machine is too
few, and "about as often" has no number, so any result can be read as a pass.
Fix: 24 clips, 12 pairs: the SAME line once real, once machine. Real clips from consenting people other than the
listener (release signed; parent too if under 18). Every clip goes through the same finishing: same loudness, same
room tone, the game's room reverb, same file format. Clips cover the hard kinds (whisper, frightened, shout, crying,
joke), not just ordinary talk. Pass rule: he gets 16 or fewer of 24 right (17 or more right by guessing alone has
about a 3 % chance, so 17+ means he can hear the difference). Report the result per kind; a kind he gets right
every time goes to real recording. About 4 minutes.

4. MAJOR. The first proof is too big and the deciding question comes last.
Where: part 9.
Problem: 60 lines × 8 takes, 4 characters × 4 candidates, breaths, radio chain, three Danny states and faces is weeks
of work; the one result that decides the route (can a machine make whisper, fear, shout and crying pass his ear?) is
step 8. Owen (14), the hardest age for every route, is not in it at all; nor are the lines the story rests on.
Fix: Add a "proof zero" before part 9, about two days: 12 lines only. Danny: 1 joke, 1 thrown-away ("...Yeah."),
1 whisper, 1 shout, his crying last line, and the Kessler impression "Miss Brooks. Is something funny?".
Kayla: 1 radio, 1 frightened ("It's in. It's inside, Danny."), her laugh-while-crying. Owen: 1 whisper, 1 question,
1 scared. Best machine take of each from the best engine (the old Danny 1 / Kayla 1 included where they exist), plus a
real recording of the same 12 by consenting people: these are the 12 pairs of the blind test in finding 3.
Decide each kind's route from that, THEN run part 9 only for the kinds that machines passed.

5. MAJOR. The hardest lines are sent to the route that is weakest at them, and it breaks the plan's own rule.
Where: part 2 route B and the mix table ("Crying, screaming, strain, real laughs -> A or B"); part 5 rule 1; 4.4 rung 6.
Problem: Route B (converting a performance into a designed voice) is a resynthesis through a neural vocoder, which
part 5 rule 1 forbids for every human line; the plan's own route B section says it is weak on whispers, screams and
big age or sex jumps, i.e. exactly the lines sent to it. Separately, REAL-ONLY lines keep their failed machine take
"with a red debug tag" in the working build: he plays those builds and will judge the crying scenes by them.
Fix: In part 5 rule 1 write the exception plainly: "Route B is allowed only if the converted line passes the same
buzz and wobble gates as the performer's original (wobble loss 15 % or less, buzz change 1.5 dB or less) and the
same-person check of finding 1." Send whispers, screams and crying to route A only. Cast real performers first for the
characters with the most hard lines (Danny, Kayla), so finding 1 does not force a mix. In builds he plays, a
REAL-ONLY line shows "[temporary voice]" in its subtitle so he knows it is not the finished sound.

6. MAJOR. The main engine may not take emotion directions the way the plan uses it.
Where: part 2 route C (Qwen3-TTS row); 4.2 ("Qwen instruct 'frightened, whispering'"); 3 "three designed reference clips".
Problem: Qwen3-TTS comes as three models: VoiceDesign (voice from a description), Base (copies a reference clip) and
CustomVoice (its own preset speakers with instructions) [checked: raw.githubusercontent.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/HEAD/README.md
via search]. As far as I know, the designed-then-copied voice runs on Base, which takes no emotion instruction; its
emotion comes only from the reference clip [from memory: verify in the README before relying on it]. The plan makes
only three references per voice (calm, frightened, whispered), so shouted, crying, laughing, joking and winded lines
have no reference to copy from and will come out calm or "fast talking" (part 1's own warning).
Fix: Before the proof, the builder runs the README's design-then-clone example once with an instruction and writes
down whether the instruction changes the output. Make one designed reference per kind (calm, joking, frightened,
whisper, shout, crying, winded), each from the same description plus the emotion, each passing the identity check
against the calm one. The script sheet's State/Fear/Loudness columns pick the reference. Move the Qwen "instruct"
takes to the VoiceDesign model only, and identity-check them.

7. MAJOR. Kinds of voice the story needs that the plan never routes.
Where: part 1 table, part 3 voice sheets, part 9 slice; game map parts 2 and 8, audio draft 5 sections 4 and 8.
Problem: (a) Danny's Kessler impression (his voice doing another voice) is the last line of the game; an identity gate
will reject it whichever voice it sounds like. (b) Humming and whistling: Mari hums between lines, the teacher hums
her tag, the janitor whistles his, the hunter hums a taken person's tag wrongly; text-to-speech engines cannot hum or
whistle. (c) Kayla "laughs, crying". (d) Danny's scream cut off mid-breath, which the hunter whispers back. (e) Small
speaking parts named in the map: the halftime announcer (whose words are the first whispers), the IT technician, the
laundromat boy; none has a voice sheet.
Fix: Add rows to part 1 and to the mix table: impression lines, humming, whistling, laugh-while-crying, cut-off
screams = REAL-ONLY from the start (the person playing that character performs them; the impression and the scream
are in Danny's performer's session list). For the impression on the machine track, make it a separate
"Danny doing Kessler" anchor and identity-check it against Danny's anchor only loosely, with the owner's ear deciding.
Add voice sheets for the announcer, technician and laundromat boy, and for any soldier calls.

8. MAJOR. Tools whose licence or fit on his PC is not settled are used as main candidates.
Where: part 2 route C table and "Usable only with conditions"; 4.3 table; part 11.
Problem: (a) Maya1 is listed as Apache-2.0, but whether its 3B model started from Meta's Llama weights (whose licence
would then apply) is still not answered [checked: marktechpost.com/2025/11/11/maya1..., osai-index.eu/model/maya:
"Llama-style backbone", base not stated]; it is nonetheless a main audition candidate in part 9 step 3.
(b) emotion2vec+ is under "license: other", FunASR's own model licence, which has not been read [checked:
huggingface.co/emotion2vec/emotion2vec_plus_large card via search]. (c) Parakeet runs through NVIDIA NeMo, which is
hard to install on Windows; there is an ONNX route (onnx-asr package, word timestamps) [checked: pypi.org/project/onnx-asr].
(d) "Chatterbox archived August 2026" could not be confirmed today; a mirror says archived 5 January 2026 and
Resemble says the models stay open and free but are no longer sold [checked: gitverse.ru/muravjov/chatterbox;
resemble.ai/chatterbox]. Qwen3-TTS, Dia2 (Apache-2.0, English only), Seed-VC (GPL-3.0, changed from MIT), Step-Audio-EditX
(Apache-2.0) and UTMOSv2 (MIT) do exist under the licences the plan states [checked: search results for each].
Fix: Maya1 stays OUT of every audition until a runnable check is done: open its config.json and tokenizer files; if
it uses the Llama 3 tokenizer or names a Llama base, treat it as under Meta's Llama licence (record the text) or drop
it; replacement candidate: a second Qwen3 VoiceDesign description. Read FunASR's MODEL_LICENSE file into LICENCES4
before using emotion2vec+, or drop the emotion score (its 25 % then goes to pitch). Use onnx-asr for Parakeet on
Windows. Record Chatterbox's archive state from the GitHub page itself, with the date.

9. MAJOR. Step-Audio-EditX will probably not fit as described.
Where: part 2 route C (Step-Audio-EditX row), 9 step 1.
Problem: Its makers say 12 GB of video memory is "a critical value", 16 GB safer, tested on Linux only [checked:
github.com/stepfun-ai/Step-Audio-EditX]. The plan runs it in WSL2, which by default gets only half of the PC's memory,
about 8 GB of the 16 GB [checked: dev.classmethod.jp/articles/learn_wsl2_wslconfig; docs.nvidia.com/nim/wsl2/latest/troubleshooting.html];
a third-party guide asks for 32 GB of system memory [checked: adg.csdn.net/695248195b9f5f31781b5a07.html].
Fix: Try the 4-bit checkpoint the README lists (Step-Audio-EditX-AWQ-4bit) first; set .wslconfig memory to 12 GB
with 16 GB swap; log peak memory. Treat rung 5 of the ladder as absent until this test passes, and do not let any
plan figure depend on it.

10. MAJOR. Nothing checks that the hunter sounds eerie, and his earlier pick is dropped.
Where: part 7, part 8 item 3.
Problem: He asked for "an eerie creepy version" of the stolen voices. The only listening check is "who is it?", which
tests that the voice is recognisable, not that it is creepy. His earlier choice (the Monster 5 close dry whisper with
a breath before it, audio draft 5 section 4) is not the starting point or the yardstick; the plan's chain is new and
puts the inhale "where no one would breathe" instead.
Fix: Start the chain from the Monster 5 process. In the "who is it?" session add one question per clip, "creepy
enough?" (yes / more / too much), and one short side-by-side: Monster 5's process against the new chain on the same
two lines; he picks. Pass: 5 of 6 named right AND most answers "yes".

11. MAJOR. Names and short thrown-away lines slip through the checks.
Where: 4.3 "names right", length and pacing gates; part 3 pronunciation list.
Problem: A recogniser writes "Reyes" when it guesses the word, not when it was said "RAY-ess"; a spelling check is not a
pronunciation check. One- and two-word lines ("...Yeah.", "Four.", "...oh.") are where engines are least natural and
recognisers most often invent words, and the words-per-second, pitch-spread and pause-spread gates mean nothing on
them, yet these lines are the "thrown away casually" moments the story leans on.
Fix: Check names with a phoneme recogniser against the expected sounds (e.g. Meta's wav2vec2 espeak phoneme model,
licence to be read and recorded [from memory: Apache-2.0]); never give the recogniser the names as a hint. For lines
under four words, make them inside the line before them as context and cut the short line out at the silence (a trim,
which part 5 allows), skip the rhythm gates, and send every short line in a scene to the owner's chapter sample.

12. MINOR. People talking over each other will sound staged.
Where: part 6 "Overlaps"; route C Dia2 row.
Problem: Engines finish a written cut-off ("I was gonna—") with a falling, completed tune, so the interruption sounds
rehearsed. Dia2 makes both speakers in one mixed track [checked: raw.githubusercontent.com/nari-labs/dia/HEAD/README.md
via search: voices vary unless given a prefix clip], so it cannot drive two faces or be mixed per speaker.
Fix: Make the interrupted line WITH its continuation ("I was gonna say we should") and cut it mid-word, with a 5 to 10 ms
fade, exactly where the other speaker starts. Use Dia2 only to find natural overlap timing, never for shipped lines.

13. MINOR. Consent steps missing for some uses.
Where: part 2 route A release; part 6 breaths; part 8 blind test; route D Voice Changer.
Problem: The release does not name: breaths placed under a DIFFERENT character's machine voice; clips used in the
listening tests; uploading a person's recordings to an outside company (ElevenLabs' Voice Changer) if that route is
ever chosen. Breaths "from a consenting person of similar age" means teenagers for most of the cast, so a parent or
guardian must sign for those too. Withdrawal before release must also remove that person from the hunter's bank.
Fix: Add those four items to the release text, with "parent or guardian also signs for anyone under 18" next to each;
make the build refuse any breath or test clip without a release number in vo_manifest.json; on withdrawal, rebuild
the hunter's bank without that person.

14. MINOR. Paid-route facts are out of date; a store rule is missing.
Where: part 2 route D, part 10, part 11.
Problem: What part 11 calls unverified is now answered: content made while on a paid ElevenLabs plan keeps a commercial
licence after the plan ends; content made on the free plan never has one [checked:
elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/account/general/what-happens-to-my-content-after-my-subscription-ends]. Eleven v4
launched 28 September 2026; standard API price $80 per million characters [checked: techcrunch.com/2026/09/28/elevenlabs-new-v4-speech-model...].
The cost assumes 1,200 lines; audio draft 5 counts about 3,100, which multiplies every figure by about 2.6. Nothing
here is presented as agreed (good). Steam asks games to state AI-made voices (audio draft 5, 7.1) and the plan does
not say so.
Fix: Update part 10 with both counts (1,200 and 3,100 lines) and the $80 per million API price; move the
after-subscription answer out of part 11; keep "nothing is bought without his yes". Add one line: "If the game goes on
a store, its AI-voice notice lists which characters are machine-voiced."
