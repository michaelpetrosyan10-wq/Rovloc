# Stay Behind: the world, the puzzles and the detail (draft 4 of 6, the level and puzzle designer)

What this is: the places of the game, every puzzle chapter by chapter, the power system, the alien symbol language,
the city, the mothership, the pacing, the secrets and the dressing, written so a builder can block it out. The owner's
answers (requirements.md sections 7 and 8) are settled and not marked. **(P1)** to **(P9)** are the lead's working
proposals. **[Proposal]** marks a smaller design choice of mine. **[D1]** to **[D8]** point to "Decisions for the
owner" at the end. Names and story beats follow the story draft (1-story.md) wherever it has settled them for itself;
where this draft differs, it says so. Everything here is original; model games are named only for the pattern used.

## 1. The school as a place

### 1.1 Name, city, night
- **Rovloc High School** (the story draft's name), in the river city of **Calloway** [D8], built **1962**, renovated
  **1998**. The two eras disagree on the walls (old room names, new numbers), and that disagreement is a clue source
  the player learns to expect.
- **Friday 20 November 2026**, the first home basketball game of the season (story draft, its D6). Times [Proposal,
  to be matched with the story draft]: tip-off 7:00 pm; halftime 7:48, with a confetti-cannon and balloon routine by
  the cheer team; Danny and Kayla walk out 7:51; **the pulse 7:56** (every phone and lithium battery dies; the
  scoreboard freezes on HALF, HOME 31 GUEST 34); the small ship lands on the football field 8:01; the assistant
  principal presses LOCKDOWN 8:03; Danny, Kayla and Gus are behind the shelter door by 8:06; the building is silent by
  about 8:40.
- Phones are dead from the pulse (story and systems drafts agree, P4). So the traces people left are mostly analog:
  notes, sign-in sheets, Polaroids and film, phone cases with photos tucked inside, the wired PA, the office answering
  machine, the band room's cassette deck.

### 1.2 The building
- **One block, 112 by 81 m** (the earlier game's 400 by 288 stud footprint at 0.28 m a stud), a basement, four floors
  and a flat roof. The earlier game's layout data is reused for the blockout: its east and west stair towers become
  the **West and East stairs**, its Level 0 tunnel plan (seed 20261103: five regions, junction plates J-01 to J-18,
  crawlways, hide niches within 8 m of every point) becomes the steam tunnels.
- **Heights, set for a three-metre hunter (P7):** corridors 2.7 m to the ceiling tiles (it stoops; the tiles lift and
  knock as its head passes, a tell heard before it is seen); classrooms 3.0 m; doors 2.1 m (it ducks and turns
  sideways); the lobby 7.5 m; the gym 9 m clear (F1 to F2); the library 5 m; the theatre stage 16 m to the grid (F2 to
  the roof); the stair wells 20 m open (it can stand at full height and look down the well); tunnels 2.2 m (it goes on
  its hands); crawlways and ducts 0.8 m (it follows, folded, at a third of its speed; nothing is out of its reach).
- **Ways between floors:** the West and East stairs (basement to F4; the East stair continues to the roof penthouse);
  the lobby's grand stair (F1 to F2 only); the kitchen **freight lift** (basement to F1 only, 2 power units); the
  **dumbwaiter** (one-way down to the shelter cabinet, hatches in the F1 kitchen, the F2 staff room, the F3 staff
  lounge and the F4 home-economics kitchen); the dead passenger lift (its shaft is a hunter route); the theatre's fly
  tower ladders (F2 to F4); the 1962 **exterior fire escape** on the south face (F3 to F4 only; its lower flights were cut away in 1998).
- **The lockdown rule [D2]:** at 8:03 every stair door locked on its stair side and the grand stair's fire shutter
  dropped. The locks are fail-secure: with no power they stay locked. From inside a floor Danny can always push out
  into a stair; from a stair he cannot get onto a floor. **Each floor's release is a key switch in its electrical
  closet**, and works only while that floor's riser panel has power. Released doors latch open for good. The basement's own release is on the switchboard and is thrown at the end of
  Chapter 1; each floor's is turned the first time its closet has power (2.3, 3.1, 4.4, 5.2). So every floor is
  entered some other way, crossed, powered and released, and its stair then becomes the fast, safe way home.
- **The one key that crosses floors: the riser key** (a brass key on a red tag, ELEC 1 to 4). It opens the four
  electrical closets, stacked in the core on every floor, and turns their release switches. It is found in Chapter 2
  and is the only lock in the school with no loud way round it, so it is never missable.
- **Gus's key ring** (binder, no slot) opens the custodial closets in the core, the boiler side, the PE office and the
  mower shed. Some keys are unlabelled; Gus labels one after each chapter if Danny has helped him, and each opens a
  closet on an earlier floor (supplies, a story note). One never opens anything (secret 14).
- **Save points (P3):** the shelter's logbook and lamp; F1 the nurse's rest room; F2 the theatre lighting booth; F3
  the yearbook darkroom (red safelight); F4 the biology cold room; none on the roof.

### 1.3 Level by level (hub, spokes, locks; "loud" is the way round a lock and its noise from the systems table)

**Basement: the shelter and the tunnels (Chapter 1).** Hub: the **boiler cross**, outside the shelter door. Spokes:
the **shelter** (1962, two rooms of 14 by 8 m, yellow-and-black signs, water drums, the old shortwave; barred from
inside); the **boiler room** with the diesel generator; the **switch room** (main switchboard, open); **Gus's
workshop** (blueprints, parts bins); the **PE office and locker rooms** under the gym (walkie cabinet, stopwatch;
Gus's key); the **kitchen undercroft** (dry storeroom: padlock, loud: pry bar 80 dB; freight lift bottom); the
**steam tunnels** north-east to the **bus garage** (three buses: the visiting team's coach and two district buses;
gate on Gus's key), with the **flooded stretch** (sump pump) and the **fuel store** (chained drum, Gus's key); the
**bricked arch**, a short cut to the garage that only the sledgehammer opens (90 dB a blow, it brings the hunter);
and south, the field-house tunnel the hunter came in by (open, and the direction of dread).

**First floor: the game (Chapter 2, Listeners).** Hub: the **lobby** (terrazzo, trophy cases, the grand stair closed
at its top). West: the **main office** (glass door: Gus's key, loud: extinguisher on the glass 75 dB), its **safe**
(dial, loud: sledgehammer on the hinge, four blows), the **bell and PA rack**, the **nurse's office** (medicine
closet: key from the safe, loud: pry bar) and its rest room. North: the **commons** (concession stand, booster
bake sale, the halftime raffle table) and the **kitchen** (walk-in freezer, freight lift top, dumbwaiter, loading
dock). East: the **gym** (bleachers out, coats on them, scoreboard frozen, the announcer's desk), the athletic
director's office, the trainer's room. South (1998 wing): the **band room**, the **tech workshop** (the crafting
bench), the **groundskeeper's cage** (bolt cutters; Gus's key). Core: electrical closet 1, custodial closet 1. Way in:
the freight lift. Way up: the bleachers to F2's running track.

**Second floor: the arts (Chapter 3, Still Ones).** Hub: the **upper lobby** behind the fire shutter. East: the gym's
**running track** (the way in). North: the **theatre** (500 seats; stage, wings, fly rail; behind it the **scene
shop**, the **dimmer room** on the stage manager's key, the costume shop), the **lighting booth** at the back of the
house, and beside it the **AV room** (camcorder). West: the **art studios** (ceramics and kilns; the sculpture room of
sheeted easels and casts). South: the **music suite** (choir room, practice rooms, the music office). Core: electrical
closet 2, the staff room. Way up: the theatre's fly gallery to F3. Loud ways here cost nothing with the Still Ones,
only with the hunter, which makes the hunter's ears the whole question on this floor.

**Third floor: the people (Chapter 4, Mimics).** Hub: the **humanities hall**. North: the **library** (reading room
under a clerestory, stacks, the card catalogue, circulation desk). West: the **yearbook room** and darkroom, the
**language lab**. South: the **counselling suite**, the **lost-and-found** closet (Gus's key after Chapter 3, loud:
bolt cutters), and the windows onto the exterior fire escape. East: classrooms with lockdown locks (a closed door
locks itself), Danny's locker. Core: electrical closet 3, the **server room** and IT office (badge reader, loud: pry
bar on the strike), the staff lounge. Way in: the theatre's fly gallery door. Way up: the fire escape.

**Fourth floor: science (Chapter 5, Swarm).** Hub: the **science corridor**. Rooms: **chemistry** lab and prep room
(balance lock, dry ice, CO2 extinguisher); **biology** and its **greenhouse room** (grow lights, heat mats: the warmest
place on the floor) and cold room; **physics** (thermal camera, crank generator from the demo kit); the
**home-economics kitchen** (kettles, dumbwaiter); the **mechanical room** (air handlers, the heating valves, the
penthouse stair); the theatre's loading gallery door. Core: electrical closet 4, custodial closet 4 (facilities
binder). Way in: the fire escape into the chemistry lab window. Way up: the roof door, chained by the roofing
contractor (bolt cutters only, 60 dB: the story's "door chain cut").

**Roof.** Gravel (+6 dB a step), rooftop air units, the fly tower box standing 4 m higher, skylights over the two
stair wells and the central light well, a parapet 1.1 m high. The mothership fills the sky to the north.

**Note where this differs from the story draft:** it puts the library on F2 and the science labs on F3. This draft
puts the library on F3 (the Mimics' "figures in the stacks") and science on F4 (the Swarm's cold and heat tools sit
there in the creature and systems drafts). The lead reconciles.

### 1.4 How earlier floors change when Danny comes back
Each chapter starts with a short re-crossing of the floor below, 3 to 5 minutes, through a space the player knows:
- **Chapter 3, the gym again:** the rain on the gym roof has stopped (no masking but the heating knocks); the
  Listeners have gone back to the gym; two coats on the bleacher route have slid off, so the soft path is broken.
- **Chapter 4, the theatre again:** the Still Ones have moved into the wings and one stands on the fly gallery
  ladder; the board remembers the last cue, but a fuse has blown again.
- **Chapter 5, the counselling suite again:** a Mimic waits on the landing calling in a voice from Danny's own radio
  talk of Chapter 4 (creature draft: they learn what they hear).
- **The basement on every return:** Dweller scratches over Danny's chalk marks; the tunnel lights only as the power
  choices left them; the generator reserve shown on the gauge and in the room.
- **Any floor left powered** keeps costing units (section 3). Turning old floors off is good housekeeping and the
  player learns it.

## 2. The puzzles, chapter by chapter

**Rules for every puzzle.**
- **Tiers** (the puzzle setting, separate from creatures): **Light** puts the clue beside the lock and the notebook
  writes the fact down; **Standard** puts it in another room, two steps; **Hard** makes it oblique or adds a decoy.
  The tier can be lowered at a chapter start, never raised (P5); lowering re-rolls that chapter's values only.
- **Reshuffle:** one seed rolled at New Game and kept through every save. Clue carriers never move; their values
  change. Item spots come from 3 to 5 hand-placed spots. Story items never move. **No voice line ever says a value.**
- **The hint ladder:** at about 4, 8 and 12 minutes stuck, and only in a quiet moment, Kayla on the radio gives H1
  (where to look), H2 (what kind of thing), H3 (the action). An H3 that would need a value is **written into the
  binder map in her blue ink**, never spoken. In Chapter 1 Gus gives them in person. After Kayla is taken (Chapters
  6 to 8) the hints come from Danny's own notebook, in his hand, headed "what would K say" [Proposal].
- **The loud way:** every school lock has one, except the riser key's closets and story items [D3]. Its cost is noise
  and the floor's creature answers it by its own rule; on the Still Ones' and the Swarm's floors only the hunter hears.
- Chapter 6's puzzles are in section 5 and Chapter 8's in section 6.
- **Never** a puzzle that needs a resource the player can run out of; never a paused world; every step confirmed by a
  click, a light or a notebook tick.

### Prologue: "Halftime" (one teaching beat)
**P0 The plan on the wall (1 min).** By the band-room door, the F1 fire-evacuation plan shows "SHELTER (B)". Danny
lifts it off its hooks and it becomes his first map. Teaches: maps come off walls; the binder holds them.

### Chapter 1: "The Shelter" (basement, Dark Dwellers)
**1.1 Dead Start (7 min).** *Situation:* the generator never took over at the pulse; Gus pulled its start relay on
Tuesday to replace it. *Pieces:* the generator's nameplate (relay part number, 12 V); six similar relays in the
labelled bins of Gus's workshop; the start card on the wall (prime, choke, pull) and Gus's logbook, which adds the
one fact the card lacks ("bleed the filter screw first or she dies on you"). *Steps:* match the part number, fit the
relay, open the tagged supply valve, bleed, prime until the sight glass fills, choke, pull (85 dB, one to three
pulls). *Creature:* none hunting; Gus is beside him. *Hints (Gus):* "the plate tells you what she eats"; "my bins say
it the same way"; he points. *L:* one relay fits, the card is whole. *S:* as above. *H:* two relays share the number,
one is 24 V; Gus's log is in his shorthand. *Moves:* the part number, the bin, the prime count (shown by the glass).

**1.2 The Pantry (7 min).** *Situation:* food for eight is in the undercroft dry store, through the near tunnels,
where the hunter is heard in the pipes only. *Pieces:* a three-number padlock; the kitchen manager's delivery
clipboard. *Steps:* read the code; open. *L:* on the clipboard's front. *S:* on the back of the last invoice. *H:* the
back says "store = the three cases we got short", and the front lists ordered against delivered. *Creature:* the
pipe knocks are the hunter's true position (an honest cue). *Moves:* the numbers and which invoice. *Loud:* pry bar on
the hasp, 80 dB: the hunter comes to check within a minute.

**1.3 Count the Empty (4 min, on the path).** The hunter has scratched a count on three doors (records room, band
storage cage, bus drivers' room). Inside each, chairs pushed back, coats, a lunch bag: the people who were there.
Matching the glyph to the count in all three confirms **Numbers I** (section 4). The records room also holds the 1962
tunnel blueprint that 1.4 needs, so counting sits on the path. *L:* the notebook draws the count beside the glyph.
*S:* count yourself. *H:* one room holds Gus's own coat (he says so; it does not count). *Moves:* the counts.

**1.4 FLARE ROAD (large, 25 min).** *Situation:* the first outage is scripted (fuel); Gus's last can gives two ticks
to restart, and the fuel is far: the fuel-store drum or the buses (siphon, 40 s a tick). The tunnels are dark.
*Pieces:* (a) the tunnel sub-panel at J-01, six breakers named the 1962 way (COAL RUN, MILK DOCK, ORCHESTRA STORE...),
whose main holds only three at once; (b) the blueprint from 1.3, with the old names; (c) the junction plates J-01 to
J-18 and the 1998 tunnel map on the switch-room door, numbers only; (d) four road flares and a box of the cheer team's
glow sticks; (e) the flooded stretch on the short route, with its sump pump (2 units); (f) the drum (Gus's key) and
the buses. *Steps:* read old names onto J stretches; light the three sections that leave the fewest dark gaps; for
the flood, run the pump (the shelter heater goes off and someone says so), wade (+6 dB a step, for the hunter), or
take the long way (one more gap); bridge each gap with a flare (60 s) or glow sticks (90 s pools), four flares for a
route that needs five (creature set piece); fill the cans; come home; refuel at the tick line. On the way back Gus
radios that the switchboard can keep only two of the shelter, the corridor home and the infirmary fridge (the
creature draft's "two of three", a quiet choice). *Creature:* Dwellers own every dark stretch and cannot enter light;
the hunter is in the tunnels for the first time and light does not stop it. *L:* Gus has pencilled two names on the
J map; the main holds four. *S:* as above. *H:* Gus swapped two breakers in 2014 (his note), and one section is dead
and must be found by testing (each test is half a minute of dark). *Moves:* the wiring (three authored sets), which
bus is dry, the glow-stick box. *Fixed:* blueprint, plates, drum. *Loud:* on the way back, the bricked arch to the
garage, three sledgehammer blows (90 dB) halve the walk home and bring the hunter. *Hints:* "the panel's names are
older than the plates"; "the records room had the old plans"; H3 written: the blueprint traced onto the map.
Chapter end: the KITCHEN LIFT breaker (2 units) brings the freight lift down; it is the way up.

### Chapter 2: "Halftime Draw" (first floor, Listeners: blind, hear everything, go to where the sound was)
**2.1 Drill Log (8 min).** *Situation:* the office safe holds the **riser key** and the nurse's closet key. *Pieces:*
the emergency binder in the principal's office ("Safe: date of the last lockdown drill, MMDD"); the drill log on the
reception wall (fire, lockdown and shelter drills, dated); the wall calendar. *Steps:* read the rule, find the date,
dial it. *Creature:* a Listener in the attendance office; each number set is a 20 dB click and the handle 45 dB, so
each number is turned in one heating knock (every 40 to 70 s, 4 s, masking 20 dB). Carpet is silent; the spilled
forms in reception rustle. *L:* the binder gives the code. *S:* rule plus log. *H:* "the last drill before the
break", two lockdown drills logged, the calendar shows the break. *Moves:* the drill dates. *Loud:* four sledgehammer
blows on the hinge; every Listener on the floor rushes the office. *Hints:* "the office kept a binder for the worst
day"; "drills get logged where everyone walks past"; H3 written: the log's place.

**2.2 The Nurse's Closet (6 min).** *Situation:* the story's needs (Gus's hearing-aid cells, Mari's inhaler,
painkillers). Prescription bins are labelled by **student ID, not name** (privacy, as real schools do). Mari gave
Danny her student card; Gus gave him his battery case (the size is on it). *Steps:* find her bin by the ID; match the
cell size among four tab colours. *Creature:* the plastic bins rattle (+10 if dropped); a Listener waits in the
waiting room. The nurse's white-noise machine by the door (real; it is there for privacy) masks 20 dB within 3 m if
given a battery pair, and a spare pair is guaranteed in her desk. *L:* names on bins. *S:* IDs. *H:* two IDs differ
by one worn digit; the class year on the card decides. *Moves:* the ID, the shelf, the drawer.

**2.3 HALFTIME DRAW (large, 20 min).** *Situation:* three Listeners nest in the gym, which Danny must cross to the
bleachers (the way up), the band room (Marcus's drumstick) and the locker corridor (Priya's glasses). The maple floor
is +4 and carpeted in confetti, cups and balloons (+30 a pop). The plan: walk the pack out and shut it in the kitchen.
*Pieces:* (a) the bell and PA rack in the main office: nine zone buttons and a chime; it needs electrical closet 1
(riser key) and the PA circuit found on the closet's directory card (1 unit); (b) zone stickers on speaker grilles
(Z1 to Z9) in the rooms Danny passes; (c) the run sheet on the athletic director's clipboard in his office off the
gym ("halftime: band in gym; raffle in the commons; seniors to the lobby; after the game the kitchen opens for the
booster dinner"), which names the areas and shows the breezeway doors; (d) the kitchen's double doors (closed: 15 dB
less). *Steps:* power the PA; work out the zones; chime gym, then breezeway, then commons, then kitchen, each once
the pack has reached the last (listen: their hum stops when they arrive); close the kitchen doors in a heating knock
(the closer is 38 dB); cross the gym in the waves of rain on its roof. *Creature:* a chime draws them at 1.6 m/s; two
chimes within 6 s make them rush. The scoreboard horn at the announcer's desk is the panic button (100 dB): they
scatter everywhere. *L:* zone names printed by the buttons. *S:* numbers only. *H:* the 1998 rewire put the
breezeway and the lobby on one zone (a custodian's note on the rack), so the pack splits unless the lobby is chimed
first. *Moves:* zone numbers per room, one dead speaker. *Loud:* the horn or banged pans, which leave the pack
scattered, not shut away: harder, never blocked. *Hints:* "the office ran the whole night over the speakers"; "the AD
never put his clipboard down"; H3 written: arrows on the map.

**2.4 The Coats (5 min).** The bleachers: twelve aluminium rows (+8), the top 1.6 m below the running-track rail. The
coats, scarves and blankets the crowd left (-5) make a soft path, not a straight one; the torch shows it from the
floor before he climbs. A coat with a thermos in its pocket clanks. *L:* one unbroken path. *S:* one gap, crossed in
a heating knock. *H:* two gaps and a balloon under a blanket. *Moves:* the coat layout (three sets). *Loud:* the
bleacher motor makes a ramp of the rows: 70 dB for 20 s.

### Chapter 3: "Still Life" (second floor, Still Ones: eyes, light or a recording camera hold them; mirrors do not)
**3.1 The Camcorder (7 min).** *Situation:* electrical closet 2 is in the core corridor with two Still Ones, one in
an alcove; working the panel (20 s facing it) means looking away. *Pieces:* the AV room's camcorder and tripod (4 min
of battery; red light on, it watches its cone); the corridor's convex mirror, which does nothing (the player who tries
it learns the rule again). *Steps:* set the camcorder where its flip-out screen shows both (only the corridor's end
takes in the alcove); record; find the corridor and theatre circuits on the card; switch on; release the stair doors.
*L:* one Still One, 6 min of battery. *S:* two, 4 min. *H:* two, and the frame must also hold the art-room doorway a
third comes from when the lights change. *Moves:* start poses (three sets). *Hints:* "something here was always
filming"; "the AV room's camera, and it has to see both"; H3 written: the tripod spot.

**3.2 Brownout (5 min).** With F2's circuits on, the generator is at its last units: the voltage sags and the floor's
fluorescent tubes flicker, and every dark flicker is a step for them (the creature draft's twist). The closet's needle
sits in the yellow; Kayla says the shelter lights dipped too. Choose one: Gus turns off the shelter heater (2 units;
the survivors are cold and say so once; remembered); or shed F2's art and music circuits (3.4 is then done by torch);
or turn off the boiler-side corridor lights (Dwellers on the walk home). *H:* two circuits on the card share the name
STUDIO and only one is the art wing.

**3.3 CURTAIN (large, 22 min).** *Situation:* the way to F3 is the fly gallery above stage right. The stage holds the
December choir concert's set (painted winter trees on flats) and five Still Ones among the trees; one more stands in
the house aisle. The stage lights are dead. *Pieces:* (a) the dimmer room behind stage left, two of four cartridge
fuses blown, spares in a tin in the scene shop (ratings stamped on caps and rack), its key on the stage manager's
lanyard at her desk in the stage-right wings, so one dark crossing of the wings comes first (the camcorder can hold
the corridor behind him); (b) the lighting console in the booth at the back of the house; (c) her prompt book with the
concert's cue list ("full stage, the snow song"; "work lights, for the set change"); (d) the fly rail: a line holding
the night-sky drop that hides the gallery ladder. *Steps:* fuses; in the booth, fire the full-stage cue (it holds
every figure on stage) and park the work-lights cue on top (it lights the wings and the ladder); walk the house and
the stage edge keeping the lit figures in view; unlock the rope lock and haul the drop out (a 6 s hold on a heavy
line); climb (looking up is looking away: the work lights are what hold them); the gallery door to F3. **Mari** (story)
stays in the booth on the board as a second pair of eyes on the house; the Mark and her taking can fall here, when the
hunter's whisper turns Danny's head (the story draft's call). *Twist:* the concert's haze cue sits next to the
full-stage cue, and haze breaks every line of sight. *L:* a sticky note marks the two cues; one fuse blown; the haze
cue locked out. *S:* as above. *H:* the cue list is in stage directions only ("when the snow falls, everyone is seen;
when they move the trees, the work lights"), and the console holds two cue lists with offset numbers. *Moves:* cue
numbers, which fuses, the tin's spot. *Loud:* none clean; the fallback is the booth's follow-spot, one figure held at
a time, a slow and frightening crossing. *Hints:* "it's a show; shows have scripts"; "the stage manager runs the
lights from her book"; H3 written: the two cue numbers in blue.

**3.4 Concert Keys (7 min).** *Situation:* Mari's guitar strings are in the music office's accessories cabinet, a
word lock with letters A to G. The choir folder says "Cabinet: the keys of the three songs, in concert order."
*Pieces:* the concert programme on the chairs; the conductor's scores on three stands (each first page names its key).
*Creature:* Kayla holds the room's three Still Ones on CCTV while he reads; mid-puzzle her camera dies (the creature
draft's set piece) and he finishes with the torch on them, glancing at the scores. *L:* the folder gives the letters.
*S:* programme order plus scores. *H:* the programme gives titles only, and one stand holds last year's score of the
same song in another key (its date tells). *Moves:* order and keys. *Loud:* bolt cutters on the hasp (60 dB; only the
hunter cares).

### Chapter 4: "Who's There" (third floor, Mimics: face away, call in learned voices, never answer)
**4.1 Proof Sheets (6 min).** The yearbook adviser's cabinet holds the club camera with flash (the creature tool).
Her contact sheets on the wall include one candid frame of her dropping the cabinet key into the hollow base of a
journalism award (or a mug, or a plant pot: three versions). *L:* the frame is circled. *S:* one in 36. *H:* the frame
is cropped to the shelf's books and a window: match the book order to the real shelf. *Loud:* pry bar (80 dB): the
floor's Mimics come to look. Next door, the darkroom is the floor's save point.

**4.2 Reading Room (8 min).** Four figures stand facing away among the stacks; one is a real student hiding (a quiet
choice: Danny can leave her). Tells: the code word ("What time?" / "Four"; Mimics say it back, she is too scared to
answer); a thrown book (she flinches); and her breath: the broken clerestory has let the November cold in, and under
the torch a real breath fogs [Proposal, for the creature draft]. *L:* two figures, and she answers. *S:* four, and she
does not. *H:* four, and one Mimic flinches late, copying the others; only the breath is sure. *Moves:* which figure,
which stacks. *No loud way:* a wrong guess within 3 m is a maul; a checkpoint sits at the library door.

**4.3 The Language Lab (7 min).** A Mimic stands between the lab and the server-room corridor. The lab's console plays
any booth's recording through that booth's speaker, and Mimics go to a voice they have not learned yet [Proposal, for
the creature draft]. *Pieces:* the console (the lab circuit on: F3 riser, which cannot carry the lab and the server
room at once), the booth map, the recording lengths on the console screen (the corridor-to-far-booth walk is 25 s).
*Steps:* play a recording over 40 s from the far booth; the Mimic walks in; pull the lab door shut from the hall (a
closed classroom door locks itself in lockdown); it knocks for the rest of the game. *L:* one long recording, its
booth marked. *S:* read the lengths. *H:* the longest is in a booth by the door, where it would turn to face him, so
the second longest is the answer. *Moves:* lengths and booths.

**4.4 THE ANTENNA (large, 22 min).** *Situation:* the shelter's shortwave hears the rescue on its indoor wire, but can be heard back only through the roof
antenna, whose cable runs up the riser and through the server room's patch panel (the story's antenna part); the F3
and F4 camera feeds die there too. Kayla has limped up in person (story) and reads while Danny patches. *Pieces:* (a)
the server-room door, a badge reader on its own old lead-acid backup; the IT technician's badge on her lanyard in the counselling

## 3. The power story: one system the player comes to understand

The school's power is four layers, met in this order, each taught once and then tested:

1. **The generator** (boiler room): fuel is time, load is burn rate (systems draft: 8 ticks, a can adds 2, capacity
   6 units). In Chapter 1 it is a clock; afterwards it is the shelter's reserve, spent by each chapter away and by
   any power Danny uses upstairs (systems draft 6.3).
2. **The main switchboard** (switch room): one big breaker per load. Over 6 units it trips with a bang and 5 s of
   dark (taught once). At the last unit the voltage sags and fluorescent tubes flicker: harmless in the basement,
   deadly on the Still Ones' floor (3.2). Gus can work basement breakers when Danny radios; nobody can work the
   floors' closets but Danny.
3. **The electrical closets**, stacked in the core on every floor (the riser key): a main breaker, 8 to 12 circuits,
   a directory card on the inside of the door written in a different hand on each floor, a needle, and the **stair
   release**. Each closet also has its own limit (2 units), so a floor's systems compete with each other (4.3 and
   4.4).
4. **Things on their own batteries**: exit signs (lit through Chapter 2, dying in Chapter 3, dead from Chapter 4: the
   player notices), the server-room badge reader (an old lead-acid backup), the traffic cabinets in the city, the lead-acid fire panel that
   held the lockdown at 8:03 and has since gone flat.

**Loads (units).** From the systems draft: shelter lights 1, heater 2, radio and CCTV 1, boiler-side lights 1, tunnel
lights 1, undercroft 1, sump pump 2, freight lift 2. Added here [Proposal]: F1 PA and bells 1; F2 corridor 1 and
theatre dimmers 1; F3 server room 1 or language lab 1 (not both: the closet's limit); F4 greenhouse mats 1; roof
floodlights 2 (one timed stretch).

**What power means on each floor** (the reason the system stays interesting): in the basement it is **light** (the
Dwellers); on F1 it is **sound you control** (the PA), since light does nothing to the blind; on F2 it is **watching**
(lights hold the Still Ones, a sag frees them); on F3 it is **information** (cameras, the antenna, recordings); on F4
it is **heat**, which the Swarm follows, so turning power on can call them; on the roof it is **being seen** by the
helicopter, and by everything else. On the mothership the alien board twists it once more (section 6).

**The lesson curve:** Chapter 1 teaches the budget and its cost in comfort; Chapter 2 teaches a closet as a map of a
floor's systems; Chapter 3 tests the budget under a creature; Chapter 4 adds the closet's own limit; Chapter 5 turns
power into a lure; the self-destruct asks for the whole idea backwards.

## 4. The alien symbol language

**How it looks.** Every glyph is three claw strokes, so it can be scratched by the hunter, sprayed by the Patrol or lit
on a panel. **Numbers** are a long curved stroke with short cross-ticks: 1 to 4 ticks; 5 is the stroke hooked shut;
6 to 9 are the hook with 1 to 4 ticks; 0 is a lone short bar. They are **base ten, written left to right** [D5], so a
count can always be checked against something in the room. If the owner picks the story draft's Voice Collectors, each
glyph also has a tone; nothing else here changes.

**The notebook.** Every glyph Danny sees is drawn into it in his hand. Meanings come from a word list that grows only
when the context has been seen. A page is confirmed with a stamp and a sound only when every glyph on it is right
(the Chants of Sennaar and Obra Dinn pattern: guessing gets nothing). Light gives one glyph after two wrong tries;
Hard shows no overlay on any panel. The Patrol scanner reads glyph locks, with fewer guesses the more he knows
(systems draft). Pages 1 to 7 are guaranteed on the critical path; page 8 is optional.

| Page | Glyphs | Met | Confirmed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Numbers I | 1 to 5 | the hunter's counts on basement doors (Ch1) | counting the coats and chairs in three rooms (1.3) |
| 2 Numbers II | 0, 6 to 9 | counts on F1 to F3 doors: the band room, a classroom, the theatre's "1 0", the library | five rooms matched (by Ch4). Then the gym door, scratched since the first night, can be read: **6 1 8** [D4] |
| 3 People | person, taken, ship, alive | "taken" beside every count since Ch1; the small ship's hull (Ch2, story); cradle tags on the Patrol's carriers in the camera footage | Ch4, when the cameras come back and the footage can be replayed |
| 4 Doors | open, close, go, stop, danger | Patrol barricades and door marks in the city (Ch6) | the sweep marks (6.2) and the hatch (6.4) |
| 5 Energy | empty, full, energy | the gun's cells, the pylons (Ch6) | lifting the gun after a shot and after a recharge |
| 6 Flight | left, right, up, down, home | the cockpit (Ch7) | the danger lanes (7.1) |
| 7 Grammar | not, all, end, hold, and the order: the thing first, then what is done, "not" last | the Underdeck doors (8.1) | the doors and the Spine's lock B |
| 8 Lore | name, remember, child, sleep, first | the knocked-out soldier's diary, the Nest | optional (secrets 12 and 15) |

Thirty-six glyphs in all. Chapter 6 and 8 puzzles are in sections 5 and 6. **Puzzles that rely on it:** 6.2, 6.4, 7.1, 8.1 to 8.4 and every stage of the self-destruct.

## 6. The mothership (Chapter 8, about 120 minutes)

**Decks as a hub.** All decks open off **the Well**, a vertical shaft about 80 m tall with a slow lift disc and a glyph
sign at every deck mouth; the player returns to it between decks, as he returned to the stair landings. The creature
draft places one kind per deck:
- **Hangar** (arrival, the crash; Patrol crew).
- **Underdeck** (unlit service spaces; Dark Dwellers as cleaners). Save alcove 1.
- **Garden deck** (Still Ones rooted in the walls, moving only along their roots).
- **The Holds**, three long halls of the taken (Listeners as wardens, Mimics as voice-keepers, the Brute as loader).
  Save alcove 2 in their antechamber.
- **The Nest**, the hunter's own room off the Holds (Marcus and Mari, too far gone; Kayla, the newest: story).
- **The Spine**, the low-gravity axis (the Swarm as a warm drifting cloud), with the pod bay at its end. Save alcove 3
  at its root; checkpoints at every self-destruct stage.
- **The Core**.

**8.1 Grammar Doors (8 min, Underdeck to the Well).** Four doors labelled with short glyph sentences ("door open",
"door open not", "all go"). Trying them teaches the order rule and "not". *H:* one door's sign is half burnt.

**8.2 The Garden Roots (8 min).** The rooted Still Ones move only along their roots, which run in plain sight across
the deck's floor and walls. The deck's lamps dim on a cycle whose count runs down in alien numerals beside each lamp.
Danny reads which floor plates no root reaches and crosses from one rootless island to the next before the count hits
zero and the lamps go out. *L:* rootless plates are paler. *S:* read the roots. *H:* some roots run under floor
grilles and show only in the torch beam.

**8.3 THE HOLDS (large, 25 min).** Rows of soft cradles under frosted lids. Nothing graphic: a shape, a hand flat
against the inside, murmuring. Each cradle tag reads person, alive, a day count, and whose mark (the Patrol's; the
hunter's catches are not here). At each cradle's foot sits a tray of the person's things, which is how Danny finds the
people he knows (Ray's son Caleb by his team warm-up shirt, Owen's sister by her lanyard). *Pieces:* the hall's power board
(every cradle opened costs power somewhere else: a hall's lights or a door, systems draft); the wardens (a waking
person gasps at 40 dB); the Brute's loading round, heard from the next hall. *Steps:* read the tags; plan an order that
wakes the far rows first and darkens the hall being left; let the woken walk to the pod bay on their own (story: never
an escort). *L:* tags overlaid, one warden. *S:* as above. *H:* the board's limit is written in glyphs, two wardens.
Who is there is the ledger's (story draft).

**8.4 Kayla (6 min, the Nest).** Her cradle is the hunter's kind, sealed with a number dial. The Nest's wall is
covered with the hunter's own counts, one for each catch, the last one freshly scratched: the seal opens to that last
number, because she is its newest. Reading the wall also shows how many it kept (Marcus and Mari among them). The
hunter is away; its whisper places it truly (P1). *H:* the counts are written over each other and the newest is the
one with no dust in its strokes.

**The low-gravity stretch (the Spine, about 20 minutes inside stage 1).** No walking: push off, drift, grab, and the
gun as a thruster (systems draft). Down changes once, not more. The Brute is heavy and turns slowly; the Swarm cloud
drifts toward Danny's warmth.

**THE SELF-DESTRUCT** (about 40 minutes in stages, each a checkpoint; every stage reuses something taught):
1. **Three locks across the Spine** (story, with the Brute in pursuit). *Lock A, the board:* three alien conduits,
   two can stay; the twist is that the alien board is drawn, not pushed (joining a conduit cuts its power). Cut the
   gravity (the Spine stays weightless: easier for Danny, harder for the Brute) or the lights (the Underdeck's
   Dwellers spread up the Well). The pod bay's conduit must stay. Chapter 1's "two of three", backwards. *Lock B, the
   order:* three clamp handles, pulled in the order a glyph sentence on the panel gives (the thing, the act, "not"). *Lock C, the drift:* a lock across open space,
   closed by pushing a drifted pod into its clamp.
2. **Route the pods** (story): the pod-bay board shows each pod's seats in glyphs; the woken board on their own legs.
3. **Kayla's pod** (story cutscene).
4. **The run to the core** as the ship comes apart (lights failing, a deck tilting), the hunter using every habit it
   has learned.
5. **The final exam:** read the core's panel, compose **"ship all end"**, and read the countdown in alien numerals.
   The panel answers with one more glyph the player now knows: **hold**. Wrong glyphs cost countdown time, never a game
   over before zero [D6]. Light adds an overlay and time.
6. **The hold** (story): one button, Danny's hand on the core; letting go only makes the pods wait and Kayla ask
   "Danny?". This is the act that makes him stay.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **How much of the game is puzzles? [D1]** (a) As drafted: about 2 h 50 of the 9 h 15 is puzzle solving, with almost all
   of it done with a creature near. (b) Fewer and shorter puzzles, more stealth and story (about 1 h 45). (c) More and
   longer puzzles (about 4 h). **Recommend (a):** you asked for real, complex puzzles, and the Light setting and the
   hints keep it from being too hard.
2. **How does Danny climb the school? [D2]** (a) The 8:03 lockdown locked every stair door; he gets onto each floor
   another way (the freight lift, the gym bleachers, the theatre's fly tower, the outside fire escape), powers it and
   unlocks its stairs, which become his safe way home. (b) The stairs are open and each floor is locked by keys on its
   landings. (c) Only the freight lift goes up, one floor per chapter. **Recommend (a):** every floor is entered in a
   new way and he opens his own short cuts home, which makes the school one place he really learns.
3. **Does every lock have a loud way round it? [D3]** (a) Yes, except the electrical closets and story items. (b) Only
   some locks; the rest must be solved. (c) No loud ways. **Recommend (a):** a stuck player can always pay in noise and
   danger instead of being blocked.
4. **The hunter counts the people it takes [D4].** It scratches the count on each door; the gym door reads 618, which
   he can only read by the third floor. (a) Yes. (b) Counts on small rooms only, never the gym. (c) No counts.
   **Recommend (a):** it shows how many were taken without ever showing it, and it teaches the alien numbers.
5. **How do the alien numbers work? [D5]** (a) Base ten, like ours, made of tally strokes. (b) Base six, so even
   counting has to be worked out. (c) Words only, no number glyphs. **Recommend (a):** numbers come first and must
   always be checkable against a room, and the code timers on the ship must be readable under pressure.
6. **Can the final symbol puzzle fail you? [D6]** (a) Wrong symbols cost countdown time; it only fails at zero, with
   a checkpoint at its start. (b) It cannot fail at all. (c) One wrong command and the attempt ends. **Recommend (a):**
   the pressure is real but the ending is never lost to one slip.
7. **Secret endings on New Game Plus? [D7]** (a) Both the secret "count" ending and the joke drive-thru ending. (b)
   Only the secret one. (c) None. **Recommend (a):** both sit behind a finished game, so the sad ending is never
   touched, and replays get a reason.
8. **The city's name [D8].** (a) Calloway, a river city. (b) Named by you. (c) Never named. **Recommend (a) or your
   own:** a name makes the signs, the radio and the notes feel real.
