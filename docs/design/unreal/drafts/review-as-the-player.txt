STAY BEHIND: REVIEW AS THE PLAYER (the owner's own eyes), drafts 1 to 6, 21 Nov 2026

VERDICT
The six drafts make a strong, coherent horror game. The hunter design, the four school creatures, the power system,
the radio and the "Go. I'm right behind you" ending are real strengths. Seen through his eyes it falls short in five
ways. (1) His favourite monster gives the softest death in the game, and the gore he asked for is nearly absent for
five hours. (2) The drafts contradict each other in ways that break play: with the systems draft's speeds the hunter
outruns a sprinting Danny and catches him in every duct. (3) A death can cost 25 minutes. (4) The four school floors
are one loop played four times, and their big puzzles are building maintenance. (5) The ending leaves the hunter
waiting passively at the climax.
Weakest chapter: Chapter 6, the city (80 minutes of standard stealth guards, a dead radio, paperwork puzzles, and
the "instantly spotted" moment reduced to one line).
Scary 7/10: the hunter and the floor creatures are frightening, but the drafts promise so much fairness that the
hunter can be read like a timer by Chapter 3, and its catch fades to black.
Sad 8/10: the callbacks, the stolen voices, the names on the wall and Kayla alone at the gate land. The passive
button-hold and the thin time in the shelter weaken it.
Fun 6/10: each creature changes play (except the Mimics), but the repeated floor formula, the paper-code puzzles and
the cost of dying pull it down.
Crazy 6/10: the helicopter, the parachute, the flight and low gravity are big, but the school chapters lack showpiece
moments, and the two moments that should be the wildest (the hunter's catch and the final hold) are the tamest.

FINDINGS, WORST FIRST

1. [BLOCKER] The hunter's catch has no death scene, and graphic horror is nearly absent in the school
Where: 2-creatures 1.8 and its Decisions 1; 3-systems 9 ("Who does what"); 5-audio 4 (hunter catch); 6-production 3, item 9.
Problem: He answered "graphic creature horror (Dead Space-level gore for the aliens and Danny's own death scenes)".
The drafts make the hunter's catch a 5-second drag and a cut to black, "never gore". The hunter is active about 45% of
the game, so it is the creature he will be caught by most, and his favourite monster gives the weakest death in the
game. Nothing dies in the school, and a careful player is caught about once a chapter, so a good player may see
almost no gore in the first five hours.
Fix: Keep "it takes alive", but make the hunter's take the signature body-horror scene of the game, in first person:
its long fingers go into Danny's mouth and throat to pull his voice out (jaw forced open, the scream cut off
mid-breath, the pinpricks narrowing), then the drag into the dark. On the next attempt it whispers that scream back to
him, once. About 6 seconds, skippable after the first time, covered by the existing "cut away" option. Also add one
authored gore tableau per chapter that is never a student: a Listener the hunter swatted, split open against the
lockers; a Dweller burst by a flare; a Patrol soldier mauled by strays in the city; a carcass the Brute has torn
apart on the ship.

2. [BLOCKER] The drafts contradict each other in ways that break play and would confuse him
Where: 3-systems 2.1 against 2-creatures "Units" and 1.2 (speeds); 3-systems 3.4, 2-creatures 1.3 and 2.2, and 5-audio 3
(three noise tables, each claiming to be the only one); 3-systems 7.1 and 5-audio 7.2 against 1-story 2 (Marcus);
3-systems 7.3 against 1-story Ch5 and 2-creatures 1.9 (the breach); 2-creatures 2.2 and 3-systems 3.2, 5 and 14 against
1-story D6 (a "dance"); 2-creatures 2.2 against 5-audio 1 and 4 (Listener clicks); 1-story 3, 3-systems 3.3 and 5-audio 6
(three phone rules), and 5-audio 9 (Song 1 played from a phone); 1-story Ch3 and Ch4 against 4-puzzles 1.3 (floors);
3-systems 7.1 against the 1-story table (survivors' needs); 1-story 9 against 5-audio 9 (Song 3); 6-production 4 ("no
hugs, no handing things over") against 1-story cutscene 9 (Kayla clips the chute onto Danny).
Problem: With the systems draft's speeds the hunter outruns a sprinting Danny (chase 4.7 m/s against sprint 4.5;
walk 2.4 against 1.5) and catches him in every duct (crawl 1.1 against 0.5), so every chase ends in a death. That
breaks the creature draft's promise that "in a duct he can always stay ahead". The hunter's hearing threshold is 25,
28 or 30 dB depending on the draft. Marcus is both taken at halftime and one of the six survivors. The breach falls
after Chapter 3 in one draft and in Chapter 5 in two others. The event is a basketball game, yet the floors are
littered with "dance" leftovers. The Listeners "do not click" in one draft and click in another. Song 1 plays from a
phone the prologue destroyed.
Fix: Before the map goes to him, publish one canon sheet:
 - One movement and noise table: the audio draft's L values and thresholds, and the creature draft's speeds, with the
   hunter's chase speed below Danny's sprint and its duct crawl below his crawl.
 - Marcus is taken at halftime.
 - The breach is in Chapter 5, through the Mark; delete systems 7.3's after-Chapter-3 breach through the wall.
 - "Dance" leftovers become the halftime confetti-and-balloon routine (as 4-puzzles 1.1 already has it).
 - Listeners hum and never click.
 - One phone rule (the story's lithium pulse). The camcorder, flash camera and thermal camera become non-lithium
   devices, as 4-puzzles suggests. Song 1 is sung by Owen or the score, never played from a phone.
 - The library is on F3 and science on F4.
 - The survivors' needs come from the story table.
 - The production draft's "no handing things over" rule gets a written exception for the chute scene.

3. [MAJOR] Dying costs too much for "i also dont want the game to be too hard"
Where: 3-systems 9 ("What dying costs"), 10 (save points 15 to 25 minutes apart) and 11 (a death every 20 to 30
minutes); 3-systems 3.1 (6 minutes of torch per battery pair); against 2-creatures Decisions 4 and the "checkpoint at
the library door" in 4-puzzles 4.2.
Problem: The recommended setting aims for a death every 20 to 30 minutes, and each death sends him back to the last
lantern, up to 25 minutes earlier. One wrong Mimic guess in the Reading Room, or a last Swarm sting, can cost 20
minutes of solved puzzle. On top of that, the torch lasts 6 minutes per pair while the Still Ones and the Dwellers
both need light, and a catch is final unless a tool sits in the pocket. Together this is the "too hard" he said he
does not want.
Fix: On Story and Survivor, add invisible retry checkpoints at the entrance of every encounter space, at every stage
of a large puzzle and after every solved puzzle step, and keep the puzzle's state on retry. Lantern saves stay, for
quitting and for Stay Behind. Raise the torch to 12 to 15 minutes per pair on Survivor. On Survivor, make the first
hunter catch in each chapter a struggle he can win even with an empty pocket.

4. [MAJOR] The hunter is built to be read like a radar for nine hours
Where: 1-story 6 (rules); 2-creatures 1.4 (rules 1, 3 and 6) and 1.5 ("Hiding pays"); 3-systems 2.4 ("Rule the player
learns once"); 5-audio 4 (whisper chain, rule 5).
Problem: Every rule that keeps it fair is also handed to the player as a promise: always heard before it arrives,
the whisper always comes from where it is, it never looks into a hiding place it did not see him enter, it searches
20 to 40 seconds after a noise. By Chapter 3 the player hears Mari's stolen voice and thinks "good, now I know where it
is", then hides on the clock. That is about 4 hours of active hunter (45% of 9 hours, a beat every 30 seconds while
walking), so the familiarity Alien: Isolation was criticised for in its middle hours arrives early.
Fix: Keep the guarantees as hidden tuning only: never state them in dialogue, notes, captions or "the rule the
player learns" lines. Let it whisper only within about 10 m of walked path, so a whisper always means "it is right
here", never "it is over there". Once per chapter, let a learned habit break the pattern in plain view (for example,
it waits in silence at the locker row he has hidden in twice). In Chapters 2 to 4, cut its active share to about 30%
and spend what is saved on two or three authored stalking sequences per chapter.

5. [MAJOR] The heavy, far-heard footsteps he asked for are missing from most of the game, and from the first slice
Where: 2-creatures 1.2 (heavy steps only when upright in tall spaces; a soft patter when crawling); 4-puzzles 1.2
(tunnels 2.2 m, "it goes on its hands"; corridors 2.7 m, stooped); 5-audio 4 (the hunter's far tell); 6-production 8,
item 3 (the slice is a tunnel with "heavy far-heard steps").
Problem: His wish was "His footsteps are heavy and heard from far away, so you know he is around". In the drafts it
holds only in tall rooms. Chapter 1 and the first slice are in 2.2 m tunnels, where by the drafts' own heights the
hunter crawls with a soft patter. So the first thing he judges the hunter on cannot have the steps he asked for.
Fix: Make the steam tunnels 2.8 to 3.0 m high, so it walks stooped there. Give the stooped walk the full heavy
footfalls (about 65 dB, heard through two walls), with the ceiling-tile rattle on top. Keep the crawl and the patter
for ducts and crawlways under 1 m only.

6. [MAJOR] The four school floors repeat one loop, and the big puzzles are building maintenance
Where: 3-systems 1 ("the same five beats" in every school chapter); 4-puzzles 1.2 (the lockdown rule: every floor is
entered another way, its closet powered and its stair released); the large puzzles 1.4, 2.3, 3.3, 4.4 and 5.2
(breakers, PA zones, stage fuses and lighting cues, a 48-port network patch panel, heating valves).
Problem: Chapters 2 to 5 are four 55-minute repeats of the same steps: the briefing, the climb, learning the rule,
finding the electrical closet, powering it, releasing the stair door, going home. Every centrepiece is a
facilities job; the Mimic floor's big puzzle is patching network cables. The creature changes but the job does not,
across three to four hours in the middle of the game. That is the "boring" risk.
Fix: Only two floors use the closet-and-release pattern; every other floor gets a different goal.
 - Chapter 3 ends without a calm return: the Mark falls, Danny wakes hurt on the floor and must get home with the
   floor still live.
 - Chapter 4's centrepiece becomes a people puzzle, "Roll Call". The counselling suite's sign-in sheet and the
   darkroom's contact prints show who is really still hiding on F3, and the real IT technician, alive among the
   Mimics, has the badge. The patch panel shrinks to a two-link step.
 - Chapter 5 is reversed: the breach brings the group up to him, and the floor is crossed escorting no one, against
   the clock of the helicopter.

7. [MAJOR] The small puzzles are homework, and his five games are not visible as their sources
Where: 4-puzzles 1.1, 1.2, 2.1, 2.2, 3.4, 4.1, 5.1 and 6.1, and its "Rules for every puzzle"; the pattern sources named
(Chants of Sennaar, Obra Dinn) in 4-puzzles 4.
Problem: Eight small puzzles are the same thing: find a number or a word on paper and enter it (a relay part number,
an invoice code, an MMDD drill date, a student ID, the musical keys of three scores, a key hidden in a contact sheet,
a balance mass, a traffic manual's table). Puzzle 3.4 needs the player to read the key off a page of sheet music,
which most players cannot do. He asked for puzzles that are "well thought out and complex", taken from the Silent
Hill 2 remake, the Dead Space remake, Alien: Isolation, Outlast 2 and Amnesia: The Bunker. The drafts cite other
games and rarely say which of his five a mechanic comes from.
Fix: At most one paper-code lock per chapter. Turn the rest into puzzles played against the floor's creature. 2.1,
dialled only during the heating knocks, already does this and stays. 3.4 becomes matching the colours of the three
choir folders to the set on stage while the Still Ones close in under the light. Give each floor one Silent Hill
2-style centrepiece object with a short story riddle, on the Hard tier. Add a "seen in:" line naming one of his five
games to every puzzle and small mechanic, so he can see his references were used.

8. [MAJOR] Chapter 6, the city, is the weakest chapter
Where: 1-story Ch6; 2-creatures 2.6; 4-puzzles 5 (6.1 and 6.2) and 7 (80 minutes); 3-systems 12.
Problem: At 80 minutes it is the longest chapter after the mothership, and the flattest.
 - The Patrol is the one creature with no horror twist: guards with lamp cones and a scanner.
 - The radio is dead, so Danny is alone and silent for 100 minutes (the city and the flight).
 - The gun arrives only at the end, in 6.3, so most of the city is the same hiding as before.
 - Its puzzles are a traffic-signal manual and counting spray marks.
 - His "instantly get spotted" is one line: "Run, drop, hide."
Fix: Open with a 3-minute scripted chase from the landing: a Patrol craft's searchlight, rooftops, a fire-escape
drop, ending in the first hide. Give the Patrol one horror rule: they take people in front of him (the collar of
light, the person lifted into the craft, still calling out), and for one street they lead a stray on a leash (a
Listener used as a hound). Get the gun at the midpoint, so the second half is "hunted, with six shots". Make talking
to the dead radio a mechanic (press to talk, 30 dB), so his voice and jokes carry the chapter. Cut it to 60 to 65
minutes.

9. [MAJOR] The Mimics do not change how he moves
Where: 2-creatures 2.4 and 4.4 (the simulated players); 4-puzzles 4.2; 5-audio 4 (Mimics: "The test is the radio").
Problem: He said each floor's creature makes you "hide and move in different ways". The Mimics stand still facing
away and strike only within 3 m. The answer is to ask "What time?", and the simulation guarantees that a player who
asks is never caught. After the first figure the floor is a checklist of questions, and nothing changes how he
moves. The audio draft's "the radio proves who is alive" contradicts the code word.
Fix: Make speaking the cost. The code word must be said aloud (50 dB spoken, 30 dB whispered within 2 m), and Mimics
walk to any voice they hear, his and the radio's included. The floor's rule becomes "go silent": radio off, no
questions unless he must, and thrown objects for the silent flinch test. They move only while out of his torch and
always end up facing away, in his route, so he routes around figures rather than through them. Delete the radio
test from the audio draft.

10. [MAJOR] The ending is passive at the climax and drops the run to the pods he asked for
Where: 1-story Ch8, beats 7 to 10; 4-puzzles 6 (self-destruct stages 3 to 6); his answer in requirements 8 ("the run
to the pods as the ship comes apart").
Problem: The climax for his favourite monster is the hunter crouching beside Danny and waiting. The player's last act
is holding a button with no cost ("if you let go, nothing is punished"), which can feel cheap and borrowed. Kayla's
pod leaves before the ship comes apart, so his run to the pods becomes a lone run to the core.
Fix: Reorder the last minutes.
 - The final exam at the core comes first ("ship all end"), and the ship starts to die.
 - Then the run with Kayla and the woken to the pod bay through collapsing decks: his set piece.
 - "Go. I'm right behind you." Danny turns back.
 - The hold becomes a fight. One input holds the lock. The other spends what is left in his pockets (the last flare,
   the last cell, the extinguisher) to keep the hunter's hand off him, and each pod launch shows the face of the
   person inside.
 - When his pockets are empty, the hunter's hand closes over his mouth for his voice. He gets the last line to Kayla
   out first, in his own voice: the one thing it never takes. Then white.

11. [MAJOR] Too little time in the shelter for "Red Dead depth"
Where: 4-puzzles 7 (30 minutes in the safe room out of 555); 1-story "The shelter on each return" (chores reward
"only a line or a look"); 3-systems 7.2 (two or three night talks per survivor).
Problem: He chose "Red Dead depth": a group you live with and lose. Thirty minutes of shelter across nine hours, over
four returns, with chores that give one line, is too little time to love six people before they are lost. Mari, the
fixed loss, gets one song scene and one floor walk.
Fix: Give the shelter 60 to 75 minutes across the game. On each return, one playable activity with a survivor:
 - Ray's card game on the water drum;
 - Mari teaching Danny the camp song's three chords, which Owen later plays badly;
 - Gus's tunnel walk with his Walter Hale stories;
 - Priya's symbol board;
 - Owen's lamp walk.
Also give each survivor one personal errand on a floor that ends in a scene of its own (Ray's jacket for Caleb
already exists; add one for each of the others).

12. [MAJOR] The best-loved tool of one of his five games is missing (Outlast 2's camcorder)
Where: 2-creatures 2.3 and 4-puzzles 3.1 (the camcorder only on a tripod); 3-systems 5 (item list).
Problem: He named Outlast 2, but its signature carried tool appears only as a tripod prop for one floor. A held
camcorder is the kind of mechanic that makes people say "crazy".
Fix: From Chapter 3, Danny carries the AV room's tape camcorder (non-lithium, so it survived the pulse). It has a
night-vision viewfinder that drains AA pairs, so it competes with the torch. Its red recording light holds a Still
One while he films it. Its microphone brings far sounds closer. The short clips he records for Kayla become the tapes
she plays in the epilogue, and they count toward STAYED ON THE LINE.

13. [MAJOR] The prologue's "sneakily" is a guided run, and Chapter 1 starts slowly
Where: 1-story Prologue step 9 ("Play, guided"); 2-creatures 1.9, prologue ("Scripted only ... No failure"); 1-story
Ch1 with 4-puzzles 1.1 to 1.4; 2-creatures 1.9, Ch1 (the first 20 minutes heard only).
Problem: His brief says "so you sneakily go hide back into the school". The drafts make it a guided run past a hunter
seen once through glass. Chapter 1 then opens with four paper puzzles in a row (part numbers, an invoice code,
counting chairs, 1962 breaker names), and the first real threat comes about 45 minutes after the prologue. That is
a boredom risk in the first hour.
Fix: Make the way back into the school a 3 to 4 minute playable sneak with Kayla, along the parked cars and the
bleachers, while the hunter unfolds on the field and turns its head. It is his first lesson in crouching and
sight lines, with a near miss and no failure on the first try. In Chapter 1, put a hunter near miss in the first 15
minutes: Ray hammers at the door, and the hunter's hand is on the wired glass behind him as Gus opens it. Fold 1.3
into Flare Road's path, and keep the 1962 renaming layer for Hard only.

14. [MINOR] The hidden ledger can punish careful play
Where: 1-story 5 (STAYED ON THE LINE = "seconds of radio kept on in risky places"; moments 2 and 5); 3-systems 8 (the
volume knob).
Problem: On the Listener floor the careful move is to turn the radio low or off. If seconds of radio in risky places
feed the best ending, a cautious player gets "The recording" without knowing why, and would feel cheated when he
learns it.
Fix: Count "not switched off" at any volume (low and the earpiece included), calls answered in calm moments and the
climb monologue. Never count volume level or seconds under threat.

15. [MINOR] Forty-eight owner decisions, several duplicated or already answered
Where: "Decisions for the owner" at the end of all six drafts.
Problem: There are 48 questions. Several are duplicated:
 - how a catch works (creatures 1, systems 2);
 - where he restarts (creatures 4, systems 2 and 4);
 - what the gun does (creatures 6, systems 5);
 - the noise ring (systems 8, audio 8);
 - Kayla's voice (creatures 2, audio 4).
Some he has already answered: audio 5 against his "whisper in the voices of people it took" and "says your name
sometimes and mocks you"; story 7 against "not all of them make it". A 48-question quiz is homework and could stall
his approval.
Fix: Put one merged list of at most 12 questions in the game map, each in his own words and with a picture where it
is visual. Merge the duplicates, mark the answered ones as settled, and let everything else go ahead on the
recommendation unless he objects.
