# Stay Behind: survival and systems design (document 3 of 6)

**Second edition, 21 November 2026: agrees with the game map and the canon sheet; where they differ from this document, they win.**

The systems designer's document. The first draft built on research reports 01 (survival systems), 02 (puzzles,
sections 1, 2 and 5) and 06 (audio: the noise table and the listening devices). This edition applies the game map
(`game-map.md`), the owner's twelve answers ("use recomended": the recommended option for every one), the rulings in
`drafts/CHANGES-NEEDED.md` and the canon sheet (`canon.md`).

**Order of authority.** The owner's own words (requirements.md sections 7 to 10, owner-checklist.md) > the game map >
CHANGES-NEEDED.md > the canon sheet > this document. `cutscenes.md` is the authority on every cutscene; where this
document mentions one, it only points at it.

**What this document owns, and what it cites.** It owns Danny's moves and body (canon 5), light and power (canon 6),
the items (canon 10), saving, the restart points and the difficulty presets (canon 11), and the gun, the scanner, the
flight and low gravity (canon 13). Where it gives one of those numbers it is the canon value; only this document
retunes them, and then the canon sheet is updated in the same change. Everything else is cited as "canon n" and not
copied: people, needs and fates (canon 1, story), the aliens (canon 2), chapter minutes and the evening's times
(canon 3), places, heights, ways in and lantern rooms (canon 4, world), the noise table, occlusion, every hearing
threshold and mask (canon 7, audio), the hunter (canon 8, creatures), the seven kinds (canon 9, creatures), the symbol
language (canon 12, world), the self-destruct (canon 13, world), sound, music and Kayla's radio rules (canon 14) and the
build (canon 15, production).

**How to read it.** All numbers are starting values for the **Survivor** preset, to be tuned from playtest logs.
"Units" of power, "ticks" of fuel and decibels of noise are internal and never shown to the player. The first draft's
[PROPOSAL] and [Pn] marks are gone: every point they marked is now settled by the map, the owner's answers or the
rulings (see "Decided" at the end).

**Three rules held everywhere.** Nothing in the school can be killed: every tool lights, hides, distracts or scares
off. Crafting is game logic only (named objects snap together; no real recipe for anything dangerous). Graphic
horror is for the creatures and Danny's own deaths, the hunter's catch included; nothing graphic ever happens on screen
to a student or to anyone taken.

## 1. The core loop of a school chapter (Chapters 2 to 5; lengths: canon 3)

One floor, one creature rule, one way in and one way home. Every school chapter has the same five beats, so the
player learns the rhythm and the floors change what the rhythm costs. Chapter 3 and Chapter 5 break the pattern on
purpose (below).

| Beat | Minutes | What the player does | What it costs or gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The shelter | 4 to 5 | Hears the floor's goal (the way up) and one survivor's request; watches Kayla's camera view of the floor; packs from the cabinet; winds the stopwatch; writes in the logbook | Choosing what to carry is the first decision: 8 slots, and what he leaves the shelter keeps |
| 2. Into the floor by its own way in | 2 to 4 | Every stair door is locked on its stair side (canon 4), so each floor is entered its own way: F1 by the freight lift, F2 by the bleachers to the running track, F3 by the theatre's fly gallery, F4 by the exterior fire escape into the chemistry window. The hunter can be on any of them | A short route he has not used before, and the first look at the floor's creature |
| 3. The floor | 35 to 38 | Learns the creature's rule in a safe first room, then the floor's big puzzle (two or three clue rooms), side rooms with supplies, the survivor's item, the floor's evacuation plan (its map), its one lantern room, a dumbwaiter hatch to send things down. He props the floor's stair door open from the inside: that is his way home | Every room is a trade of light against noise against time. The loud way through any lock is always there and always brings the floor's creature |
| 4. Home by the stair he propped open | 3 to 5 | Once the way up is open, Danny goes back down the stair he propped, with what he found | The floor's creature and the hunter both know he has been there; the return is short and tense, never a slog |
| 5. What changed | 10 to 11 | Gives the request item (or doesn't), hears who is worse or better, the return's one playable activity, the night talk at the generator, rests to heal, writes in the logbook | The hidden ledger moves (section 7). A payback arrives (an oiled door, a fixed camera, a parcel in the dumbwaiter). A new key on the ring opens a closet on an earlier floor |

Beats 1 and 5 together are the chapter's time in the shelter (about 15 minutes after each of Chapters 2, 3 and 4:
canon 3); the other three beats are the floor.

**The power-closet pattern** (the floor's way up depends on powering its electrical closet from the basement) is used
on two floors only: **F1**, where the closet powers the office's bell and PA rack for Halftime Draw, and **F4**, where
the stair doors are held by powered locks (canon 4). The riser key (Ch2, the office safe) opens every closet. On F2
and F3 the closets feed puzzles (the theatre dimmers, the server room or the language lab: section 6.3) but are not the
way up.

**Chapter 3 has no calm return.** After the Mark on the theatre stage (canon 2), Danny wakes hurt and must cross the
live floor home in failing light, the Still Ones still on it: beat 4 is that crossing, and beat 5 is a grief return
(Mari's empty cot; the scene itself: `cutscenes.md`).

**Chapter 5 runs backwards.** Danny goes up first to clear the route (beats 1 to 3: Heat Map, the roof's chained door).
Then the breach: the hunter follows the Mark to the shelter and cuts the generator, and the group flees up the stair in
the dark. Danny must power F4's closet and release its stair door before they reach it, with the helicopter coming.
There is no beat 4 or 5: the chapter ends on the roof.

**What changes between visits.** While Danny is away, a quiet director changes the floors he has finished: lights
he left on are off, a Still One stands somewhere new, vent covers are open, survivor chalk arrows appear. Going back
to an earlier floor by its propped stair, for a closet his new key opens, is always optional and always worth one or
two supplies.

**The rhythm inside a floor** follows the owner's numbers for the hunter: something happens (heard close, glimpsed, a
near miss) about every 15 to 20 seconds while Danny sprints, 30 while he walks, 60 while he crouches (the full rhythm,
the same on every preset: canon 8). Sneaking is slower but buys quiet. Each floor's own creature sits on top of that
with its own rule.

**Chapter 1 (the basement; length: canon 3)** is the loop's long first lesson, in this order (the story's order,
`1-story.md`; puzzle minutes are the world document's, section 2, and its pacing table wins; the rest of the chapter's 75 minutes is exploring and story):

| Part | About | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| The shelter forms, guided; within the first 15 minutes Ray at the door, the hunter's hand on the wired glass behind him | 10 min | the steel door, the barred shelter, the hunter's first near miss |
| Start the generator with Gus; learn the gauge, the wind-up stopwatch and the circuits | 7 min (world 1.1, Gus's Ears) | the live clock (section 6) |
| The steam tunnels to the kitchen undercroft for food, the hunter heard in the pipes; Gus's workshop for tools | 7 min (world 1.2, The Pantry) | the tunnels, the loud and the quiet way |
| The first power failure, scripted, inside the shelter (everyone counting aloud in the dark), which brings the Dark Dwellers | 5 min | the Dwellers' rule, taught while the hunter is away |
| **Flare Road**, the fuel run from the far fuel store: which three circuits to light, the gaps bridged with flares (the steps are the world document's) | 20 min (world 1.3) | light as a currency |
| Free play to bring back the survivors' first needs and open the way up (the freight lift) | 5 min | the request loop |
| The first night in the shelter: Ray's cards, Owen's walk past the dark tunnel | 10 min | the shelter as home (beat 5) |

The shelter parts make about 20 minutes (canon 3). The generator is a live clock only here (section 6).

## 2. Movement and the body

### 2.1 Moving (this document owns the speeds; the step levels are audio's, canon 5 and 7)

| Move | Speed (m/s) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Walk | 1.5 | Default. Hunter rhythm about every 30 s (canon 8) |
| Crouch | 0.9 | Toggle or hold (option). Hunter rhythm about every 60 s. Fits under desks and through low gaps |
| Crawl | 1.0 | From crouch. Under the stage, ducts and crawlways (about 110 cm wide and 120 cm high, so the hunter can follow: canon 4), collapsed ceilings. The torch must be in the mouth or off (see 2.5). Faster than the hunter in a duct (its duct crawl: canon 8) |
| Sprint | 4.5 | About 6 s of breath, 8 s to recover. Hunter rhythm every 15 to 20 s. After a sprint his breathing is loud for 6 s. A little faster than the hunter's chase (canon 8) |
| Limp (Badly hurt) | 1.1 | No sprint (section 9) |
| Lean | n/a | Q / E, about 30 cm. Silent. Works standing and crouched, and from inside a locker by its slats |
| Peek under a door | n/a | Silent. Crouch at a door: the view drops to the gap. Light and feet on the other side are visible |
| Listen at a door or vent | n/a | Silent. Hold: the world ducks 10 dB, the other side loses its wall muffle, a caption names what is heard ("[slow steps, moving left]"). Fixed view, so he is exposed while he listens |
| Climb | 0.6 | Context only: shelves, a desk to a ceiling tile, a fence, ladders. No jumping puzzles. A drop landing is loud (canon 5) |
| Vault and drop | n/a | Over a counter or a fallen locker; dropping from a ledge |

Every gait has one step level, modified by the surface and the debris underfoot and by a full pack; all of those are
audio's (canon 5 and 7). There is a jump (short) because players expect one; nothing in the game needs it. The vault's
and the jump's landing levels are not yet in canon 7 (see "Depends on other documents").

### 2.2 Breath

- Breath is a sound, not a bar. Calm, uneasy, frightened and winded each have their own loop.
- **Hold breath** (a held button, any time): cuts breath out of the noise sum. The strain is heard from 10 s; at
  14 s (7 s when winded or hurt) he gasps (canon 5 and 7) unless he lets go early with a quiet exhale. As it runs out
  the edges of the view close in and his heartbeat rises. Letting go early is the skill.
- In cold rooms (the walk-in freezer, the cold room, the roof, the Holds on the mothership) his breath shows as fog in
  the torch beam. The hunter can see the fog at short range (canon 8): a small honest risk. A person's breath fogs and
  a Mimic's does not (canon 9), so the same fog is a test on F3.

### 2.3 Doors

- Doors open by degrees with the mouse or stick. A slow open makes no creak; a normal one creaks, a fast one bangs
  (eased, normal and slammed: canon 7).
- **Peek** through the wired glass panel that every classroom door has, or through a crack.
- **Hold a door shut** against a push: both hands on screen, the door judders, hold the button and push back with the
  mouse for about 4 s. Lose and he is knocked back a step (Fine to Hurt). Only the hunter, the Patrol and the Brute
  push doors; animals scratch. The Still Ones cannot turn handles (canon 9).
- **Wedge** a door with a chair, a mop or a rubber wedge: one use; it creaks loudly when tested, which tells Danny
  something is on the other side. A hunter that has learned barred doors holds against a wedge for less (its habit:
  canon 8).
- **Lock behind** with a key from the ring: the turn is heard (canon 7).

### 2.4 Hiding

Each kind of hiding place protects from some things and not others. The price of every one is the same idea: the
longer you stay, the louder you get and the more likely the hunter comes back to where it last heard you.

| Place | Where | Protects from | Does not protect from | Price of staying |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Locker | Every corridor | Sight (hunter, Mimics, Patrol) | Hearing (Listeners hear breath through the door); the hunter if it watched him enter, if it heard him there, or once it has learned to open lockers first (canon 8) | Breath louder after 40 s; torch must be off (light leaks through the slats) |
| Under (desk, bed, stage, bus seat) | Classrooms, nurse, theatre, bus garage | Sight from standing height | The hunter if it watched him go under, or once it has learned to sweep low (canon 8); the Swarm, which comes down from above | Dust: the cough comes after 30 s unless he holds breath |
| Behind (curtain, coat rack, shower stall) | Theatre, changing rooms | Sight from the front | Feet show under a curtain in torchlight | Cannot see out except by leaning |
| Cold (walk-in freezer, cold room) | Kitchens, the science floor | The Swarm (warmth) | Everything else; the door is loud | After 45 s he shivers (a sound); after 90 s he starts to be Hurt |
| Dark corner | Anywhere unlit | The hunter's sight beyond 6 m while he is still, 10 m while he moves (canon 8) | Dark Dwellers, who live there; the Still Ones, who move when unseen | He must kill his light, so he cannot see either |
| Duct or crawlway | Ceilings and walls | Sight, and things too big to fit | The hunter (it follows through ducts, slower than Danny, always heard: canon 8), and once it has learned a duct it waits at the far end; the Swarm (ducts are its home) | Sheet metal is loud underfoot (canon 7); turning round takes 2 s |
| Bin, laundry cart, trophy-cabinet room | Placed | Sight | Hearing at close range | Getting out is loud |
| The shelter, door barred, lit | Fixed | Everything (the hunter never enters a lit, barred shelter: canon 8) | The one breach, in Chapter 5, which cuts the generator first | None, except the generator clock on Stay Behind |

On the mothership the Brute taps and sniffs a hiding place before it tears it open; metal and small places hold
(canon 9).

**Hidden tuning (never told to the player, in any text):** the hunter searches where it last heard Danny after 20 to
40 s, and it can search a hiding place it heard him in. Hiding is how he waits out a pass, not where he lives.

### 2.5 Danny's body is the interface

- **Hands.** Left hand holds the light; right hand does everything else (doors, items, the radio button). When he
  uses both hands (bandaging, holding a door, carrying the sledgehammer or the extinguisher, crawling with no strap
  clip) the light goes in his mouth (dimmer, wobbles with his breathing) or off. Hands shake with fear, and the torch
  beam shakes with them: a shaky beam is the readout of his fear. A blood-dark sleeve shows when he is Hurt. From
  Chapter 3 the Mark's black frost-lines on his forearm show whenever his sleeve rides up (canon 2).
- **Breath** says how winded and how frightened he is (above).
- **Pulse** is a heartbeat heard only above a fear threshold and while hidden. It is information for the player, not
  noise for the creatures.
- **What is drawn on screen:** nothing by default. A small dot appears only when something usable is in reach (it can
  be turned off). A two-second line names what he picked up ("Batteries, two"). Subtitles and sound captions. The
  backpack when he opens it. Prompts appear on first use only (always, on the Story preset).
- The research's fitness-band idea is dropped: the lithium pulse (section 3.3) would have killed a band, and the body
  already says what it said.

## 3. Light and noise: the two currencies

Every floor charges a different mix of the two. Light lets him see, keeps the Dark Dwellers back and holds the Still
Ones; it shows him to the hunter and the Patrol, and the Mimics move only while out of it. Noise lets him go fast,
force locks and lure; every creature but the Still Ones can hear it (the Dark Dwellers only in darkness: canon 7 and 9).

### 3.1 The torch and its battery

- **Danny's torch** is the janitor's old work torch (Gus gives it in Chapter 1). Two AA cells (one "pair").
- A fresh pair gives **12 to 15 minutes of on-time**, the same life on every preset (presets change how many pairs are
  found: canon 6 and 11). Off costs nothing, so switching it on and off is the skill. Below 25% the beam yellows and
  narrows; below 10% it flickers. A shake brings 5 s of brightness back (a rattle).
- **Cupped beam** (hold the light button): his fingers cover the lens, a dim red glow, enough to read a note or find
  a step at 1 m, at half the drain, and not seen beyond 4 m. Small, cheap, and the move players remember. It does not
  keep a Dark Dweller off: only the open beam and real lights do (the owner's rule of 21 November).
- Changing batteries: 2 s, both hands; the old pair goes into the pack, and it can be thrown as a lure (canon 7).
- **The free fallback: the crank torch** (a camping torch from Gus's workshop, Chapter 1). Three cranks give 20 s of
  weaker light, and the crank is heard (40 dB, canon 6 and 7). He is never stuck in the dark, only made louder.
- **Who sees the beam:** the hunter notices a beam from 20 m (30 m once it has learned the torch: canon 8); the Patrol
  from 30 m; Listeners are blind; Mimics move only while out of the beam; the Dark Dwellers are held back by its cone
  (9 m, 30 degrees); the Still Ones are held only while lit and looked at (canon 9).

### 3.3 Why phones are no help: the lithium pulse (canon 6; the story owns the event)

At 7:56 on Friday (the pulse: canon 3) every lithium battery in the city swells, goes hot and dies: phones, laptops,
car fobs, e-bikes, modern cameras. The grid fails with them. Screens go black and warm in pockets. What still works is
what runs on something else:

- **alkaline cells:** AA torches, the school's analogue walkie-talkies (switched off in the PE office's steel
  cabinet), the AA tape camcorder, the AA flash camera, the band room's cassette deck;
- **zinc-air:** Gus's hearing aids;
- **lead-acid:** the generator's starter, the 1962 shortwave base, the badge reader, the bell timer, and older cars,
  whose alarms still go off;
- **NiMH:** the thermal camera, which the crank charges;
- **anything mechanical:** Kayla's film camera, the coach's wind-up stopwatch, the crank torch;
- **whatever the generator feeds.**

Alien technology is untouched, and the rescue comes from outside the city. The rule makes light, maps and calls things
he earns, and it makes the walkie-talkie link with Kayla precious. A dead phone in the pack is a keepsake, nothing more.

### 3.4 Noise (audio's, canon 7)

One table drives three things: how loud each sound plays (so the ear learns the scale), what each creature hears, and
the optional noise ring. That table belongs to audio and is canon 7; this document keeps no copy. In short: every
action and every step has one level at 1 m; what reaches a creature is that level less the loss over the walked path
and less the occlusion of doors, walls and floors; the creature hears it when it beats its threshold. The "heard at"
distances for a walk and a sprint, for each kind, are in canon 7, and so are the masks (the F1 heating knocks, the
rain on the gym roof, the nurse's white-noise machine, the running generator in the basement). Power off in the
basement means the generator's mask goes with it, and his steps suddenly carry.

The actions this document adds (the stopwatch, the timer rattle, the crank, the gun and the rest) take their levels
from canon 7. The few sounds this document needs that canon 7 does not yet hold are listed under "Depends on other
documents", for audio to add.

### 3.5 How the player reads his own noise

- **By ear first.** The mix favours his own steps and clothing when he sneaks; the room's echo tells him how big it is,
  and big rooms announce him. Within a chapter the ear knows glass from carpet.
- **By the world.** A Listener's hum stops when one has locked on; the hunter's whisper stops when it has noticed him
  (its alert tell is silence: canon 8).
- **The noise ring:** an optional faint ring at the foot of the screen that swells with each sound he makes, using
  canon 7's numbers. On by default on Story only; off by default on Survivor and Stay Behind (canon 7 and 11). The
  real microphone is never used.

## 4. The backpack and the binder (canon 5)

- **The pack: 8 slots**, a real school backpack. Opening it swings it to his front; he looks down into it; items sit in
  it as real objects in a 4 x 2 grid. **The world never pauses** (a pause is an option on the Story preset only).
  Opening and closing take about half a second, and the zip is heard if he opens it fast, silent if slow (hold).
- **Two chest pockets**, on quick buttons: **the light pocket** (torch, crank torch, lighter or glow stick) and **the
  throw pocket** (one throwable or one scare-off tool). A scare-off tool in the throw pocket turns a catch into a
  struggle he can win on every preset (section 9).
- **Also on the chest strap, taking no slot:** the walkie-talkie and the coach's wind-up stopwatch.
- **What takes space:** battery pairs stack 4 to a slot; glow sticks 3; bandages 2; parts (tape, wire) 5; food tins
  2; a fuel can takes 2 slots and sloshes when he runs, silent when full; tools 1 or 2; the sledgehammer and the
  extinguisher are carried in both hands, never in the pack (he cannot hold a light, and dropping them is loud).
- **No upgrade:** the backpack stays small, 8 slots, all game; there is no bigger bag in Chapter 4 (the owner's rule
  of 21 November).
- **The binder** (never costs a slot): Gus's key ring (every key he earns), notes and letters, the floor maps (each
  floor's fire-evacuation plan, taken off the wall), the symbol pages, the chalk, and Danny's own pocket notebook. The
  map marks itself in his pencil (an X on a locked door, a circle on a puzzle). Kayla's notes come in blue ink, added
  when she tells him something. Opening the binder is the same: no pause.
- **Notes can be turned over.** Some clues are on the back.
- **Stashes.** The shelter's cabinet holds anything, without limit. Each lantern room has one shelf of 4 slots that
  stays there. **The dumbwaiter**: from a hatch on any school floor (the F1 kitchen, the F2 staff room, the F3 staff
  lounge, the F4 home-economics kitchen; the world document places them) he can send items down to the shelter's
  cabinet (one way; heard while it runs, canon 7). Sending medicine down before the return is how he helps someone in
  time.

## 6. The generator and the power budget (canon 6; seen in: Amnesia: The Bunker, the fuel clock; Alien: Isolation and the Dead Space remake, choosing which rooms get power)

### 6.1 How it works (Chapter 1)

- **The machine** is the school's diesel standby set in the boiler room, through the wall from the shelter (Gus's
  workshop behind a steel door).
- **The gauge** is a needle over 8 ticks. A fuel can adds 2. Refuelling just before the needle crosses a tick saves
  a little (Gus's note hints at it).
- **Starting it** is physical: the right relay, bleed, prime, choke, pull. Once she is ready she catches on the first
  pull. Each pull is 80 dB (canon 7), loud enough for the whole basement, and the hunter answers it in the pipes; a
  failed pull says by its sound what is wrong (Gus's Ears, the owner's choice: world 1.1). Gus teaches the cupped beam
  here (3.1). A start in the dark with something near is the scene players remember.
- **Burn:** 0.25 ticks a minute at idle plus 0.1 for each power unit in use. **Capacity is 6 units at once**; switch on
  more and the breaker trips with a bang and a 5-second blackout (it teaches the limit once).

| Circuit | Units | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Shelter lights | 1 | The room is lit; the survivors calm |
| Shelter heater | 2 | Warmth; the sick get better |
| Radio base and cameras | 1 | Kayla can see and talk; the tape logger records the open channel |
| Boiler-side corridor lights | 1 | Keeps Dark Dwellers out of the near corridor |
| Tunnel lights | 1 | Keeps Dark Dwellers out of the tunnels |
| Kitchen undercroft lights | 1 | The way to the food and the dumbwaiter's foot |
| Sump pump | 2 | Drains the flooded stretch (a one-time job) |
| Freight lift | 2 | Opens the way up at the chapter's end |

With 4 units on, a full tank lasts about 12 minutes; with everything allowed (6) about 9; at idle with only the
shelter's lights, about 23. The Story preset burns at 0.65 of that, Stay Behind at 1.25 (canon 11).

- **The stopwatch.** Danny sets the coach's wind-up stopwatch to the gauge (hold a button at the generator). Lifting
  his wrist shows the minutes left; it rings at 60 s. The ring is a noise he can silence (canon 7), at the price of the
  warning.
- **When fuel runs out** the lights fade over 4 s, never snap. The hum winds down over 3 s and then there is real
  silence. The exit signs stay lit on their own batteries. The Dark Dwellers come out of every unlit space. Their tell
  comes first: the generator's note sags a minute before the fuel runs out (canon 9).
- **Fuel sources:** cans (4 to 6 in the chapter, with the pity rule); the fuel-store drum (10 ticks in all, then
  dry); and the free fallback, **siphoning from the school buses** in the bus garage: 40 s per tick, a quiet gurgle
  (canon 7), far from home. He is never stuck, only made to take a risk.
- **The first failure is scripted** so that the Dark Dwellers' arrival is taught, not punished; the gauge is real after
  that. **Flare Road**, the chapter's big puzzle, is where the circuits become a choice under pressure (which three to
  light; its steps are the world document's).

### 6.2 What each choice costs

Every unit is someone's comfort. Lighting the tunnels for the trip out leaves the room cold. Running the pump drains
the flood but eats two units for as long as it runs. Keeping the radio base on means Kayla can warn him; switching it
off buys about three minutes of fuel and silence on the radio. The survivors say what they lose, briefly, once
("Heater's off again. Okay.").

**Not a chore:** the clock stops while Danny is in the shelter with the door shut (except on Stay Behind); no puzzle
needs fuel he can run out of; the slow free source is always there.

### 6.3 After the basement

From Chapter 2 the generator becomes **the shelter's reserve**, not a stopwatch:

- Gus sets it to the night setting. Each chapter Danny is away costs the shelter 4 ticks. When he comes back, the
  gauge shows what is left, and the room shows it too (lights low, the heater off, Dwellers' scratches on the outside
  of the steel door if it reached zero).
- **Each floor has an electrical closet fed from the basement** (ELEC 1 to 4, opened by the riser key; each closet's
  limit is 2 units). Powering a floor circuit draws from the same reserve:

| Floor circuit | Units | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| F1 PA and bells | 1 | Halftime Draw (F1's way up: the power-closet pattern) |
| F2 corridor lights | 1 | Light on the Still Ones' floor |
| F2 theatre dimmers | 1 | Curtain, the stage lights |
| F3 server room **or** language lab | 1 | one of the two, never both |
| F4 greenhouse mats | 1 | Heat Map's decoy |
| F4 stair-door locks | set by the world document | released before the fleeing group arrives (F4's way home: the power-closet pattern); fed from the penthouse can after the breach (canon 6) |

- Every unit spent upstairs is heat taken from the survivors. That is the choice, stated in one line by Gus.
- Bringing a can home is visible care. **Nothing on the path ever fails for lack of fuel**; only the shelter's comfort,
  its survivors' health and the ledger feel it.
- **The roof (Chapter 5):** the roof floodlights (2 units) run on **their own sealed can in the penthouse**, so they
  always light. Whatever is left in the reserve only adds time on the roof; nothing fails for lack of it.

### 7.2 How it runs

- **About 65 minutes in the shelter across the game** (60 to 75; the split between returns: canon 3), so the player

- **One playable activity per return** (the story document's table): Ray's cards and Owen's walk past the dark

## 8. Kayla on the radio (her rules: canon 14, story)

- **The device.** One of the school's analogue walkie-talkies on Danny's chest strap, push-to-talk on a button. A
  **volume knob** with four stops: off, 1, 2 and 3 (their levels: canon 7; 3 opens the squelch). At 1, words can be
  lost under the generator, the rain, a creature. Turning her down or off never counts against him. **Priya's
  earpiece** (the Ch4 return) takes the radio's sound out of the noise sum entirely; it is the
  ledger's best payback.
- **The channel is recorded.** Priya's tape logger on the 1962 base station records the channel whenever it is open
  (the walkie itself records nothing); its tape is played in the epilogue.
- **What she can see.** The camera monitors at the radio base, and only while its circuit has power. Working cameras:
  basement 6, F1 4, F2 3, **F3 none** in Chapter 4, F4 1 (Roll Call's two-link patch restores the roof antenna and F4's
  one camera: canon 14). Her help fades as he climbs, which is the point.
- **What she says.** A creature's place by camera name ("It's in the east stair. Going down."); the floor's layout from
  the evacuation plan in the shelter; things she remembers about the school; her own fear. Half her calls are not about
  the task. She is wrong once (Chapter 2) and says sorry. She never speaks a code's value: it goes onto his map in her
  blue ink. She never answers a Mimic's question for him.
- **When she speaks.** In calm moments, or when he calls. While a creature hunts him, only warnings of 6 s or less. She
  is silent when the radio is off and when the base has no power. **On F3 he switches her off**, because the Mimics walk
  toward any voice, the radio's included (canon 9); she comes up in person by the stair he propped open. From the
  moment she is taken at the crash, at the end of Chapter 5, the radio is static only; it is never faked.
- **Talking to the dead radio (Chapter 6).** He can still press to talk, quietly (canon 7): his voice and his jokes
  carry the city, and nobody answers. At the wreck his own voice comes out of Kayla's walkie (`cutscenes.md`). In the

- **Hints, by a ladder** tied to the puzzle tier (canon 11; seen in: the Silent Hill 2 remake, puzzle difficulty set
  apart from the scares). When he is stuck on a puzzle step (no progress for the time below), she asks what he sees;

  next rung early. In Chapter 1 Gus gives the hints in the shelter; after Kayla is taken, the hints come from Danny's
  notebook ("what would K say").

## 9. Health, injury and death

- **Three states, no number: Fine, Hurt, Badly hurt** (canon 5). Hurt: heavier breath, a dark sleeve, shakier hands.
  Badly hurt: a limp (1.1 m/s, no sprint), his hand on the wall as he goes, breath a Listener can hear close by, and
  **bleeding**: fresh drips the hunter follows for 30 s. A bandage stops the bleeding.
- **Hits** (an animal's swipe or sting, a door lost, a fall over 3 m) drop him one state. **Catches** end the attempt,
  unless he struggles free (below).
- **What a catch is (canon 9, creatures).** The animals (Dark Dwellers, Listeners, Still Ones, Mimics, the Swarm, the
  Brute) **maul**: a short, graphic first-person death of Danny, 3 to 5 s, skippable after the first time each is seen,
  with a cut-away option in the settings. **The hunter's catch** is its own scene: its fingers go into Danny's mouth and
  throat to pull his voice out, the scream cut off, the drag into the dark, about 6 s, skippable after the first; on the
  next attempt it whispers that scream back once (canon 8). **The Patrol take**: a collar of light, and he is lifted
  away. Every catch has a tell in the 2 s before it (the hunter's, at least 5 s).
- **Escaping a catch.** If a scare-off tool (a flare, the air horn, the extinguisher, or from the city the charged gun)
  is in the throw pocket, a catch turns into a struggle (a hold, never mashing); win it and the tool is spent, Danny is
  Hurt, and he is free. The window is 1.5 s on Survivor, 2.0 on Story, 1.0 on Stay Behind (canon 11). **On Survivor the
  first hunter catch in each chapter is a struggle he can win even with an empty pocket**; on Story, the first catch by
  anything in each chapter; on Stay Behind, none free.
- **Healing:** bandage (4 s), first-aid kit (8 s), painkillers (no heal, no limp for 3 min), mend gel (3 s, from the
  city on). All take both hands on screen and can be interrupted. **Rest** on a cot in the shelter heals one state and
  costs the shelter one tick. Story adds slow self-healing from Hurt to Fine after 60 s without harm.
- **What dying costs (the owner's decision 2): almost nothing but the fright.** On Story and Survivor a death restarts
  Danny close by, at the start of that encounter, that large-puzzle stage or that solved step, with puzzle progress
  kept, in under 5 s. There is a restart point at every encounter's entrance, every large-puzzle stage and every solved
  step. Restart points are not saves: they hold nothing across a quit. On Stay Behind a death goes back to the last
  save (section 10). On Story and Survivor the flight has a checkpoint at each segment and the self-destruct one at
  each stage; Stay Behind has only the player's own saves, with no checkpoints at all (the owner's rule of 21
  November).
  The world keeps the same codes after a death (the seed is fixed at New Game).

## 10. Saving (canon 11; lantern rooms: canon 4)

- **The shelter: the logbook.** Danny writes one line (3 s, the pen scratching); the line is a short diary beat that
  changes with what has happened. These lines, read back on the load screen, become a quiet record of the whole game.
- **Every later area has one small lantern room:** a room with a lock where a survivor or Danny has left a lantern. He
  lights it and writes in his pocket notebook. One per school floor (F1 the nurse's rest room, F2 the lighting booth, F3
  the yearbook darkroom, F4 the biology prep office beside the cold room), two in the city (it is about 85 minutes: the
  laundromat in Market Square, the parking kiosk in Civic Center Plaza), three on the mothership (the Underdeck, the
  Holds, the root of the Spine). They sit 15 to 25 minutes of play apart, deliberately uneven. Story adds one extra
  lantern per area. The roof has none.
- **What the lanterns are for now.** On Story and Survivor the restart points take care of deaths, so the logbook and
  the lanterns are for stopping and coming back, and for Stay Behind, where they are the only way back after a death.
  (Reading the lantern rooms as the owner's "safe rooms" is a designer reading: see "Open".)
- **Not while hunted:** within 25 m of a searching creature the lamp flickers and he stops writing.
- **Chapter autosave** at the start of each chapter, as he asked.
- **Quit safely:** "Quit and keep my place" from the pause menu, anywhere, writes a one-time save that is deleted when
  loaded: he can stop for dinner without losing progress or getting a free retry.
- Three manual slots plus the autosave and the quit save. A save never rolls new codes.

## 12. The city and the mothership: what changes (canon 13)

**The gun (from the city's midpoint, the Takedown).**
- **Six energy cells** glow on its side; he checks them by lifting it (no HUD). Its charge whine rises with the stored
  energy, so the ear counts the last shots.
- **Quick bolt** (1 cell): staggers a Patrol soldier 4 s, scatters a Swarm group or a Dark Dweller, drives the hunter
  back 30 s. **Charged shot** (hold 1.5 s, 3 cells): kills a Patrol soldier, drives the hunter off 90 s, staggers the
  Brute 3 s. It never kills the hunter.
- Every shot is 90 dB: the block hears it (canon 7). **Recharge** at alien pylons (20 s for 3 cells, a hum the Patrol
  notices within 30 m) or from energy cartridges taken from soldiers (2 cells each).
- **Takedown:** the first one is by hand, from behind, in first person, under the player's input (the world document's
  big puzzle of Chapter 6). Later ones are a point-blank bolt to an unaware lone soldier's neck plate: 1 cell, 50 dB.
- The rule stays: **hiding is still the first answer.** Six cells do not win a street. The first half of the city is
  played without the gun; the second half is hunted, with its few shots.

**The scanner.** Danny's phone has been dead since the pulse; the scanner is the first map and the first tracker he
earns, and like every information tool it costs. A **ping** shows warm life within 25 m for 4 s through walls (the
taken in their cradles glow differently), **spends one of the gun's cells** (a cartridge refills two), then needs
20 s before the next, and is a 40 dB chirp the Patrol hears. It **reads glyph
locks** with the symbols he has learned (the more he knows, the fewer the guesses), and **shows the map** of a deck once
linked at a node. There is no other locator, and no free one.

**The city (Chapter 6, at night; the route: canon 4).** It opens with a three-minute scripted chase: a Patrol
searchlight finds him on the Tannery Building roof within seconds, and he runs over the roofs and down a fire escape to
the first hide. No generator and no shelter; two lantern rooms; the torch and the backpack as before; street fires as
light (the traffic lights are dead); **the alarms of older cars** (lead-acid, not fobs) as huge lures, set off by a
thrown brick, and an alarm or a live line blinds a Patrol scanner for a while (canon 9). The radio is static, but he can
press to talk (section 8). The Patrol's room-by-room search makes doors and lines of sight the new currency, and they
take people in front of him.

**The mothership (Chapter 8).** The alien power budget returns as a twisted generator: the Holds' cradles, the doors
and the lights draw from one alien board, so every cradle opened costs power somewhere else (the ledger sets who is
there; the halls' board is the world document's). The self-destruct's first lock twists it once more (world, canon
13). The Brute tears soft hiding places open after its 2.5 s tap-and-sniff tell, and it is too big for narrow doors, so
moving and looping beat hiding (canon 9).

**Low gravity** (the Spine, about 15 minutes): no walking; he pushes off surfaces (aimed with the view), drifts in a
straight line, grabs handholds, and can **fire the gun behind him as a thruster** (1 cell) when he has drifted wrong.
Drift slows itself on Story; "down" changes once; the air is thin; a sound and a soft vignette mark the moment he is out
of reach of any surface. (Seen in: the Dead Space remake.)

## 13. The flight (Chapter 7, about 18 minutes; canon 13)

- **A set course in six segments**, a checkpoint at each (not on Stay Behind: section 9), lifting straight off the
  Chapter 6 landing pad:

| Segment | About | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Lift-off and the hunter's hand | 2 min | Straight up from the pad; the hunter's hand on the canopy for a long minute until he scrapes it off against a mast |
| The rooftops | 3 min | Low over the city's roofs, the first pursuers |
| The pack | 4 min | A growing pack of craft; three forks read by their danger-lane glyphs (canon 12) |
| The cloud | 3 min | The pursuers fall away; Song 2 plays while he still steers; the talk button (below) |
| The fins | 4 min | The mothership's outer fins, a vast space |
| The hangar mouth | 2 min | The run in and the crash into a hangar |

  No canyon and no bridge: the city stays below as a far field of lights under cloud. The ship keeps moving forward on
  its own; he steers inside a corridor about 80 m wide and 40 m high around the course.
- **Controls:** steer (mouse or stick), **dodge** (a sidestep roll, 0.4 s of no hits, 1.2 s cooldown), **boost** (3 s,
  refills in 6 s; boosting shows a bright trail that pursuers lock on to faster), **shoot** (unlimited, overheats after
  3 s of fire and cools in 2 s).
- **Fair:** every missile sounds a lock tone 1.5 s before it fires (2.5 s on Story); scraping scenery costs a hit, never
  a death; the hull shows damage as cracks across the canopy glass and an alarm (4 hits, 6 on Story); a checkpoint at
  each segment (not on Stay Behind); aim assist on by default.
- **Exciting:** speed and space change every segment (the close rooftops, the open cloud, a vast fin), pursuers grow
  from two to a pack, one quiet segment in the middle (the cloud: no enemies, the city a field of lights below).
- **The talk button:** in the cloud, if he holds the radio's talk button, Danny tells Kayla, who cannot hear, what he

## 15. Options and access

- **Subtitles:** on by default; size (4 steps), background box, speaker names, colours per speaker, and **sound
  captions** for every gameplay sound with a direction mark ("[whisper, behind left]", "[glass crunch, ahead]"); the
  hunter's whisper is captioned with its words.
- **Controls:** full rebinding for keyboard, mouse and controller; hold or toggle for crouch, lean, breath and
  sprint; a hold instead of mashing for every struggle; mouse and stick sensitivity; invert; aim assist (gun and flight).
- **Comfort:** field of view 70 to 110; head bob, camera shake and motion blur each 0 to 100%; flicker and strobe
  reduction (lights fade instead of flashing, the flash camera's flash is softened); a horizon lock in the flight and in
  low gravity; a "cut away" option for Danny's death scenes; a softened look for the Swarm for players who fear small
  crawling things.
- **Audio:** separate volumes for voices, radio, music, effects and ambience; dynamic range (headphones, speakers,
  night); headphone 3D sound on or off; mono.
- **Help:** the noise ring (on by default on Story only); the usable-thing dot; the "what now" radio call; prompts
  always on.
- **Photo mode:** from the pause menu when no creature is hunting him: a free camera within 3 m, focus and exposure,
  hide hands, a few film looks, no creature posing. Players will share the world, and the world is the selling point.
  (It is on the cut list if the schedule runs long: canon 15.)
- **Single-player, built tidy:** every system here is single-player, with nothing that would block co-op being added
  later as its own mode (the owner's answer on co-op).
