**Second edition, 21 November 2026: agrees with the game map and the canon sheet; where they differ from this document, they win.**

## 1. The premise, the themes, the title

**The premise in ten lines**
1. A cold Friday night in late November. Rovloc High is packed for the first home basketball game of the season.
2. At halftime Kayla tells Danny her family is moving away in June. He makes a joke and walks out, as he always does. She follows him.
3. In the parking lot every phone dies, the grid fails street by street, and a ship the size of a city district settles over the city.
4. A small ship lands on the football field. Something very tall crawls out of it and unfolds toward the school (whether it rises onto its hind legs here waits on the owner's answer: cutscenes.md rule 6).
5. Danny and Kayla sneak back inside and down to the basement, where Gus, the night janitor, bars a steel door. Above them, the full building goes quiet.
6. Most of those people are not dead. They are taken, alive, up into the ship.
7. Six survivors hold out in the old fallout shelter under the school. Kayla works the radio. Danny goes up floor by floor for what they need, past a different creature on each floor and the hunter on all of them. Not all of them make it.
8. A rescue helicopter lifts them off the roof and is shot down. Kayla is taken. Danny parachutes into the dark city alone.
9. He knocks out an alien, takes its gun and its ship, flies up to the mothership, frees who he can and puts Kayla in an escape pod.
10. Then he stays behind and holds the self-destruct by hand. The boy who always left is the one who stays.

**The three themes**
1. **Leaving and staying.** Running is the safe move in a horror game, and it is Danny's flaw. Every chapter asks him to stay for someone. Leaving is never punished. It is just remembered.
2. **Counting people.** Ms. Kessler counts her kids. The aliens collect a city; their soldiers spray counts of the taken on every room they clear. The hidden ledger counts what Danny does. The survivors write the names of the taken on the shelter wall. The question underneath: who gets counted, and who does the counting.
3. **Voices.** What the hunter takes from people is their voices. Danny hides his real voice behind impressions and jokes. Kayla's voice on the radio is the one warm sound in the game. The last thing Danny gives away is his own voice, on purpose.

**What "stay behind" means at four points**
1. **Prologue, a joke.** Kayla's news. Danny: "So I'm the one who stays behind. Cool. Love that for me." The player hears the title said about Kayla leaving, as a throwaway line, in the first ten minutes.
2. **Chapter 1, a rule.** Ms. Kessler, as the steel door is barred: "We stay behind this door until help comes." Here staying means hiding. Danny breaks the rule every chapter, because someone has to.
3. **Chapter 5, a gift.** The helicopter has too few seats. Gus stays behind on the roof ("Somebody keeps the lights on"), and in the falling helicopter Kayla straps the only parachute onto Danny: "Go. I'm right behind you." She is the one who stays behind in the wreck.
4. **Chapter 8, a choice.** Danny holds the pod release so the pods can go. At Kayla's pod he tells her "Go. I'm right behind you." It is her line from the helicopter, and this time it is a lie.

The epilogue turns it once more. Now Kayla is the one left behind, alive and carrying him.

**How the title's spoiler becomes a strength** (pattern: Red Dead Redemption 2 tells you early that its hero is dying, so the question becomes what he does with the time left)
- The title is first said as a joke about someone else, so for a while the player hears it as being about Kayla.
- Every chapter offers someone else who could stay behind: Kessler at the door, Ray outside it, Gus on the roof, Kayla in the wreck. The player keeps guessing who.
- In Chapter 3 the hunter marks Danny (the Mark, decided). From then on the player knows he will not get home before he will admit it. What is left to find out is how, for whom, and what he says.
- The last meaning is something the player does: they hold the release, and fight with the other hand to keep holding it. The ending they expected is in their own hands.

## 2. The cast

The people, their ages, where each is chapter by chapter, and their fates are canon 1; this section is their source.

### Danny Reyes, 16
- **Who:** the class clown. He does impressions (his Ms. Kessler is famous: chin in, flat voice, "Mister Reyes. Is something funny?"). He makes jokes at the worst moments, and he is quick, light and kind underneath. He wears a red beanie and a grey hoodie. His mother is a night-shift nurse.
- **The jokes are armour.** Rules for writing them:
  - he jokes hardest when he is most afraid;
  - he names the hunter "Stretch" and keeps it up until Chapter 3;
  - he must be really funny at least four times (the prologue twice, two Chapter 2 nights), or the armour reads as a writer's trick;
  - the jokes get shorter and darker each chapter;
  - in Chapter 3 one joke fails in front of everyone (section 8, line 9);
  - in the city, with nobody answering, his jokes into the dead radio carry the chapter;
  - his last line before the true one is a joke, and it lands (section 4, Chapter 8).
- **His flaw:** he leaves. When something gets hard he makes a joke and walks out of the room, the team or the conversation. Tonight he walks out on Kayla's news. In a survival game leaving is often the smart move, so every chapter tests him with it, and his arc ends when he chooses to stay.
- **What he walked away from before (decided): his father.** His dad left when Danny was twelve, and Danny is afraid he is the same kind of person. The jokes started that year. Staying behind answers the question his whole life asks, and it needs no extra scenes.
- **His face is seen often:** in third-person scenes, and in play in dark windows, the shelter's mirror and the trophy case glass (pattern: Red Dead Redemption 2 keeps its hero's face on screen).
- **What he carries** (the items themselves: canon 10): the red beanie; Marcus's drumstick (from Chapter 2); the AV room's tape camcorder (from Chapter 3), on which he films short clips for Kayla; Kayla's film camera and her walkie, from the wreck (Chapter 6); his notebook of alien symbols, whose handwriting gets worse as the Mark spreads.

### Kayla Brooks, 16
- **To him:** best friend since second grade. They grew up in the same apartment block. Everyone assumes they are together. Neither has said anything, and the game never settles it.
- **Her voice:** dry, fast, amused. She is a planner, the yearbook photographer, and she carries her grandfather's mechanical film camera.
- **What she wants:** to leave this town and see more of the world. Before she goes, she wants Danny to say one true thing without a joke on it. He only does it at the very end, in his own voice.
- **Why she is the one on the radio:** she sprains her ankle on the basement stairs in the prologue, so she cannot climb the floors. She is the best of them at Gus's old blueprints and at the school's camera feeds, so she takes the radio base. She keeps Danny company, gives directions and stops him from going quiet. Her phrase: **"Stay on the line, okay?"**, because he went silent on her once before.
- **The radio rules** (the story owns these; canon 14 carries them):
  - at least half her calls are not about the task in hand;
  - she is wrong once (Chapter 2), and says sorry;
  - she speaks for 6 seconds at most while something is hunting him;
  - she warns him by camera name, and her camera feeds die floor by floor, so her help fades as they climb. Working cameras: basement 6, F1 4, F2 3, F3 none in Chapter 4 (Roll Call's patch restores the antenna and F4's one camera), F4 1;
  - when he is stuck she asks what he sees, then remembers something, then hints strongly (the rungs: canon 11); a code goes onto his map in her blue ink and is never spoken;
  - on the third floor the Mimics walk to any voice, the radio's too, so there he switches her off;
  - after she is taken, the radio gives static only.
- **Kayla in person:** the end of Chapter 1 (the code word), the Chapter 3 night (the Mark), the Mimic floor in Chapter 4 (up the stair Danny propped open), the roof and the helicopter in Chapter 5, and Chapter 8 (woken first, and the only one who runs the last stretch with him).
- **The code word:** "What time?" / "Four." It comes from their standing plan, Sal's at four, and they set it at the end of Chapter 1. A Mimic echoes the question back in the voice it wears and never gives the answer (canon 9). Asking costs: speaking draws Mimics, so on F3 Danny tests figures silently first (a thrown object, the hand mirror round a corner, cold breath) and asks only if he must. Kayla never gives the answer over the radio; on Story it is written on a card in the binder.
- **Her voice is used twice, and never on the radio (decided):** a Mimic copies it once (Chapter 4, "Two Kaylas") and the hunter whispers it once, from a city rooftop (Chapter 6). Nothing ever fakes Kayla on the radio. The radio is always her.

## 3. The aliens: the Voice Collectors (decided)

The owner chose the Voice Collectors. The other two versions of the first edition (the Harvest, the Return) are not
used. Canon 2 carries this section's rules.

**Who they are.** They lost speech so long ago that their language is written light and tone: every glyph has a
tone. They take the living and keep them asleep because a sleeping mind still talks in its own language, and the ship
listens. Each ship is a library of peoples: it fills, moves on, and fills again. This one leaves full at **Tuesday
dawn**; Danny reads this in the stolen ship in Chapter 6, and it gives the last chapters their clock.

**What holds everywhere**
- **The Patrol are the aliens themselves**, in grown armour. They search room by room and take people alive. Their calls are tones from their scanners and armour; they have no speech. They spray counts of the taken on every room they clear (the first numbers Danny learns; canon 12), and in the city they take people in front of him.
- **The hunter is not one of them. It is their collector:** the first people they ever took, bred over ages into a collector and paid in voices. Its own glyph means "the first one taken". Its body, height and habits are canon 8.
  - It takes the ones who hide or resist.
  - What it takes with its own hands it does not hand over (settled yes by the designers). It carries them to its own Nest on the ship and keeps their voices. That is why it whispers in them.
  - Someone in the Nest is too far gone to wake after **about two days**. Marcus and Mari are past it, always. Anyone there less than two days can be woken: Kayla, and Ray, Owen or Gus if the hunter took them. This one rule gives the hunter's catches more weight than the Patrol's, makes rescuing Kayla urgent, and lets the whisper of Marcus and Mari stand for the people truly lost. The hunter scratches its counts only on its Nest wall.
- **The floor creatures are animals brought from other worlds:** the Dark Dwellers, Listeners, Still Ones, Mimics, Swarm and Brute (canon 9). They are let loose as beaters to flush people out of hiding. They do not take; they maul.
- **Taken means alive, asleep and whole** in a frosted cradle, breathing and murmuring. Nothing graphic is ever shown of anyone taken. The Patrol's catches go to **the Holds**, and all of them can be woken: the newest in under a minute; people taken on the first night need a symbol sequence and time.
- **The Mark** (Chapter 3, the theatre stage): the hunter holds a thread of Danny's voice and hears what he hears. It is how it finds him on every floor and in the city, why it breaks into the shelter in Chapter 5, and why he cannot ride a pod ("If I get in one, they follow it"). Black frost-lines on his forearm, cold, spreading.
- **The core** obeys only a command held by a living mind with a hand on it. At the end Danny gives his voice to the ship on purpose, the one thing the collectors never got freely.

**The lithium pulse** (7:56 pm on Friday; the story owns this event, and canon 6 carries the full list of what still
works). When the ship arrives, one pulse goes through the city:
- every lithium battery swells, goes hot and dies: phones, laptops, electronic car keys, e-bikes, modern cameras. Dead phones lie everywhere: the stuff on the floor;
- the grid fails, street by street;
- older chemistry still works: alkaline (AA torches, the school's analogue walkie-talkies, the AA tape camcorder, the AA flash camera), zinc-air (Gus's hearing aid), lead-acid (the generator's starter, the old shortwave, older cars' alarms, the badge reader, the bell timer), NiMH (the crank-charged thermal camera), anything mechanical (Kayla's film camera, the wind-up stopwatch, the crank torch), and whatever the generator feeds;
- alien technology is untouched;
- so light and calls must be earned with scarce batteries, maps are on paper (Gus's blueprints, fire-exit plans), and the rescue comes from outside the city, with its own equipment.

**In play, the idea shows as:**
- the symbol language has sounds: each symbol has a tone, panels answer to tones, and the Patrol's scanners speak them;
- the Holds hum with thousands of sleeping murmurs, the loudest quiet place in the game;
- the Mark is the hunter holding a thread of Danny's voice, so it hears what he hears;
- the core is a living lock that obeys only a command held by a living mind with a hand on it, which is why the pods can go only while a hand holds their release.

**The ending, in this idea:** Danny's last words are given freely, to Kayla and to the ship that listens, before the
hunter's hand closes over his mouth. It ties the stolen voices, the symbol tones, the Mark and the hand-held
self-destruct into one idea, and its sadness belongs to this story. *Easter egg:* in the epilogue, if Danny shared the
notebook, Kayla can read the hunter's symbol. It means "the first one taken".

### Prologue: "Halftime" (length: canon 3)
1. **Black.** A gym crowd heard through a wall; the buzzer. The announcer: "That's the half, folks. Please remain in your seats for the halftime draw." (These words come back as the hunter's first whispers.)
2. **First person: the trophy corridor.** Marcus spins a drumstick. The player can buy a cookie, look in the trophy case (Danny's face in the glass) and read the posters. A drama club poster for "The Masque, 1989" is an Easter egg from the earlier game. Kayla raises her grandfather's film camera at him: the flash opens cutscene 1.
3. **Cutscene 1, "On the Hat":** the flash; over the PA, "Please remain in your seats." Kayla, winding the film on: "My mom took the job. We move in June." Danny jokes ("So I'm the one who stays behind. Cool. Love that for me.") and pushes out into the cold. She follows.
4. Still in cutscene 1, Marcus catches them at the door: "You guys are leaving? It's halftime." He turns back for his board: "You guys'll wait, right?" Danny: "We'll be at the gate." "Swear on the hat." "On the hat." Through the wired glass of the closing door he runs back toward the trophy cases: the last time anyone sees him awake.
5. **Play: the parking lot** (about 4 minutes, walking, banter). Danny tries three jokes. The second one gets a laugh out of her (section 8, line 1). She almost says something real.
6. **The pulse.** Kayla's phone goes hot in her pocket; she throws it and it hisses on the asphalt. The e-bikes by the fence go hot and dark. The streetlights go dark street by street toward them, then the school's windows: the grid is gone. Behind the gym walls the crowd's noise turns to confusion.
7. **Cutscene 2, "Last Lights": the mothership and the small ship.** The clouds light from above. A dark shape, endless, its underside crawling with slow lights, settles over the city without a sound. Two small figures seen from behind; the gym goes silent; Kayla's hand closes on his sleeve. "Okay. That's... a lot of ceiling." The stadium's four light banks die one by one.
8. Still in cutscene 2, the small ship slams onto the football field between them and the gate. Steam, and a landing light flashing on and off: in its flashes, the hunter half out of the open hatch, hood and shoulders out, its long clawed hands flat on the turf (a held pose: the change asked of cutscenes.md scene 2, so the player sees it crawl out), then standing, back turned (its body: canon 8; whether it has risen onto its hind legs here waits on the owner's answer: cutscenes.md rule 6). The movement between the poses, and its unfolding, happen in the dark between the flashes (the staging is cutscenes.md's).
9. **Play: the sneak back** (3 to 4 minutes, with Kayla). Not toward the gate: back toward the school, crouched along the parked cars and under the bleachers, while out on the field the hunter, already standing (it unfolded in the dark of cutscene 2; whether it has risen onto its hind legs waits on the owner's answer: cutscenes.md rule 6), turns its head toward the school. It is the first lesson in crouching and sight lines. Once it turns their way, and they freeze behind a car while it looks: a near miss. It cannot fail the first time. In through the band-room door.
10. **The halls are dark.** The panic is suggested, never shown: running shadows across door glass, a scream cut off by a closing door, the PA crackling "remain calm" and dying. Kayla falls on the basement stairs (the ankle).
11. **Gus** steps out with a torch: "You two. Down. Now." The hunter is heard far off, heavy, then seen once at the end of a hall through wired glass, squeezing through a doorway front limbs first.
12. **Cutscene 3, "The Board" (the last image):** Gus bars the steel door. Through its small window the corridor lights die toward them, and in the last of them Marcus's skateboard rolls in on its own and stops, one wheel still turning. Black. Then, from right behind the door, the announcer's words come back in pieces: "...remain... in your... seats." (the hunter's first whisper, and the only one in a cutscene: canon 8). The title. Back in first person at the black window, Gus: "Away from the glass."

### Chapter 1: "The Shelter" (basement, Dark Dwellers; length: canon 3)
1. Gus's boiler room. The torch, the first batteries, Kayla's ankle strapped up.
2. **Within the first 15 minutes, the near miss at the door** (first person, never a cutscene). Ray, bleeding at the knee, hammers on the shelter door. As Gus opens it, the hunter's hand is flat on the wired glass behind him. Gus pulls Ray in and bars the door; the hand slides away. Survivors keep arriving one by one, and Gus decides whether to open each time: Kessler with Owen and Mari by the stair beside the band room; Priya down the AV cable chase.
3. The fallout shelter from 1962 (yellow-and-black signs, water drums, the old shortwave base). It becomes home; the door is barred behind the last of them.
4. **The generator, "Gus's Ears"** (world 1.1): the right part, bleed, prime, pull, loud enough for the whole basement (the steps: canon 6). Gus's hearing aid is nearly flat, so Danny listens for him: how she fails says what is wrong, and heavy steps answer each pull in the pipes. Lights. The camera monitors flicker on and Kayla takes the radio base. Priya sets up the tape logger on the 1962 base station, so the channel is recorded whenever it is open.
5. **The steam tunnels** to the kitchen undercroft for food. The tunnels are tall enough for the hunter to walk them, and it is heard in the pipes only: heavy steps behind the wall. The first time Danny goes quiet on Kayla, once the thing has gone, she says "Stay on the line, okay?": in Chapter 1 only then, not in her first call (the owner's choice, 22 November).
6. **The power fails.** The Dark Dwellers come with the dark (taught while the hunter is away). The first outage is inside the shelter: everyone counting aloud in the dark while Gus works. Then **Flare Road**, the fuel run from the far fuel store through dark tunnels: choosing which three circuits to light and bridging the gaps with flares, four flares for a route that needs five. On its path, counting three rooms against the Patrol's sprayed tallies teaches the first numbers. Gore: a Dweller burst open on the tunnel floor, found, not caused: a circuit's lights came on over it before Danny arrived. His own light never kills one; it only drives them back.

## 6. The hunter's voices

**Whose words, and in what order, are the story's; how often, how near and how it sounds are the creature and audio
documents' (canon 8).** These rules are **hidden tuning**: they are never stated to the player in dialogue, notes,
captions or tips, so the whisper stays frightening instead of becoming a radar.
- It only whispers.
- It only uses broken pieces of words that someone it took really said earlier, on screen or on the radio.
- The whisper comes from where it really is, and only when it is close to him by walked path (the distance: canon 8): a whisper means it is right here, never over there.
- It whispers rarely (the spacing, and the rule for his name: canon 8). It never repeats a fragment within a chapter. It is silent in cutscenes (except once, in cutscene 3, The Board, where the announcer's words through the steel door are its first whisper), grief scenes, the shelter and the epilogue. Outside chases it falls silent once it has noticed him; in a chase it whispers once, as it closes (canon 8).
- Whoever is on the LEFT list is the voice it uses most from then on.
- After it catches him, on his next attempt it whispers his own cut-off scream back, once.
- The Mimics are not the hunter: they call in a clean voice with no breath (canon 9). Nothing fakes the radio.

(only that one; "...tell her... I said hi..." is cut: the owner's choice, 22 November) |
| from the end of Ch1 | Marcus | "you'll... wait... right" |

## 8. Tone: twelve sample exchanges

## 9. The three songs (original)

- **The camp song** is the seed of all three. Mari's short song on the guitar in the shelter, Chapter 1 nights; she teaches Danny its three chords after Chapter 2; Owen plays it badly after she is gone. Its hook returns in every song below.
- **Song 1, "The Lift"** (Ch5, in the helicopter cabin; the score's voice, or Owen humming if he boarded). *About:* we made it out, look how small home is from up here. Hope. **It is cut off mid-line by the missile.**
- **Song 2, "The Climb"** (Ch7, the calm through cloud, the player still steering). *About:* going back for someone, the moment you stop running. It is Danny's decision, in a song.
- **Song 3, "Stay Behind"** (from the epilogue's gate, then the credits). *About:* the one who is left carrying someone, written from Kayla's side. It is sung softly. More voices join the chorus if more of the group lived; it is sung alone in "Only her".
- **The hold has no music.**
- The lyrics are written fresh; the owner may write them himself, if he wants them truly his. No line from any existing song.

## Decided

- **Why the aliens came:** the Voice Collectors (section 3).
- **Kayla:** the hunter takes her from the wreck; her voice is used twice, by a Mimic once in Chapter 4 and by the hunter's whisper once in Chapter 6, never on the radio.
- **The Mark:** yes, in Chapter 3, on the theatre stage.
- **Marcus:** taken by the hunter at halftime, the first familiar whisper, found lost in the Nest.
- **What Danny walked away from before:** his father.
- **The language:** real but rationed: strong words at about eight peaks, never slurs.
- **The voices:** the voices chosen for the earlier game are the working track; real recordings by the owner and people he knows replace the main cast when they are ready.
- **Where Danny starts again after a death:** close by, progress kept, on Story and Survivor (canon 11).
- **Single-player now;** co-op comes later as its own mode, outside this story.
- **One survivor is always lost** (Mari, in Chapter 3): settled by his own "not all of them make it".
- **The hunter says Danny's name and mocks him** with the stolen voices: settled by his own words.
- **The evening event** is the first home basketball game, taken as read from the opening he picked.
- **Ray** is the assistant basketball coach, taken as read unless he objects.
- **The hunter keeps its own catches** in its Nest, apart from the Patrol's: settled yes by the designers.
