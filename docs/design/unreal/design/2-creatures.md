# Stay Behind: creature and encounter bible (document 2 of 6)

**Second edition, 21 November 2026: agrees with the game map and the canon sheet; where they differ from this document, they win.**

Creature designer's document. The first draft (21 Nov 2026) built on research reports 03 (creatures), 06 (audio,
sections 4.2 to 4.3), 01 (survival, section 8) and the earlier hunter spec (`docs/design/monster/monster-v3-spec.md`,
its numbers carried over by name). This edition applies the game map (`game-map.md`), the owner's twelve answers
("use recomended": the recommended option for every one), the rulings in `drafts/CHANGES-NEEDED.md` and the canon
sheet (`canon.md`). Every number is a starting value for tuning on **Survivor** unless a preset is named, not a
measured fact.

**Order of authority.** The owner's own words (requirements.md sections 7 to 10, owner-checklist.md) > the game map >
CHANGES-NEEDED.md > the canon sheet > this document. `cutscenes.md` is the authority on every cutscene; where this
document mentions one, it only points at it.

**What this document owns, and what it cites.** It owns the hunter and the seven kinds: canon sections 8 and 9. Where
it gives one of those numbers it is the canon value; only this document retunes them, and then the canon sheet is
updated in the same change. Everything else is cited as "canon n" and not copied: people and fates (canon 1), the
aliens (canon 2), chapter minutes and times (canon 3), places and heights (canon 4), Danny's moves and speeds (canon 5,
systems), light and power (canon 6, systems), the noise table, occlusion, every hearing threshold and the "heard at"
distances (canon 7, audio), items (canon 10, systems), saving, restart points and the difficulty presets (canon 11,
systems), the symbol language (canon 12, world), the gun, scanner, flight and self-destruct (canon 13), sound and music
(canon 14, audio) and the build (canon 15, production).

**Units.** Metres and seconds. How loud a sound is where a creature stands, and whether that creature hears it, is
worked out only by canon 7 (the level at 1 m, less path loss over the walked path, less occlusion, against the kind's
threshold). Distances given below as "heard at" are read from canon 7 or worked from its formula, never set here.

**Three things that hold for every creature.**
1. **Tell before touch.** Nothing reaches Danny unless the player could have heard or seen it within the previous 2 s
   (5 s for the hunter). The builder tests this with simulated players (section 4.4).
2. **One rule per kind, said in one sentence**, never changed, only combined with other rules. A twist adds pressure; it
   never rewrites a rule. The kinds' rules are the ones in canon 9 and the map's part 4. The hunter's fairness rules are
   different: they are hidden tuning, never told to the player (1.4).
3. **Patterns, never content.** No creature, look, sound, name or set piece below is taken from the model games. Where a
   pattern came from one, it is named only to say where it was seen.

## 1. The hunter

### 1.1 What it is
The thing that crawls out of the small ship on the football field. The owner chose the Voice Collectors (canon 2): the
aliens lost speech long ago, their language is light and tone, and they keep the living asleep because a sleeping mind
still talks and the ship listens. The hunter is the first people they ever took, bred into a collector and paid in
voices; its own glyph means "the first one taken". It finds people and takes them alive (taken: canon 2), and its own
catches go to **the Nest**, not the Holds (canon 2). It hunts Danny on every floor, in the tunnels, in the city and on the
mothership (owner-checklist line 24), and has one beat in the flight (map).

**The Mark** (Chapter 3, the theatre stage; canon 2): it holds a thread of Danny's voice and hears what he hears. That
is how it finds him, why it breaks into the shelter in Chapter 5 (1.9), and why he cannot ride a pod. Black frost-lines
on his forearm, cold, spreading. The story owns what the Mark means; this document owns only the hunter's part in it.

### 1.2 The body, from the favourite picture
The picture (test picture B3) shows it leaning over a locker row from above, head hanging down, one hand gripping the
locker edge. That pose is its signature and its most-used "seen" beat. The design stays exactly; this turns it into a
real-scale body (canon 8). The body has to frighten before the face is ever seen: the height, the stoop, the hands
(owner-checklist lines 19 to 21).

- **Height about 3.0 m upright**, only in tall spaces (the gym, stairwells, the lobby, the boiler room, the stage,
  outdoors, the mothership). Shoulders only 40 cm wide; chest deep and narrow; legs long and thin, knees high.
- **Face:** about 55 cm long and 18 cm wide at the brow, slim like the body, tapering to a point; pale, slightly
  translucent skin; a deep wrinkled brow; huge black slanted almond eye holes, each with one pinprick of light; two
  dark streaks running down from the eyes; a fine vertical seam from the brow ridge to the tip; a small horizontal
  slit near the tip (the mouth, used only to whisper). The face is rigid like a mask of bone under skin: it never makes
  expressions. Only the tilt of the head and the pinpricks (which narrow when it focuses) show what it is doing.
- **Hood:** reads as heavy grey worn cloth; up close it is a loose fold of its own felted hide. It sits low over the
  brow. In bright light the hood draws forward.
- **Hands:** the picture's hands exactly: big, old, veined, a black web of veins over the back, very long fingers (the
  middle finger about 22 cm). The arms reach below the knees. The hands are its most animated part: they touch
  everything (door frames, locker edges, ceiling grids) before the body follows.
- **The fold.** Its ribs are hinged and its shoulders unlock. It can fold to pass a 70 x 70 cm opening, the size of
  every crawlway and maintenance duct in the school (canon 4). Folding takes 2 s each way, so it is never instant. It
  always happens out of sight: it is heard, as joint creak and cloth, and never seen in play.

**Moving through a school built for people.** Ceiling and door heights: canon 4.
- **Stoop walk** (corridors, classrooms, the tunnels): bent at the hips, neck forward, head carried at about 2.2 m,
  hands sometimes sliding along the ceiling grid. The tunnels are 2.8 to 3.0 m high (canon 4), tall enough for it to
  walk there, never crawl ("the monster walking around the tunnels", owner-checklist line 24). Ceiling tiles lift and
  drop back as its fingers pass: a line of tiles rattling at walking pace is a hunter tell (the Swarm's tile tell on
  F4 is different: bulging and dust, with chitter).
- **Doors:** hand first on the frame, head turned sideways under the lintel, then the body. About 1.5 s; it cannot do
  this silently.
- **Upright walk** (tall spaces): slow, long strides.
- **Its footsteps are heavy and heard from far away, in both walks**, upright and stooped, as the owner asked ("I also
  want his footsteps to be louder so people know when hes around"): about 85 dB at 1 m on hard floors (80 stooped),
  above Danny's loudest possible step (a sprint over broken glass on a metal stair with a full pack, 83: canon 7 and
  14), heard through two walls. The stooped walk adds the ceiling-tile rattle on top of the
  steps; it never replaces them.
- **Crawl** (only where the space is under 1 m high: ducts, crawlways, under the stage; on the ship, its wall
  channels): all four limbs, a soft multi-point patter, never footsteps. The patter is heard nowhere else.
- **Climb and hang:** it climbs lockers, shelving, pipes and stair rails, and hangs head-down from above. It does not
  walk on ceilings (the Dark Dwellers do; that keeps the two apart).
- **Speeds** (canon 8, the same on every preset): creep 1.4 m/s, walk 2.4, **chase 4.2**, a little under Danny's
  sprint (canon 5) and over his walk; **duct crawl 0.45**, well under Danny's crawl (canon 5): in a duct he always
  stays ahead if he keeps moving ("why cant he go thru the small little holes": it follows, slower than him and always
  heard). It is faster only on city roofs (6.0 m/s on all fours) and in the ship's wall channels (1.8 m/s).
- **It never teleports.** When it must be moved to a new area unseen, it travels only through spaces with no line to
  Danny, and the move takes the real walking time (carried over from the earlier spec).

### 1.3 Senses
| Sense | Value (Survivor) |
|---|---|
| Sight, focus cone | 60 degrees: a torch beam or a lit Danny at **20 m**; an unlit moving Danny at 10 m; an unlit still Danny at 6 m |
| Sight, side vision | 150 degrees, motion only, 10 m |
| Breath | Danny's breath fog in a cold room is seen at 3 m |
| Hidden Danny | never seen inside a hiding place it did not watch him enter |
| Hearing | threshold 30 (canon 7): a walk on a hard floor is heard at about 4 m, a sprint at about 25 m (canon 7); a crouch on carpet not at all |
| Blood | follows the drips of a badly hurt Danny for 30 s (canon 5) |
| Light | it does not fear light; light draws its eyes (the beam at 20 m). A lit room protects only against the Dark Dwellers, and a lit, barred shelter (1.4) |

Continuous noise does not pull it straight to Danny. The interaction clock (1.5) is its answer to how loudly he
moves; only a single loud noise above the threshold earns a direct "reply" (a few heavy steps toward the sound), and
only a chase token turns noise into pursuit. This is carried over from the earlier spec, where hearing that counted
twice made it unfair.

### 1.4 Its rules, the same for the whole game (hidden tuning)
These rules keep it fair. They are **never told to the player**: not in dialogue, notes, captions, hints or loading
text (map part 4; owner's map: "The rules that keep it fair are never told to you"). The player may come to feel them;
the game never promises them.
1. **There is always a tell before it can reach Danny**, at least 5 s before any catch. Every arrival is warned.
2. **It never enters a lit shelter whose door is barred.** (The one breach, in Chapter 5, cuts the generator first, so
   the light fails, not the rule: 1.9.)
3. **It cannot see into a hiding place it did not watch him enter.** It can search one if it heard him there.
4. **Fire, cold, loud noise and the gun drive it off for a while; nothing kills it**, the gun included (what each
   does and for how long: canon 6, 10, 11 and 13).
5. **It takes; it does not kill.** Its catches go to the Nest (canon 2).
6. **Its whisper is always where it is**, within about 10 m of walked path (1.6).
7. **It remembers what Danny keeps doing** (1.7), and once a chapter a learned habit breaks the pattern in plain view.
8. **It never teleports** (1.2).

### 1.5 What carries over from the earlier design, with its numbers
The two brains (a director steers it to an area; the creature finds Danny with its own senses), the interaction
clock, strain and backstage, the catalogue, chase tokens and the near miss, all keep their names so the old simulation
targets can be reused as tests.

**The interaction clock** (the owner's cadence: "one every 20 sec or 15 if sprinting and 30 sec if walking and like 1
min if crouching", and "I dont want the monster coming every minute": noticed often, attacks rarely). Mean gaps, the
same on every preset (a preset changes danger, never how often it makes itself felt; canon 8 and 11):

| How Danny moves | Gap (s) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sprint | 14 | a breath-limited sprinter measures 15 to 20 s |
| Walk | 30 | |
| Working (a puzzle, a valve, a lock) | 40 | |
| Crouch | 60 | |
| Still, upright and quiet | 75 | |
| Hiding | 75 | pass-bys and sniffs first |
| In a duct or crawlway | 40 | duct beats only |

**Threshold (Roblox), his pacing reference** (owner-checklist line 32): the rhythm is also checked against it. The
monster-logic research (`docs/design/research/monster.md`, section 1) found no published appearance rhythm for its
entity, only its pattern (noise as the main input, scarce batteries for its warning radar, break line of sight then hide
quietly), which this clock already follows. So before the first slice is judged, the builder times the entity's
appearances in a recorded play-through of Threshold, writes them beside these gaps, and tunes the simulated Walker's
seen-or-heard rate against them; if the two disagree, his own numbers above win and the gap is told to him.

Kept as they were: the threshold drawn between 0.6 and 1.4 of a gap; a lull (gap doubled) every 5 to 7 interactions
so it never feels like a timer; Danny's own loud noises add a lump to the clock (door slam 0.25, debris underfoot 0.35,
generator start 0.5); the first heard beat no sooner than 20 s and the first seen beat no sooner than 40 s after a
stretch starts.

**Seen or heard at least every 60 to 90 s, everywhere it hunts** (owner-checklist line 25: "i never really see the
monster", "make the monster appear more"). On every school floor, in the tunnels, the city and the ship, outside the
shelter, grief scenes and cutscenes, Danny sees or hears the hunter at least every 60 to 90 s. When it is not
simulated near him, the director plays a heard beat from its real off-stage position at that rate (H1 far heavy steps
through two walls, H3 in the pipes, H4 a breath through a wall). It is never closer than 25 m walked and never an
attack. Teach phases keep these far beats only, so the local kind's rule is still learned alone.

**Strain** (how much pressure Danny has just had, 0 to 1): rises 0.04/s while he sees it, 0.06/s while it is within
5 m walked, 0.05/s in a chase; falls 0.02/s after 5 s of nothing. Close beats only below 0.45, seen beats only below
0.65; at 0.8 it goes **backstage** for 25 to 40 s (its clock at half rate, kept at least 25 m walked away) until strain
is 0.35 or less.

**The catalogue, as beats in a 3D school** (tier 0 heard, 1 seen, 2 close):
H1 heavy steps somewhere near (upright or stooped); H1b running steps; H2 a door or locker it touches in the next room;
H3 in the ducts or pipes; H4 a slow breath through a wall; H5 the whisper (1.6: at most one every 2 to 3 minutes); S1
it crosses ahead at a corridor end; S2 it leans out of a doorway or over a locker row (the picture's pose); S3 it stands
watching at the dark end of a corridor; S4 its face at a vent grate; C1 it walks past close while Danny keeps still;
C2 it passes his hiding place; C3 a warned arrival; C4 it sniffs at his hiding place and leaves; F a sound the building
makes that only might be it; Reply; NearMiss.

**The leash:** it keeps 12 to 30 m walked from Danny (Story 18 to 40, Stay Behind 10 to 25; canon 11), never closer
than 5 m outside a close beat or a chase ("just make it stay in a radius").

**Chases** run on a token: one token per 6 minutes of hunter-active play on Survivor (Story 10, Stay Behind 4; canon
11), at most one held, none in a teach phase. A chase trigger without a token becomes the **near miss**: it screeches,
takes two steps at him, and backs away. **No chase or near miss within 90 s of another** (the owner's "never two within
about 60 to 90 s"). Chase rules: it gives up after losing sight for 8 s if Danny is hidden or two rooms away; every
chase route the level offers has at least one exit it did not watch (a door he can bar, a crawlway, a hiding place);
it can catch only within 1.5 m. After a chase or near miss: 25 to 40 s of nothing. The Chapter 5 roof chase is authored
(about 90 s) and needs no token.

**Hiding pays:** hide within 20 s of a beat and a pass-by is owed 12 to 20 s later. A sniff it was not led to by Danny
never ends in a catch.

**Authored stalking sequences.** In Chapters 2 to 4 its simulated share is cut to about 30% (1.9), and what that saves
is spent on two or three authored stalking sequences per chapter, placed by the level. They obey every rule in 1.4.

### 1.6 The whisper
Story owns whose words; audio owns the sound (canon 8). The rules below are hidden tuning, never stated to the player.
- **Only whispers, and only pieces of words people it took really said on screen or on the radio**, cut into new
  broken fragments ("Danny ... you said ... the door"); sometimes his name, sometimes mocking ("say the name sometimes
  or like mock you or something but dont overdo it"). Processing as research 06 section 4.3 (whisper re-synthesis
  keeping the speaker's identity, uneven timing, a second take 40 ms late, a frozen tail), as audio sets it. **Every
  word stays clear enough to understand**: intelligibility is a hard gate ("Make sure he talks good English").
- **Always from its true position, with true occlusion, and only within about 10 m of walked path**, often from right
  behind Danny. A whisper means "it is right here", never "it is over there". It is a warning, never a lure. The Mimics
  are the liars (2.4). **Nothing ever fakes Kayla on the radio.**
- **Budget:** at most one whisper every 2 to 3 minutes (well under one a minute); never the same fragment twice in a
  chapter; his name at most once a chapter; never during a cutscene, a grief scene, in the shelter or in the epilogue,
  **except once, in cutscene 3 (The Board), where the announcer's words through the steel door are its first whisper**.
- **In a chase it whispers once** (owner-checklist line 57: "speak to you like if hes behind you ... or if he is
  chasing you"), as it closes to within about 3 m: one fragment, from right behind Danny's head, counted in the
  2-to-3-minute budget. Outside chases, falling silent stays its tell that it has noticed him.
- **Order of the voices** (canon 8): at the prologue's end (cutscene 3), the announcer's "...remain... in your...
  seats." > in Chapter 1, the announcer's halftime words ("remain... in your... seats") and
  the prologue's strangers, people the player heard alive (story names them) > from the end of Chapter 1, Marcus
  ("you'll... wait... right") > from Chapter 3, Mari > from Chapter 4, whoever the LEFT list adds, heard most > the
  scream-back after a catch (1.8) > **Kayla once, in Chapter 6**, from a rooftop ("stay... on the... line"), while the
  radio on Danny's chest stays dead > last, in the hold, **Danny's own Chapter 1 promise**, through the Mark.
- **Kayla's voice** is used twice in the whole game, never on the radio (owner's decision 3): once by a Mimic in
  Chapter 4 (2.4, "Two Kaylas") and once by the hunter in Chapter 6 (above).
- **Its silence is its worst sound:** outside a chase, once it has noticed Danny, the whispering stops.

### 1.7 Habits it learns (seen in: Alien: Isolation, the stalker that unlocks behaviours)
The game keeps a ledger per save. When a count passes its threshold, one habit unlocks (canon 8):

| Danny keeps doing | Threshold | It learns to |
|---|---|---|
| hiding in lockers | 6 hides | open lockers first when it searches |
| hiding under desks and beds | 6 | sweep low with a hand when it searches |
| using one duct or crawlway | 4 uses | wait at that duct's far end (its face at the grate is shown) |
| throwing distractions | 6 throws | ignore the first throw and look back toward the thrower |
| slamming or barring doors | 5 | test barred doors; a wedged door holds 4 s less |
| scaring it with flares | 2 flares | wait at the edge of the flare's light instead of leaving |
| using the torch a lot | 10 minutes lit per chapter | see a beam from 30 m |

Rules: never learned from a death; at most one new habit per chapter; at most four active at once; Story learns none
of the first three (lockers, desks, ducts). **Each habit is shown once in plain view, and once a chapter a learned habit
breaks the pattern in plain view**, so the player sees it change (it opens the locker next to Danny's, slowly, and
walks on; or it waits in silence at the locker row he has hidden in twice). A habit fades if Danny stops the behaviour
for two whole chapters, so the player can answer it. On the mothership every learned habit is active, up to the cap:
the final exam.

### 1.8 A catch by the hunter (taken), and the gore of each chapter
**The catch** (canon 8; map part 4): the game's signature body-horror scene, in first person, on Danny only. The long
hand comes over his shoulder or from above; the view is lifted off the floor; the face fills the screen with the two
pinpricks narrowing; its long fingers go into his mouth and throat to pull his voice out, the jaw forced open, **the
scream cut off mid-breath**; then he is dragged backwards into the dark, his fingers scraping the floor in view; the
picture cuts to black while the drag sound continues for one second, then silence. **About 6 s, skippable after the
first time**, covered by the cut-away option in the settings. **On the next attempt it whispers that scream back to
him, once** (1.6). Danny then restarts close by, progress kept, on Story and Survivor (canon 11; the owner's decision
2); on Stay Behind only the saves.

**The struggle.** On Survivor its first catch each chapter is a struggle Danny can win even with an empty pocket; on
Story the first catch by anything each chapter; on Stay Behind none (canon 11). With a scare-off in the chest pocket
(a flare, the extinguisher, later the gun) a catch opens a struggle window; the windows, the scare-off lengths and what
a win costs are canon 11's.

**One authored gore tableau per chapter, never a student** (Dead Space-level gore on the creatures, owner-checklist
line 14; the taken stay off screen):

## 2. The seven kinds

The presets are Story, Survivor (recommended) and Stay Behind (open from the start); creature and survival settings
change at any time (canon 11). Every catch by a local kind is a **maul** (canon 9): a graphic first-person death of 3
to 5 s, skippable after the first time, with a setting to cut away early; the Patrol's catch is **taken** instead.
Every catch has a tell in the 2 s before it. Gore is on the creature and on Danny only, never on a student or anyone
taken. All looks and sounds are original, and the owner approves each look (section 5) before it is modelled. The
summary of each kind (rule, senses, tells, counters, tool, catch) is canon 9; the detail is here.

### 2.1 The Dark Dwellers (basement, Chapter 1, when the power fails)
- **Rule:** "They live only in darkness and cannot enter light." In play: keep the generator fed, and when it dies,
  stay in torch, flare, glow-stick or exit-sign light.
- **Look and sound:** flat grey bodies like wet leather sheets about 1.6 m long, hanging in ceiling corners and over
  pipes, edged with 12 to 16 thin finger-limbs; the only face is a vertical slit of small teeth. In light they blister,
  smoke and shrink back into cracks. Danny's own light never kills one; it only drives them back. The one burst open
  in the tunnel (1.8) is found, not caused: the generator's corridor lights came on over it before he arrived (in the
  school nothing can be killed, requirements 8). Far: a wet rustle and a scrape on concrete; near: the rustle quickens and a chorus
  of slow breaths. Caught in a beam they fall silent: **silence in your beam means one is right there.** They never
  click.
- **Senses:** hearing in darkness only, threshold 28 (canon 7): a walk is heard at about 5 m, a sprint at about 32 m
  (canon 7). They smell blood at 15 m and notice any light switched off within 20 m. They cannot enter a lit volume
  (authored light volumes plus the torch cone, whose length and width are canon 6). Ceiling creep 1.0 m/s; a lunge
  from darkness covers 3 m at 6 m/s.
- **Tells:** far: the lights flicker and the generator note sags a minute before the fuel runs out (the coach's
  wind-up stopwatch rings at 60 s left, canon 10); near: the rustle and breathing just outside the beam.
- **Counters and tools:** fuel (the currency; the generator's numbers are canon 6); the switch room's breakers (which
  circuits are lit, canon 6); the torch (AA pairs; the crank torch as the free, noisy fallback); glow sticks and road
  flares (pools, lengths and the flare's effect on the hunter: canon 6); the exit signs on their own sealed batteries.
  This chapter brings the torch, the crank torch, the wind-up stopwatch, flares and glow sticks (canon 9 and 10).
- **Teach:** the first outage happens inside the shelter, with the survivors there: everyone counts aloud in the dark
  while Gus gets a battery lamp on, and one shrivels back into a crack. **Test:** a fuel run through dark storage,
  laying flares as a trail home. **Twist:** Flare Road's choice of which circuits get light (world owns its steps): a
  quiet choice between the route and the shelter.
- **Set pieces:** (1) *The lamp count*: the first outage, the survivors counting aloud as Gus works. (2) *Flare Road*:
  the fuel run through the tunnels to the bus garage with four flares for a route that needs five; the last stretch
  is crossed on glow sticks. (3) *The dark circuit*: Dwellers pressing at the edge of the stretch Danny left dark while
  he works the panel.
- **Catch (maul):** pulled up from the edge of a light pool into the ceiling; the fingers close over his face; blood
  falls past the torch beam. Graphic.
- **With the hunter:** it does not fear light, so "safe" is per creature. When it passes, the Dwellers go silent and
  pull into the cracks: silence in the dark, away from the beam, means the hunter, not safety.
- **Difficulty:** lunge Story 2 m, Survivor 3 m, Stay Behind 3.5 m; the generator burns faster or slower by preset, and
  on Stay Behind also in the shelter (canon 6 and 11).
- **Unfair, and how not:** a screen too dark to read (players raise the brightness and the horror dies): keep a floor
  of readable darkness, wet highlights on their bodies, and exit signs; light-pool edges must look exactly like their
  gameplay edges (dust in the light). They never lunge from behind without 2 s of rustle and breathing first.

### 2.2 The Listeners (first floor, Chapter 2)
- **Rule:** "Blind, they hear everything and go where the sound was." In play: walk on carpet and coats, move during
  heating knocks and rain, throw lures, freeze when the hum stops.
- **Look and sound:** about 2.3 m, eyeless, the skin pulled tight as if the body grew round a skeleton too small; **a
  smooth eyeless head whose skin ripples toward a sound**. A soft breathy hum while they search. They never click.
- **Senses:** hearing only, threshold 18 (canon 7). Worked from canon 7: a walk is heard at about 16 m and a sprint at
  about 100 m; a walk on carpet at about 8 m; a crouch on carpet at about 2 m. One sound at the threshold: the head
  turns toward it (1.5 s). A second within 6 s, or one at 20 above the threshold: they walk to the source at 1.6 m/s.
  At 35 above the threshold: they rush the source at 5.5 m/s. They go to **where the sound was**, not to Danny, and
  they grab only if he is within 1.5 m and makes a sound there.
- **Tells:** far: the hum; **the hum stopping means one has locked on**. Near: the skin of the head ripples toward the
  sound.
- **Counters and tools:** the floor's surfaces (the "stuff on the floor" is the mechanic: carpet, coats on the
  bleachers, tile, the cheer team's halftime confetti, cups and balloons, broken glass that glints in the beam, water;
  levels and the balloon pop: canon 7); the masks (the heating knocks, the waves of rain on the gym roof, the nurse's
  white-noise machine: canon 7); throwables; shutting doors; holding breath within 3 m. This floor brings the
  **kitchen-timer rattle** (built from a timer, a biscuit tin and tape, thrown, whirrs muffled in its tin, then rings; never a click: 5-audio 1), the **air horn** and
  **the first electrical closet**, which powers the office's bell and PA rack (canon 10; the riser key, canon 4). The
  real microphone is never used.
- **Teach:** a Listener behind the glass of a trophy case; glass cracks under Danny's foot and it slams the case.
  **Test:** a debris corridor with two of them and timed heating knocks. **Twist:** the hunter walks onto the floor.
- **Set pieces:** (1) *The trophy case.* (2) *The gym in the rain*: the gym abandoned mid-game, the scoreboard frozen
  at HALF, the floor still covered in the cheer team's halftime leftovers, crossed only in the waves of rain on the
  roof. (3) *Halftime Draw*, the chapter's big puzzle (world owns its steps): three Listeners nest in the gym; chime the
  gym, the breezeway, the commons, then the kitchen from the office's PA rack, each only once the pack has arrived
  (their hum stops), shut them in the kitchen during a heating knock, and cross in the rain. Chime too fast and they
  rush. Kayla is wrong once on this floor and sends Danny into their nest ("I'm sorry. I saw it wrong.").
- **Catch (maul):** pinned to the floor, a held breath on Danny's neck. Graphic.
- **With the hunter:** they hear its heavy steps and rush them; it swats one into the lockers. Its walk is cover,
  its arrival a temptation to run.
- **Difficulty:** every hearing threshold moves by one offset per preset (canon 11); rush speed Story 4.5, Survivor
  5.5, Stay Behind 6.
- **Unfair, and how not:** instant death from one noise (the criticism made of other blind-creature games): never; a
  heard noise turns them and brings them, it never kills by itself. Feedback is consistent: every sound Danny makes
  plays at its true level, so the ear learns the scale; the optional noise ring (on by default on Story only, canon 7)
  shows the same number.

### 2.3 The Still Ones (second floor, Chapter 3)
- **Rule:** "They move only when nothing watches them, and watching needs light." In play: keep them lit and in view,
  walk backwards, film them with the camcorder's red light on, shut doors.
- **Look and sound:** long folded figures of dark bark-like chitin, about 2 m when they stand. **Their faces are shown,
  but always turned away, to the wall or the ceiling**; the tell that one has moved is **which way it faces now**.
  Watched: true silence, a little dust drifting off them. Unwatched: a dry creak, placed exactly where the body is. No
  ticks, no knocks.
- **Senses:** they know whether they are **watched**: at least a quarter of the body's sample points on screen, within
  30 m, not hidden behind anything, **and** lit (room light or Danny's torch), or inside the cone of a camera that is
  recording. On screen but in darkness is not watched. Mirrors never count (taught once, by a changing-room mirror in
  the theatre that fails to hold one). The theatre's fog canister breaks sight (canon 10), so while the haze lasts they
  are free. Unwatched they move toward Danny at 3.0 m/s and kill if within 1.2 m after 0.4 s unwatched.
- **Tells:** far: the creak from out of view, always positioned; a face that has turned since he last looked; near: a
  creak behind him within 5 m means turn now.
- **Counters and tools:** keep them lit and in view; walk backwards along walls; close doors (they cannot turn
  handles). This floor brings **the camcorder**, carried from now on (2 AA and tape; night vision; its microphone hears
  far sounds; how long a pair lasts and how night vision drains it: canon 6). While it records, its cone holds them, in
  the dark only with night vision (canon 6). And **Kayla's eyes**: through the school's cameras (how many work on F2:
  canon 14), a camera Kayla is watching holds the Still Ones in its view. Mari comes along on this floor as a second pair
  of eyes (story).
- **Teach:** one in a lit room of the music suite; Danny leaves and comes back and it has moved two music stands
  closer, its face now turned toward the ceiling. **Test:** a corridor of them under a failing fluorescent light.
  **Twist:** the floor's power strobes, and each dark frame is a step.
- **Set pieces:** (1) *Two stands closer.* (2) *Kayla's eyes*: she holds the west corridor on camera while Danny works
  the music suite's puzzle (matching the choir folders' colours to the stage set, world owns its steps); mid-puzzle
  her camera goes dark (the hunter tore it down) and she has to say so. (3) *The art studio*: a room of sheeted easels
  and sculptures where only some shapes are Still Ones; the camcorder can hold one wall. (4) *Curtain*, the chapter's
  big puzzle (world owns its steps): bringing the stage lights back from the stage manager's cue book so every figure on
  stage stays lit and held while Danny climbs to the fly gallery. The stage is also where the hunter sets the floor's
  trap and leaves the Mark (1.9).
- **Catch (maul):** Danny turns and it is there, its face turned to him for the first and only time, and it folds him
  in. Fast and graphic.
- **With the hunter:** its whisper from behind makes Danny turn his head (twice at most); watching the Still Ones never
  slows the hunter.
- **Difficulty:** Story: they freeze while Danny opens a door, need 0.8 s unwatched, kill range 1.0 m. Survivor: 0.4 s,
  1.2 m. Stay Behind: 0.25 s, 1.4 m, and opening a door is unwatched time (canon 11).
- **Unfair, and how not:** "it got me while I was looking" (so watching is generous: a quarter of the body is enough);
  walking backwards forever (answered by doors whose handle must be looked at, the strobe, and pairs on opposite sides);
  deaths without warning (none spawn unseen within 6 m; every kill needs a creak in the 3 s before).

### 2.4 The Mimics (third floor, Chapter 4)
- **Rule:** "From behind they look like people and call in voices you know; voices draw them." In play: go silent
  (radio off, no questions unless he must), test figures with a thrown object, the hand mirror or cold breath, and
  route around them.
- **Look and sound:** from behind, a person-shaped growth: a hood and "hair" of their own fibrous skin, the head held a
  little too still. From the front there is no face: the whole front is a ribbed hollow like the inside of a shell,
  lined with folds that make the voice. They speak in a **plain, full voice**: whole lines, perfectly clean, no breath
  between words, a faint second voice underneath, the same line with exactly the same timing each time. Their steps
  are slightly too even.
- **Where the voices come from:** voices of people Danny knows (story names whose; the Voice Collectors keep the voices
  of the taken, canon 2). **Kayla's voice is used by a Mimic once only, in "Two Kaylas"** (owner's decision 3). They
  never learn from the radio and never use it: nothing fakes Kayla on the radio.
- **Senses:** they hear at threshold 34 (canon 7) and see like a person, forward only. **They hear any voice within
  3 m (their strike range), a whisper included**; beyond 3 m canon 7 applies. That is why the Two Kaylas figure asks
  "What time?" back. **They walk to any voice they
  hear**, Danny's and the radio's included (Danny's whisper and speech levels, the radio's knob and push-to-talk:
  canon 7). **They move only while out of his torch, and always end up facing away, in his route**, so he routes
  around figures rather than through them. They need Danny within 3 m to strike; they turn in 1 s (the front
  unfolding) and lunge 2 m.
- **Tells:** they always stand facing away; the room tone drops around them; no breath between words; the repeated
  line with the same timing. **They echo the question "What time?" back, in the voice they wear, and never give the
  answer "Four".** They do not flinch at a thrown object. In a cold room a person's breath fogs and theirs does not.
- **Counters and tools:** silence; the code question, "What time?" (Kayla sets it at the shelter; the answer is
  "Four"; asked aloud, it costs noise and draws them); a thrown object (a person flinches, a Mimic does not); **the hand
  mirror** (the yearbook room's picture-day kit, canon 10), to see a figure's front round a corner without turning it;
  cold breath. This floor brings the **flash camera** (the flash shows the front and stuns a Mimic for 3 s; flashes per
  pair of AA: canon 6) and the hand mirror. On F3 the cameras die (canon 14) and the radio is a danger, so Danny
  switches Kayla off and she limps up the stair he propped open to join him (story); Priya's radio earpiece, which keeps
  Kayla silent to creatures, comes with the Chapter 4 return (canon 10).
- **Teach:** a known voice calls from the end of a corridor; when Kayla speaks on the radio, the figure turns toward
  the sound and starts to walk to it: voices draw them, the radio's too. **Test:** Roll Call (below). **Twist:** a real
  survivor too frightened to answer the question; Danny needs another tell (the flinch, the mirror, the breath).
- **Set pieces:** (1) *Two Kaylas* (map): a figure in a yellow rain jacket: "Danny, over here." He whispers "What
  time?" It asks "What time?" back, in her voice. Behind him, the real Kayla: "Four. Why are you asking me... oh."
  (2) *Roll Call*, the chapter's big puzzle (world owns its steps): in the library and the rooms round it, a sign-in
  sheet and the darkroom's contact prints tell Danny who is really still hiding on this floor; one figure among the
  Mimics, the IT technician, is alive and has the badge to patch the roof antenna for the rescue. (3) *The question
  back*: a voice behind a locked classroom door asks "What time?" back, perfectly, and the student who is really there
  says nothing; Danny has to throw something and listen for the flinch.
- **Catch (maul):** it turns, the front opens, and Danny is drawn in. Graphic.
- **With the hunter:** kept apart in sound (1.9): its whisper and their whole voices are never in the same minute.
- **Difficulty:** strike range Story 2 m, Survivor 3 m, Stay Behind 3.5 m (canon 11). On Story the code question and
  its answer are written on a card in the binder; Kayla never says "Four" on the radio, on any preset.
- **Unfair, and how not:** a mimic with no consistent tell is a coin toss (players of other games settle on one
  behaviour that does not match a person as the fair tell): ours have several, and none needs the radio. They never wear
  the body of a taken student: they grow a shape, which keeps the students off screen.

### 2.5 The Swarm (fourth floor, Chapter 5)
- **Rule:** "They follow warmth and movement." In play: go cold and keep still and they pass by; lure them with heat.
- **Look and sound:** finger-length segmented things, translucent, the heat they have taken in showing as a pale glow
  inside them, whiter as they warm; a mass of them brightens as it nears warmth. A granular chitter like rice in a pan,
  moving through the ducts overhead; a dry rush when the mass moves as one.
- **Senses:** body heat within 4 m; movement faster than 0.5 m/s within 6 m; strong heat sources draw them from 15 m;
  hearing threshold 34 (canon 7). They come through vents and ceiling tiles.
- **Tells:** far: the chitter overhead; near: ceiling tiles bulge and dust trickles; the glow rising.
- **Counters and tools:** cold (the biology cold room, the CO2 extinguisher, open windows, the cooler of dry ice, cold
  packs; what each does: canon 10); heat decoys (a kettle, a space heater, the greenhouse left warm); tape over vents;
  standing still. This floor brings the **thermal camera** (sees warmth through ceilings and shows them in the ducts;
  NiMH, charged by the crank; drains fast: canon 6), dry ice, the extinguisher and cold packs. Crafting stays game logic
  only.
- **Damage:** contact stings stack: three stings and Danny is slowed for 20 s; a mass on him starts a struggle; a mass
  on him for 6 s kills.
- **Teach:** a space heater on in a classroom; they pour onto it and ignore Danny. **Test:** a vent corridor crossed
  between cold spots. **Twist:** Danny is wounded and bleeding, so he is warmer; then the breach (1.9) sends the group
  up the stair in the dark and the floor must be crossed again, backwards, against the clock. Owen freezes under the
  Swarm on the stair (a choice, story).
- **Set pieces:** (1) *The heater.* (2) *The cold line*: a corridor where the only path is from window to window.
  (3) *The duct fight*: the Swarm covers the hunter in the duct above Danny; the ceiling sags and splits. (4) *Heat
  Map*, the chapter's big puzzle (world owns its steps): the floor's heating is stuck on full; Danny shuts it zone by
  zone, leaving the greenhouse warm as a decoy.
- **Catch (maul):** covered, stung, dragged down. Graphic.
- **With the hunter:** stillness hides Danny from one and not the other; the heater that draws the Swarm also lights
  him for the hunter.
- **Difficulty:** heat range and time to kill: Story 3 m and 9 s, Survivor 4 m and 6 s, Stay Behind 5 m and 4 s
  (canon 11).
- **Unfair, and how not:** swarms are hard to read: run them as one mass with a few lead bodies (the pattern A Plague Tale's rat swarm showed, 1.10), so the player reads one shape, not hundreds.

### 2.6 The Patrol (the city, Chapter 6, and the ship's decks)
- **Rule:** "Soldiers search room by room with lamps and heat scanners." In play: stay out of the beams, keep metal
  between yourself and the scanner, take them from behind.
- **What they are:** the aliens themselves, in grown armour (canon 2); the hunter is their collector.
- **Look and sound:** about 2.2 m, armour that looks grown rather than made, a cluster of lamps on one shoulder like
  eyes. **They do not speak.** Their calls are **tones from their scanners and armour** (each glyph has a tone, canon
  12), so learning the symbols lets the player follow them: a slow call and answer of tones while searching, a sharp
  tone when suspicious, fast overlapping tones when they have him. Audio owns the tone set.
- **Senses:** lamp cone 50 degrees, 25 m; a scanner pulse every 8 s, 18 m round, that sees heat through walls under 20
  cm thick unless metal is between; hearing threshold 36 (canon 7); a torch beam seen at 30 m (canon 6). They search in
  pairs, room by room, and check the places a person would hide.
- **Tells:** far: the pulse's sound and the tones; their craft's idle hum marks landing pads. Near: lamp light sweeping
  across the gap under a door.
- **Counters and tools:** metal hides heat (cars, dumpsters, vending machines, steel planters); interference blinds the
  scanner (an older car's alarm, set off by a thrown brick, or a live power line: 10 s; the Takedown's substation
  lever: 30 s; which cars still have working alarms: canon 6); cold water; the takedown from behind (once scripted, in
  Takedown, then systemic). This chapter brings **the alien gun**, from the city's midpoint, with very little energy,
  and the stolen **scanner**, which shows warm life through walls but whose chirp they hear (both: canon 13).
- **Horror rule:** **they take people in front of him**: a collar of light, a person lifted away, still calling out.
  Never graphic. And **for one street they lead a stray Listener on a leash**, used as a hound; the stray keeps its own
  rule (2.2).
- **Teach:** the landing: a Patrol searchlight finds Danny within seconds of his parachute landing ("instatnly get
  spotted"), and a scripted chase of about 3 minutes runs over the roofs and down a fire escape to the first hide
  (canon 4). Then, from a hide, he watches them take a person. **Test:** cross a block. **Twist:** he carries the
  scanner.
- **Set pieces:** (1) *The laundromat*: two of them search it room by room while Danny moves dryer to dryer; a boy is
  hiding there from the sweep (a choice, story). (2) *The leash*: one street crossed past a stray Listener and its
  handlers. (3) *Takedown*, at the city's midpoint (world owns its steps): it gets him the gun, the scanner and the key
  shard; the second half of the city is hunted, with six shots. (4) *The landing pad*: the ship, the Patrol and the
  hunter converge.
- **Catch:** **taken**: a collar of light, dragged into the lamp glare, cut to black. With the gun a fight is short;
  what each shot does to a soldier is canon 13 (a quick bolt staggers; a charged shot kills, graphically).
- **With the hunter:** they own the streets, it owns the roofs.
- **Difficulty:** Story: cone 40 degrees and 18 m, a pulse every 12 s. Survivor: as above. Stay Behind: 60 degrees and
  30 m, a pulse every 6 s (canon 11).
- **Unfair, and how not:** unclear vision cones: the lamps are the cones, visible in the air; few and audible beats
  many and silent.

### 2.7 The Brute (the mothership, Chapter 8)
- **Rule:** "Huge and slow, it tears soft hiding places open." In play: hide behind metal or in tight spaces, loop it
  through doors too narrow for it, or keep moving.
- **Look and sound:** about 3.5 m and very broad, hull plating grown into its flesh, veins glowing through the seams.
  Footfalls with energy under 60 Hz that shake the deck, felt in the controller; breath like a bellows; vocal fry; the
  scrape of plates; a tearing sound of its own.
- **Senses:** smell 12 m and a scent trail up to 30 s old; hearing threshold 44 (canon 7); sight 10 m. Walk 1.6 m/s.
  **No charge.** **It is too big for narrow doors**: it cannot pass them, and goes round.
- **Tells:** the deck shakes from far away; **before it tears a hiding place open it taps and sniffs it for 2.5 s**:
  that window is the fairness of its rule. Soft hiding places (membrane pods, cargo nets, resin) look soft; hard ones
  (metal lockers, conduits, spaces under 60 cm) look hard.
- **Counters:** hard or small hiding places; **loops that run through narrow doors**, which it must go round; the
  low-gravity stretch (it is heavy and slow to push off, Danny is light); a charged gun shot staggers it for 3 s
  (canon 13).
- **Teach:** from a safe vent, watch it tear a pod open. **Test:** a hold with soft and hard hiding places. **Twist:**
  the low-gravity stretch.
- **Set pieces:** (1) *The pod hall*: it tears pods while Danny crosses on the hard ones. (2) *The loop*: a ring of
  rooms joined by doors too narrow for it, which Danny circles while a door cycles open. (3) *Zero-weight*: it loses its
  footing in low gravity and tumbles past, still reaching. (4) *The Spine locks*: the first stage of the self-destruct,
  three locks across the low-gravity Spine with the Brute after him (world owns the steps; canon 13).
- **Catch (maul):** torn out of the hiding place. Graphic.
- **With the hunter:** the Brute takes hiding away and the hunter takes running away; they are never both active in
  the same room.
- **Difficulty:** tear window Story 3.5 s, Survivor 2.5 s, Stay Behind 1.8 s (canon 11); smell 9 / 12 / 15 m.
- **Unfair, and how not:** it denies the main verb (hiding), so every Brute space has a clear loop through narrow doors
  and a readable hard spot within 10 m (the old lesson: a slow pursuer works when you can out-route it, not wait it
  out).

kinds pressing at once (canon 9). The decks and their order are canon 4; one alien kit with four deck looks is

## 4. The director: menace across nine and a half hours

### 4.1 One director, one menace budget
One director (a C++ world subsystem with a pure, seeded rules library, as research 03 recommends) runs every creature.
Each chapter loads a configuration asset: which hunter beats are unlocked, the leash, the chase-token period, the
hunter's active share and its authored stalking sequences, which local kind is in which phase (teach, test, twist),
and the habits learned so far. **The hunter and the local kind spend from one strain gauge.** When a local kind has
just pressed Danny (one passed within 3 m, or a lock-on), the hunter's clock runs at half rate for 30 s. That is how
the two never stack (canon 9).

Every authored beat carries an intensity from 1 to 10 (pattern seen in the Dead Space remake's graded events). Rules:
- no two beats of 8 or more within 6 minutes; after each, at least 2 minutes at 4 or less;
- each floor's arc is **teach (intensity 3 to 5, hunter absent) -> test (5 to 7) -> twist (7 to 9)**, in shares of
  about 30%, 40% and 30% of the time on the floor (chapter minutes: canon 3), with the floor's puzzles laid through all
  three;
- one loud stinger at most per 10 minutes outside chases; the best scares are sounds stopping;
- the peak of the whole school is the roof chase; the city drops back to a lower, wider menace after its opening chase;
  the mothership climbs to the game's highest sustained level and ends in the run to the pod bay and the hold.

### 4.2 Quiet stretches, guaranteed
- **The shelter** between chapters: at least 8 minutes with no creature tell of any kind (the pipes silent, Kayla
  clear), except the authored door test after Chapter 4 (map). The breach happens in Chapter 5, during play (1.9).
- **After every chapter climax:** 3 to 5 minutes of release before the next pressure.
- **Grief scenes** (a survivor lost): creatures silent by design.
- **Rooms the player has sealed** (a barred door, a lit room on the Dwellers' map) put the hunter off stage.
- Across the game the hunter is active in about 35 to 40% of play time (the shares in 1.9 weighed by the chapter
  minutes in canon 3); at least a quarter of every chapter has no hunter pressure (far heard beats only, 1.5) and no pressing local kind.

### 4.3 The "not too hard" guarantee (Survivor)
The owner said "I also don't want the game to be too hard". On Survivor, the director promises:
- a careful player (crouches near creatures, uses the floor's tool, hides after a tell) is caught **no more than once
  per chapter on average**, and finishes each chapter within 25% of its target minutes;
- no encounter, large-puzzle stage or solved step needs more than **3 attempts** for 90% of simulated careless
  players;
- after two catches in the same stretch, the director lowers that stretch's danger quietly (leash +5 m, chase tokens
  paused, local thresholds eased by one Story step) until the stretch is passed; never shown, never on Stay Behind;
- a death restarts Danny close by, at the start of that encounter, large-puzzle stage or solved step, with puzzle
  progress kept (canon 11; the owner's decision 2), and the first hunter catch each chapter is a struggle he can win
  (1.8);
- the cadence is never lowered (the owner asked for it by name); only danger moves.

### 4.4 When a creature is "done": numbers a simulated player must hit
The rules run headless in Unreal's automation tests with seeded simulated players (old profiles: Sprinter, Walker,
Croucher, Hider, Mixed; new: Careful, Careless, Backpedaller, Answerer, Statue). They run on the **room-portal graph**
that each Layout JSON exports (path distances, occlusion per portal, light volumes, hide spots), not on a level; the
real navmesh and the rendered "watched" test are checked on a few seeded routes per creature, overnight, and the
graph's numbers are re-checked in the engine at each milestone. Over 200 seeded runs per stretch:

| Creature | Must hold on Survivor |
|---|---|
| All | 100% of catches had a tell in the previous 2 s (hunter 5 s); 0 catches while hidden in a place it did not see Danny enter; Careful caught at most 1 per chapter on average |
| Hunter | measured gaps 15 to 20 s (Sprinter), 27 to 33 s (Walker), 54 to 66 s (Croucher); seen or heard at least every 60 to 90 s on every floor outside the shelter, grief scenes and cutscenes (far heard beats included, 1.5); at most one whisper per chase; at most one chase per 6 minutes of active play; no chase or near miss within 90 s of another; every whisper within about 10 m of walked path; no catch from a sniff not led by Danny; never seen to teleport (no move with a line to Danny that is not walked); a Sprinter always gains on it in a duct |
| Dark Dwellers | Careful with fuel never caught while in a lit volume; a fuel run done with half its flares succeeds 80% of the time |
| Listeners | Careful (carpet, masks) caught under 10% per corridor; Walker caught 40 to 70%; Sprinter over 90%; every catch preceded by a hum-stop at least 1.5 s before |
| Still Ones | Backpedaller cannot clear the strobe corridor without the camcorder or a door (proves the exploit is closed); every kill preceded by a creak within 3 s; no kill while 25% of the body is on screen and lit |
| Mimics | an Answerer (walks to every voice) caught 60%+; a player who keeps the radio on is walked to by every Mimic in hearing; a silent player who tests a figure with a thrown object, the mirror or breath is never caught by the Mimic he tested |
| Swarm | Statue in a cold spot never killed; a mover through warm rooms killed 50%+ |
| Patrol | a player who keeps metal between himself and the pulse is never scanned |
| Brute | the tap-and-sniff window is always at least 2.5 s; every Brute space has a loop through narrow doors that the sim can circle for 60 s |

A creature is done when these hold, the owner has played its chapter, and its look has passed his eye.
