**SPOILERS. This map tells the whole story, the ending included.**

This is the document you approve before anything is built. **Status: decisions answered on 21 November 2026.** Six designers wrote drafts and three reviewers attacked them; every fix is already in here. Nothing you settled on 21 November has changed. Part 10 holds the twelve questions that were yours to answer; on 21 November you chose the recommended option for each. The map was checked line by line against the list of every wish you have stated (152 of them, in `owner-checklist.md`); part 11 shows where the small ones went, and part 12 describes the twelve cutscenes.

## 1. The game in one page

Stay Behind is a single-player, first-person survival horror game for your gaming PC, built in Unreal Engine 5.8: about 9 to 10 hours, in a prologue, eight chapters and an epilogue. You play in first person; the big story moments are third-person cutscenes. The horror follows the Silent Hill 2 remake, the Dead Space remake, Alien: Isolation, Outlast 2 and Amnesia: The Bunker; the story follows Red Dead Redemption 2. We use their patterns, never their content.

**The story.** Friday night, a home basketball game at Rovloc High. At halftime Danny Reyes (16) walks out on bad news from his best friend Kayla, the way he walks out of everything hard. In the parking lot every light dies, a mothership the size of a district settles over the city, and a small ship lands on the football field. Something three metres tall crawls out. Danny and Kayla sneak back inside and down to the basement, where Gus the night janitor bars a steel door. The packed building goes quiet. Most people are not dead. They are taken, alive.

Six survivors hold out in the 1962 fallout shelter with a diesel generator. Kayla, with a sprained ankle, works the radio and the cameras. Danny goes up floor by floor for what they need, past a different creature on each floor and the hunter on all of them. Not all of them make it. A rescue helicopter lifts them off the roof and is shot down. Kayla is taken. Danny parachutes into the dark city, knocks out an alien and takes its gun and ship, flies to the mothership, frees who he can and puts Kayla in a pod. Then he stays behind and holds the self-destruct by hand. The boy who always left is the one who stays. The epilogue is played as Kayla.

**What makes it this game:**
- **The hunter**, your favourite monster: heard and seen often, rarely attacking, whispering in broken pieces of the voices of people it took.
- **Seven other kinds**, each with one rule that changes how you hide and move.
- **Real survival:** a small backpack, scarce batteries, fuel and medicine, and a generator whose hum means home.
- **Puzzles** with their own difficulty, codes that change every playthrough, and an alien symbol language whose final exam is the self-destruct.
- **Quiet choices:** no meter, but the ending remembers who you helped, what you shared and who you left.
- **Graphic gore** for the creatures and Danny's own deaths; what happened to the students stays off screen.
- **A quiet, eerie score** and three original songs.

## 2. The story, chapter by chapter

Time runs from Friday night to Tuesday dawn. Lengths are for a first play on the recommended settings.

### Prologue: "Halftime" (about 22 minutes)
- **What happens:** Kayla photographs Danny with her grandfather's film camera. Marcus, his other best friend, spins a drumstick. Walking out, Kayla says her family moves in June. Danny jokes ("So I'm the one who stays behind. Cool. Love that for me.") and leaves. Marcus runs back for his skateboard: "You guys'll wait, right?" "We'll be at the gate." They don't wait.
- **What you do:** walk, look, joke with Kayla in the parking lot. Then the pulse: phones go hot and die, the streetlights go out street by street, the mothership settles silently, the small ship drops onto the field.
- **New:** a 3 to 4 minute sneak back into the school with Kayla, along the parked cars and under the bleachers, while the hunter, already standing on the field, turns its head toward the school. Your first lesson in crouching and sight lines, with a near miss; it cannot fail the first time.
- **Remembered:** Gus bars the steel door. Through its window the corridor light dies, and in the last of it a skateboard rolls across the floor on its own and stops. Then, from right behind the door, the announcer's words come back in pieces: "...remain... in your... seats." Title.

### Chapter 1: "The Shelter" (basement, Dark Dwellers, about 75 minutes)
- **What happens:** survivors arrive one by one. Early on Ray hammers at the door, and as Gus opens it the hunter's hand is flat on the wired glass behind him. Ms. Kessler: "We stay behind this door until help comes."
- **What you do:** start the dead generator with Gus (the right part, bleed, prime, pull: loud enough for the whole basement); fetch food through tunnels where the hunter is heard in the pipes. Then the power fails.
- **New:** the Dark Dwellers. The torch, crank torch, wind-up stopwatch, flares, glow sticks. Big puzzle: **Flare Road**, a fuel run through dark tunnels, choosing which three circuits to light and bridging the gaps with flares.
- **Remembered:** the first outage inside the shelter, everyone counting aloud in the dark while Gus works; four flares for a route that needs five. Gore: a Dweller burst open on the tunnel floor, where a circuit's lights came on over it before you arrived.
- **Safe room (first night):** the first count and Danny's Kessler impression. Mari plays the camp song, and Danny makes his promise: "I'm getting everyone out. All of you. Which, historically, from me, means nothing. But." The hunter will say it back at the end. Then Owen asks to be walked past the dark tunnel.

### Chapter 2: "Halftime Draw" (first floor, Listeners, about 60 minutes)
- **What happens:** the gym abandoned mid-game: coats on the bleachers, the scoreboard frozen at HALF, HOME 31 GUEST 34, the cheer team's confetti and balloons everywhere. Kayla is wrong once, sending you into the nest: "I'm sorry. I saw it wrong."
- **What you do:** the nurse's office (Gus's hearing-aid cells, Mari's inhaler, Ray's painkillers), Priya's glasses, the office safe, dialled one number per knock of the heating pipes. Big puzzle: **Halftime Draw** (part 6).
- **New:** the Listeners; the kitchen-timer rattle, the air horn, the first electrical closet.
- **Remembered:** the hunter walks onto the floor. Its heavy steps make you want to run, which the Listeners would hear; instead they rush the hunter, and it swats one into the lockers, split open. You use its walk as cover.
- **The turn:** on a camera Priya sees frosted cradles floating out to a small ship on a ribbon of light, a soldier walking beside each. Breath fogs a lid. "They're breathing."
- **Safe room:** Mari teaches Danny the camp song's three chords. Kessler starts deciding who "needs" medicine.

### Chapter 3: "Still Life" (second floor, Still Ones, about 60 minutes)
- **What happens:** the theatre, art studios and music suite. Mari comes along as a second pair of eyes.
- **What you do:** find the camcorder, get Mari's guitar strings, open the way up through the theatre's fly gallery. Big puzzle: **Curtain**, bringing the stage lights back from the stage manager's cue book so every figure on stage stays lit and held while you climb.
- **New:** the Still Ones; the camcorder, carried from now on.
- **Remembered: the Mark.** On stage the hunter's whisper behind Danny makes him turn his head: the floor's trap. It lifts him, presses one long finger to his forearm and puts him down. It takes Mari instead. He hears her call his name once, far off. He wakes hurt, black frost-lines on his arm, and must get home across a live floor in failing light. Gore: a Still One the hunter tore apart in the wings.
- **Safe room:** a joke he can't finish. Owen: "Where's Mari?" "...Yeah." Her strings are still in your pack. Show Kayla the Mark, or hide it. Overhear Kessler hiding the shortwave's news: the helicopter comes at last light on Monday, with five seats. Share the symbol notebook with Priya, or not.

### Chapter 4: "Who's There" (third floor, Mimics, about 60 minutes)
- **What happens:** library, yearbook room, counselling suite. The cameras die. Mimics walk toward any voice, the radio's too, so Danny switches Kayla off and she limps up the stair he propped open to join him.
- **What you do:** go silent; test figures with a thrown object (people flinch) or the hand mirror round a corner. Big puzzle: **Roll Call**: a sign-in sheet and the darkroom's contact prints tell you who is really still hiding here. One, the IT technician, is alive among the Mimics and has the badge to patch the roof antenna for the rescue.
- **New:** the Mimics; the flash camera; the hand mirror.
- **Remembered: Two Kaylas.** A figure in a yellow rain jacket: "Danny, over here." He whispers "What time?" It asks "What time?" back, in her voice. Behind him, the real Kayla: "Four. Why are you asking me... oh." "Something up here's wearing your jacket." "...Is it a good look?" Gore: a Mimic opened like a shell.
- **Safe room:** Ray went out alone for his son's letterman jacket and comes back knocking after dark. Kessler won't open. You can test him through the tunnel hatch and let him in, or not. Gus walks the tunnels with you, telling stories of Walter Hale, the old night watchman. Something tests the steel door, slowly.

### Chapter 5: "Five Seats" (fourth floor and roof, the Swarm, about 65 minutes)
- **What happens:** Danny goes up first to clear the route. The science floor's heating is stuck on full, and the Swarm fills the ceilings.
- **What you do:** **Heat Map**: shut the heating zone by zone, leaving the greenhouse warm as a decoy. Then the breach. "It's in. It's inside, Danny." The hunter followed the Mark, cut the generator, and the group flees up the stair in the dark. The chapter runs backwards: power this floor and release its stair door before they reach it, with the helicopter coming. Owen freezes under the Swarm (a choice).
- **New:** the Swarm; the thermal camera, dry ice, the extinguisher, cold packs.
- **Remembered:** the hunter's one long chase across the roof, a built obstacle run over ducts and skylights (your "door 100" wish), into the helicopter's light. Gus stays: "I don't fly. Somebody keeps the lights on, son." The lift, Song 1, the missile mid-line. In first person, Kayla clips the only parachute onto Danny ("Go. I'm right behind you.") and shoves him out. Falling, he sees the hunter on the wreck lift someone in a yellow jacket.

### Chapter 6: "The City Alone" (about 85 minutes)
- **What happens:** Monday night. Danny lands on a rooftop and a Patrol searchlight finds him in seconds: your "instantly spotted". Three minutes of chase over roofs and down a fire escape to the first hide.
- **What you do:** one route through a few dense blocks. The radio is dead, but you can press to talk, quietly, so his voice and jokes carry the chapter and nobody answers. Side places: Sal's Pizza; his own building, where he writes under his mother's note; a laundromat boy hiding from a sweep (a choice). At the wreck, Kayla's film camera and her walkie: when he presses talk, his own voice comes out of it. He keeps both. At the midpoint **Takedown** (part 6) gets you the gun; the second half is hunted, with six shots.
- **New:** the Patrol, who take people in front of you (a collar of light, a person lifted away, still calling out) and for one street lead a stray Listener on a leash. The gun and the scanner.
- **Remembered:** from a rooftop the hunter whispers Kayla's "stay... on the... line" while the radio on Danny's chest stays dead. Gore: a soldier mauled by strays. In the stolen ship he reads that the mothership leaves at dawn.

### Chapter 7: "The Climb" (about 18 minutes)
Lift off straight up from the landing pad. The hunter's hand is on the canopy for a long minute until you scrape it off against a mast. Then steer, dodge, boost and shoot through a growing pack of craft, reading glyph lanes. In the cloud the pursuers fall away and Song 2 plays while you still steer. Hold the talk button and Danny tells Kayla, who cannot hear, what he never said. You crash into a hangar.

### Chapter 8: "Stay Behind" (the mothership, about 120 minutes)
- **The first hour:** decks around a central shaft. The Brute is new; the others return in their natural roles (Listeners guarding the taken, the Swarm drifting in low gravity). Rows of frosted cradles murmur. The low-gravity Spine (about 15 minutes). The Nest: Marcus and Mari, lips moving with its whispers; Danny puts the drumstick in Marcus's hand.
- **The last hour:** wake Kayla (she tests him: "What time?"; he tells her: "If I get in one, they follow it."), then everyone you can; the woken walk to the pod bay on their own. The self-destruct (part 6). The run to the pods as the ship comes apart, catching Kayla's hand as a deck tilts. Her pod: "Go. I'm right behind you." What she answers depends on what you shared.
- **Remembered: the hold.** One hand holds the pod release; pods launch one by one, a face at each window. The hunter comes with every habit it learned and whispers Danny's own promise back. "Yeah. That one I'm keeping." Your other hand spends your last flare, cell and extinguisher keeping its hand off you. When the pockets are empty its hand closes over his mouth for his voice, but his last words are already out: the Kessler voice, "Miss Brooks. Is something funny?" She laughs, crying. Then, in his own voice, the true thing he never said. White.

### Epilogue: "June" (about 15 minutes, as Kayla)
The city is mending. Gus meets her at the basement door if he lived; if not, his keys hang tagged "for Kayla". She adds Danny's name to the shelter wall and does small chores, as grief. Her film is developed: the last frame is Danny pulling a face. She plays his camcorder clips and the radio tape of his last words, longer the more he stayed on the line. Then the gate. Whoever lived is waiting, and the first of them says "We waited."; she takes their picture. With no one there, she photographs the empty gate anyway. Song 3 begins.

### How your choices shape the ending
The game quietly counts whom you **helped**, what you **shared** (supplies, and three truths: the seats, the Mark, the notebook), whom you **left**, and whether you **stayed on the line** (calls answered in calm moments, night talks, clips you film for her, the climb; switching the radio off never counts against you).

| Ending | When | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone he could | helped and shared a lot | the group helps in the halls; Kayla stays on the radio to the end; a crowd at the gate |
| The line | middling, stayed on the line | two or three freed; they talk until the white |
| The recording | kept the truth to himself | few freed; Kayla learns the truth from the tape |
| Only her | left many behind | only Kayla; no one at the gate; Song 3 sung alone |

Danny stays and Marcus and Mari are lost in every version. No ending is a punishment.

**Length:** prologue 22 minutes, school 320, city 85, flight 18, mothership 120, epilogue 15: **about 9 h 40**, about 8 h 45 on Light puzzles. About 65 of those minutes are in the safe room, so you live with these people before you lose them.

## 3. The people

- **Danny Reyes, 16.** The class clown: impressions (his Ms. Kessler is famous), jokes when most afraid, kind underneath. Red beanie, grey hoodie. He leaves when things get hard; his arc ends when he stays.
- **Kayla Brooks, 16.** His best friend since second grade. Dry, fast, a planner, the yearbook photographer. She wants one true thing from him without a joke on it. "Stay on the line, okay?" Nothing ever fakes her on the radio.
- **Ms. Ruth Kessler, 52,** chemistry teacher. A frightened adult protecting "my kids" by counting them, who starts deciding who matters.
- **Gus Pruitt, 58,** the night janitor from your earlier game. Keys, the generator, the tunnels. The steady one who quietly stops obeying. Whether he lives on the roof depends on you.
- **Priya Nair, 17,** AV club. Certain everyone is dead until she sees the taken breathing; then obsessed with the symbols.
- **Mari Vega, 15,** pep band trumpet, guitar at home, the warmest person in the room. Taken in Chapter 3, every time.
- **Owen Tate, 14.** His older sister was taken. The most frightened. After Mari, he learns her song, badly.
- **Ray Dobbs, 44,** assistant basketball coach. His son Caleb was taken in front of him. Angry; goes out alone in Chapter 4.
- **Already taken:** Marcus Bell, 15, the hunter's first take, and Caleb Dobbs, 17, who can be woken if Ray is there.

Each survivor gives one errand ending in its own scene: Gus's photo of Walter Hale, Priya's tape of the halftime show (Marcus in the frame), Mari's strings, Owen's sister's hoodie, Ray's painkillers, Kessler's roll book.

**Why the aliens came** (decision 1):
- **A. The Harvest.** A long-haul people taking living cargo. The most familiar idea.
- **B. The Voice Collectors (recommended).** They lost speech long ago; their language is light and tone. They keep the living asleep because a sleeping mind still talks, and the ship listens. The hunter was the first people they took, bred into a collector and paid in voices. The Mark is it holding a thread of Danny's voice; the core obeys only a living mind with a hand on it. His last act gives his voice freely.
- **C. The Return.** Something they left under the school long ago called them back. The most twist-shaped; it adds a location.

In every version the soldiers are the aliens, the hunter is their collector, and the floor creatures are animals let loose to flush people out.

## 4. The creatures

### The hunter (everywhere)
As you chose on 21 and 22 November: it walks on all fours on long thin limbs, with a second pair of arms; a bit thicker than picture 2, with its cape of skin and long clawed fingers; alien feet; its face a huge open mouth with torn lips and teeth, no eyes, in a hood of its own skin; sometimes it rises onto its hind legs (when is still to be settled with you). The body has to frighten before you ever see the face. It squeezes through doors and follows you into crawlways built big enough for it; the tunnels are 2.8 to 3 m high so it walks there too.
- **Rule:** it hunts you everywhere by sound, light and sight, and takes whoever it catches. It stays within a radius of you instead of wandering off, so you hear or glimpse it often.
- **Surviving it:** move quietly, break its line of sight, hide where it didn't see you go in, scare it off with fire, noise or cold. It is a little slower than your sprint and slower than you in ducts. It never teleports and can never be killed. Something happens about every 15 to 20 seconds when you sprint, 30 when you walk, 60 when you crouch, and it rarely attacks.
- **Sound:** heavy steps heard from far off, through two walls, whenever it walks, louder than any sound you can make; a soft patter only in ducts. Its whisper uses real words of people it took, sometimes your name, sometimes mocking, often from right behind you, and always close: a whisper means it is right here. It is rare (well under one line a minute) and every word stays clear enough to understand. Outside a chase, when it stops whispering, it has noticed you; in a chase it whispers once, right behind you, as it closes.
- **If it gets you:** its fingers go into Danny's mouth and throat to pull his voice out, the scream cut off mid-breath, then the drag into the dark (about 6 seconds, skippable after the first). Next try, it whispers that scream back once.
- **It learns:** hide in lockers too often and it opens lockers; overuse a duct and it waits at the far end. Once a chapter a habit breaks the pattern in plain view. The rules that keep it fair are never told to you.

### The seven kinds

| Kind | Its rule | How you survive | Sound | If it gets you |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dark Dwellers** (basement) | They live only in darkness and cannot enter light. | Keep the generator fed; stay in torch, flare or exit-sign light. | A wet rustle and slow breathing in the dark; silence in your beam means it is right there. | Pulled up into the ceiling. |
| **Listeners** (first floor) | Blind, they hear everything and go where the sound was. | Walk on carpet and coats, move during heating knocks and rain, throw lures, freeze when the hum stops. | A breathy hum; when it stops, one has locked on. | Pinned to the floor. |
| **Still Ones** (second floor) | They move only when nothing watches them, and watching needs light. | Keep them lit and in view, walk backwards, film them with the camcorder's red light, shut doors. | A dry creak where one moved; watched, silence. Their faces stay turned away, and which way one faces tells you it moved. | Folded in. |
| **Mimics** (third floor) | From behind they look like people and call in voices you know; voices draw them. | Go silent; test with a thrown object, the mirror or cold breath; ask "What time?" only if you must. | A clean voice with no breath, the same line with the same timing. | Its hollow front opens and draws you in. |
| **The Swarm** (fourth floor) | They follow warmth and movement. | Go cold and still: freezers, open windows, dry ice; lure them with heat. | A chitter like rice in a pan, overhead. | Covered, stung, dragged down. |
| **The Patrol** (city) | Soldiers searching room by room with lamps and heat scanners. | Stay out of the beams, keep metal between you and the scanner, take them from behind. | Tones from scanners and armour, sharp when suspicious. | Taken in a collar of light. |
| **The Brute** (mothership) | Huge and slow, it tears soft hiding places open. | Hide behind metal or in tight spaces; loop it through doors too narrow for it. | Deck-shaking steps; it taps and sniffs for 2.5 seconds before it tears. | Torn out of hiding. |

The animals' catches are short, graphic first-person deaths, with a cut-away option in the settings.

## 5. How it plays

- **Moving.** Walk, crouch, crawl, sprint (about 6 seconds of breath), lean, peek under doors, listen at doors, hold your breath.
- **Light and noise** are the two currencies. The torch takes two AA batteries, about 12 to 15 minutes a pair on the recommended setting. The crank torch means you are never stuck in the dark, only louder. Every sound has one loudness number that creatures hear exactly as you do, so your ear learns the scale: carpet is quiet, glass is loud, a balloon pops like a shot. An on-screen noise ring is optional.
- **The backpack:** 8 slots, two chest pockets (a light, and one thing to throw or scare with), and a binder for keys, notes, maps and symbol pages. The world never pauses while you dig. Items that matter: batteries, fuel, bandages, flares, the extinguisher, bottles, the kitchen-timer rattle, the camcorder, later the gun and scanner. Item and key places change each playthrough.
- **The generator:** in Chapter 1 a live clock: every circuit you switch on (lights, heater, cameras, pump, lift) burns fuel faster. Afterwards it is the shelter's reserve, and power you spend upstairs is warmth taken from the survivors. Nothing on your path ever fails for lack of fuel.
- **The survivors:** one request a chapter; the room shows how they are (tins on the shelf, who sits alone); neglect makes them sick and quiet, then gone. One playable moment and one night talk per return.
- **Kayla on the radio:** a volume knob. She warns you by camera name, in six seconds or less while you are hunted, and her cameras die floor by floor. When you are stuck she asks what you see, then remembers something, then hints strongly; a code goes into your map in her blue ink, never spoken. After she is taken, only static.
- **Health and dying:** Fine, Hurt, Badly hurt. Badly hurt, you limp and bleed, and the hunter follows the drips. Healing takes both hands. On the recommended setting the first time the hunter catches you in each chapter is a struggle you can win. When you do die you restart close by, puzzle progress kept (decision 2).
- **Saving** by hand in the shelter's logbook and one small lantern room per area, never with a creature near, plus a chapter autosave. "Quit and keep my place" works anywhere.
- **Difficulty:** Story, Survivor (recommended), Stay Behind (open from the start), with puzzles set apart: Light, Standard, Hard. The hunter's rhythm never changes, only its danger.
- **The gun** (from the city's midpoint): six cells, with a whine that tells you how many are left. A quick shot staggers a soldier or drives the hunter back; a charged one kills a soldier. Every shot is heard a block away. It never kills the hunter.
- **The flight:** a set course; you steer, dodge, boost and shoot. Missiles sound a lock tone first, scraping costs a hit, never a death, and every segment has a checkpoint (except on Stay Behind, which has only your own saves).

## 6. The puzzles

**How they work.** Each chapter has one big puzzle and three or four small ones, almost all played with the floor's creature near. **Light** puts the clue beside the lock, **Standard** in another room, **Hard** makes it oblique and adds one story riddle object per floor, Silent Hill 2 style. Every school lock has a **loud way** round it (pry bar, sledgehammer) that brings the creature. No puzzle needs a resource you can run out of, and at most one per chapter is "find the number on paper".

**From the school: Halftime Draw (Chapter 2, about 20 minutes).** Three Listeners nest in the gym you must cross: loud maple under confetti, cups and balloons. The plan: walk the pack out and shut it in the kitchen. Power the office's bell and PA rack from the floor's electrical closet. Zone stickers on speaker grilles and the athletic director's run sheet ("after the game the kitchen opens for the booster dinner") tell you which button is which room. Chime the gym, then the breezeway, then the commons, then the kitchen, each only once the pack has arrived (their hum stops). Chime too fast and they rush. Close the kitchen doors during a heating knock, then cross in the waves of rain on the roof. The panic button, the scoreboard horn, scatters them everywhere: harder after, never blocked.

**From the city: Takedown (Chapter 6, about 20 minutes).** One soldier guards the small ship; the squad's sprayed sweep counts tell you how long the others are away. Throw the lever in a substation kiosk and the power line hums, blinding the guard's heat scanner for 30 seconds. Set off an old pickup's alarm with a thrown brick to turn him. Close in along cars and steel planters that hide your heat, then the struggle from behind, in first person, in your own hands. Take the gun, the scanner and its key shard. The loud way is the alarm right beside the ship: the squad runs back, and it still works.

**The self-destruct (Chapter 8, about 40 minutes, a checkpoint at every stage except on Stay Behind).**
1. **Three locks across the low-gravity Spine**, the Brute after you: a power board where joining a conduit cuts it (keep the pod bay's line; lose either the gravity or the lights, Chapter 1's choice backwards); three clamps pulled in the order a glyph sentence gives; a lock closed by pushing a drifting pod into its clamp.
2. **Route the pods** by the seat counts on their board, in glyphs.
3. **The final exam at the core:** compose "ship all end". Each right symbol sounds its tone, and the phrase resolves into the Danny-and-Kayla melody. The countdown is in alien numbers; wrong symbols cost time, never the game. The panel answers with one glyph you now know: **hold**.
4. The run to the pods, Kayla's pod and the hold (part 2).

**The symbol language.** Every glyph is three claw strokes, so it can be scratched, sprayed or lit. Numbers come first, tally strokes in base ten, always checkable against a room (the soldiers spray counts of the taken on every room they cleared). Then people, doors, energy, flight and the grammar ("not", "all", "end", "hold"). Danny draws each glyph into his notebook; a page is stamped only when all of it is right, so guessing gets nothing.

**Six Easter eggs.**
1. **The gold heron**, hidden once per chapter. Find all nine and it hangs from Kayla's bag in June.
2. **Your first game:** an old computer in the IT storeroom shows its title screen.
3. **Danny's locker:** a note to Kayla he never gave her. Read it and one of her epilogue lines changes.
4. **The frozen score:** HOME 31 GUEST 34, and the same numbers in glyphs on a trophy tag on the mothership.
5. **Kaylium:** an extra element pencilled onto the periodic table, their joke.
6. **Walter Hale:** the old watchman's name on a 1962 mop bucket; ask Gus.

## 7. The detail: the stuff on the floor

Every room shows three moments: **normal life** (a Friday school day and game night), **the interruption** (lights out, phones hot and dead, everyone at the windows) and **the flight** (lockdown, desks against doors, then nobody), with the creature's trace on top. The taken show only as absence: chairs pushed back one way, a coat on a chair, shoes with the laces tied, drag marks in dust. Never a student's blood.

**The look these rooms keep:** a school after hours, dark and ruined, gray-leaning but with real faded colour in the paint, lockers, wood and brick. No orange or copper wash, no black-and-white checkerboard floors, and one dark look from the school through the city to the ship. Light you can see in the air: the torch beam in dust, gray slits of light at the window edges (the mothership hides the sun until Tuesday dawn), lamps that fail.

You step over dead phones, confetti, popped balloons, raffle tickets, a dropped tuba, a fort of chairs in the library. The floor is also the game: paper rustles, glass glints and crunches, coats are quiet, and a dropped bag points the way its owner ran. The shelter changes every return, down to the June dust.

## 8. Sound, voices and music

The game is mostly quiet. You should remember the **generator's hum** (home; when it winds down, the silence is the loudest thing in the basement), **Kayla through walkie static**, the **hunter's whisper**, Danny's breath, and one sound rule per floor. Every sound is a real recording, some made by you at home on your phone (kitchen sounds for the gore). No wooden clicks, no thin synthetic tones. Every action and object has its own sound, and glass, doors and lockers get the same care as the creatures. In cutscenes the talking stays clear over everything else.

**Voices:** the voices you chose before make the working track now; real recordings can replace them (decision 8). The script is kept to about 1,200 lines so recording stays possible.

**Music:** a detuned piano, a cheap guitar, one voice humming, bowed metal. No music while the hunter is near. Three songs grow from Mari's camp song: **"The Lift"**, cut off by the missile; **"The Climb"**, through the cloud; **"Stay Behind"**, starting at the epilogue's gate, with more voices the more of the group lived. The hold has no music: only breath, the pods, the hunter and the radio.

## 9. How it gets built

**The way of working.** A Claude Code session on your PC (the builder) writes the code, builds and tests; every task leaves a note in the repository, so nothing is lost when a session ends. A second reviewer judges pictures before you see them. About once a week you get a build and three questions. Only you can mark fear, fun or feel as done.

**Your PC:** an RTX 5070 with 12 GB, a Ryzen 5 7500X3D, 16 GB of memory (one stick, three slots free) and about 600 GB free. The look is designed and judged on High, at 1080p or at 1440p with the card's upscaler (DLSS), 60 frames a second; Ultra is for pictures. 16 GB is half what Epic recommends (decision 11).

**The first slice** (6 to 8 weeks): the safe room with Danny, Kayla and two survivors (do the faces read as sixteen?), the generator and its puzzle, a stretch of tunnel, the hunter with its heavy steps, whisper and catch, and one Dark Dweller, timed on its own to learn what each extra creature costs. Three 10-second clips of the hunter moving go to you first. It is done when you say it looks and feels right.

**Then the whole game in grey:** every chapter with plain walls, stand-in creatures on their real rules, working puzzles and rough cutscenes. You play all nine hours, and cuts are made then, while changes are cheap.

|---|---|
| Set-up and the first slice | 1.5 to 2 months |

| The school finished | 3 to 5 months |

These ranges assume your new, larger plan, so little time is lost to usage limits. Earlier I told you one to two years; the reviewers sized the city and the mothership bigger than I had, which puts it in the upper part of that. On 16 GB of memory expect the top end. After the slice the schedule is rewritten from measured speeds. Every update tells you when the next build comes and what is left before it, and pictures of each finished set are sent the day they are ready.

**Higgsfield.** You asked for Higgsfield to make all the graphics it can. It makes pictures, video, voices and 3D models; the live picture is drawn by Unreal. Its jobs here: a design picture for every creature, person and place, which you approve before it is modelled (the way you picked the hunter); if Higgsfield has no credits, a paintover of the grey model is approved instead, so modelling never waits; textures, decals, posters, alien symbol art, skies and backdrops; the menu and loading art. With more credits it could also make 3D models for props and creatures and some cutscene shots. The account holds 1.57 credits (about 13 pictures), and nothing is bought without your yes (decision 10).

**Settings.** A quality menu from Low to Ultra. Ultra holds nothing back; the lower modes are for weaker gaming PCs and never change what Ultra shows. After every cutscene you have control again within two seconds.

**When the whole game is finished:** the full test of every wish on your checklist, the bug sweeps and every change you ask for, on the strongest model available at maximum effort with multi-agent workflows, as you asked, repeated until two rounds find nothing new. Then I tell you it is done.

**Most real:** the places and materials, light in the air with dust in the beam, the sound, the hunter in the dark.

**Hardest, and the plan:**
- **Teenage faces:** Epic's free MetaHuman tool builds adults. Danny and Kayla are tested first; at most ten people have speaking faces, and everyone else is heard, seen from behind or far off.
- **People moving in cutscenes:** twelve scenes staged simply (seated talk, close-ups, darkness), acted with friends on a phone where possible; the risky moments are played in first person.
- **The mothership:** no ready-made alien assets exist, so one alien kit of scanned bone, rock and wet membrane with four deck looks, tried early.
- **The hunter's movement:** it walks on all fours, so its moves are made for its own body rather than taken from human walking; its squeeze into crawlways is always heard.
- **The city on 16 GB:** only the blocks you walk are loaded; in the flight the city is a dark field of lights under cloud.

**What you do yourself:** approve installers; log in to Epic and Fab for a weekly list of free packs; keep the PC awake for long jobs; play each build and record a clip; answer the decisions; collect signed releases if friends take part (a parent signs for anyone under 18); write the song lyrics if you want them truly yours.

**Reused from the old game:** the hunter's look and model pipeline, its behaviour rules and numbers, the school layout as a starting point, 325 recorded sounds, the chosen voices and their pipeline, and the design pictures.

**If it runs long**, cuts go in this order, each put to you first: the joke ending and extra secrets, photo mode, side rooms, extra creature forms on the ship, fewer cutscenes. Never cut: anything you asked for by name.

## 12. The cutscenes

There are twelve, about 17 minutes in all. They look real because of what we show and what we hide. You see faces up close and only for a moment, hands, objects, and big things far away in the dark and the rain. You never see a crowd moving, a fight, anyone climbing or running in full light, or the hunter unfolding: those happen in a cut, a blackout or a flash of light, and the sound tells you what happened. Every line of talking is clear over everything else. Every scene ends on what Danny is looking at, so you are back in control within two seconds.

**1. On the Hat (the prologue, 95 seconds).** Kayla's flash goes off in your face and the camera pulls out of Danny's head. Over the PA: "Please remain in your seats." Kayla tells him she moves in June, he jokes ("So I'm the one who stays behind. Cool. Love that for me.") and walks out. Marcus catches them at the door, swears them to wait "on the hat", and runs back for his skateboard. You watch him go through the wired glass of the closing door, the last time anyone sees him awake. Then you are in the parking lot with Kayla.

**2. Last Lights (the prologue, 110 seconds).** From behind the two of them you see a ship the size of a district slide through the clouds, and the stadium's four floodlights die one by one with a heavy clunk. The packed gym behind them goes silent all at once, and Kayla's hand closes on his sleeve. "Okay. That's... a lot of ceiling." A small ship slams onto the field, and its landing light flashes on and off in the steam. On, it is half out of the open hatch, hood and shoulders out, both long hands flat on the turf. Off. On, it is standing there, back turned. You drop behind a car, and the sneak is yours.

**3. The Board (the title, 50 seconds).** Gus bars the steel door. Through its little window the corridor lights die toward you, and in the last one Marcus's skateboard rolls in on its own and stops, one wheel still turning. Black. Then, from right behind the door, the announcer's voice in broken pieces: "...remain... in your... seats." STAY BEHIND. You are standing at the black window when Gus says "Away from the glass."

**4. First Night (Chapter 1, 120 seconds).** Eight people in one bulb's light in a 1962 shelter: the game's picture of home. Ms. Kessler counts everyone, Danny does her voice back at her ("Is something funny?"), and Owen laughs for the first time since the field. Mari plays the camp song on a cheap guitar, Danny hums along wrong, and then he makes his promise: "I'm getting everyone out. All of you. Which, historically, from me, means nothing. But." Kayla looks down, because she is the only one who knows he means his dad. The camera drifts up to an air grille where dust is shaking; you will understand why at the very end.

**5. Where's Mari (Chapter 3, 70 seconds).** Danny comes home hurt, a silhouette against the red exit sign, and starts a joke he can't finish. Owen asks "Where's Mari?" and Danny just says "...Yeah." and sits down on her empty cot. When the steel door slams, her guitar on the nail rings an out-of-tune chord nobody played. You get control sitting on her cot, his sleeve pulled over the black marks.

**6. Knock (Chapter 4, 85 seconds).** Ray is outside the steel door after dark, and you never see him. Three knocks. Then the same three knocks, with exactly the same timing, which on the floor you just survived means a Mimic. Then one tired knock, low on the door, and "...Please." Kessler won't open it ("I count my kids, Mr. Reyes. I can't count him."), and Gus silently shines his torch on the tunnel hatch. Going round to check is up to you.

**7. Somebody Keeps the Lights On (Chapter 5, 110 seconds).** On the roof the hunter stands in the helicopter's searchlight, and for half a second its face, the huge open mouth, turns into the light before the beam flares and it's gone. The others run into the white one by one. Gus stays: "Somebody keeps the lights on, son. Go on." If you brought him his hearing-aid batteries back in Chapter 2, he hears it coming, strikes a red flare and walks off into the rain. If you didn't, he waves his torch up at you while the long pale face waits behind him at the stair door, and he never turns around. You get control at the helicopter door as the roof drops away.

**8. It's Me. Again. (Chapter 6, 55 seconds).** All night you have been talking into a dead radio. At the crashed helicopter you press talk one more time, and your own voice comes out of the wreck, from Kayla's walkie lying in rainwater, blinking red next to her film camera. "...Cool. So I've been talking to a seat." You keep both and walk on.

**9. What Time? (Chapter 8, 95 seconds).** You wipe the frost off Kayla's cradle and her breath fogs the glass. Her eyes open, and the first thing she says is the test you used on the Mimics: "What time?" "Four." She sees how far the Mark has spread, he tells her the pods would follow him, and she says the only line in the game about his father: "You're not your dad, Danny." Behind them, Marcus's and Mari's lips stop moving at the same moment, which means it has noticed you. You get control kneeling there, with the others still to wake.

**10. Right Behind You (Chapter 8, 100 seconds).** Kayla is strapped in her pod and Danny says the lie she once told him: "Go. I'm right behind you." "You're lying. You do the thing with your mouth." "I don't have a thing." "You have a thing, Danny." Their hands go flat on either side of the glass and don't line up. The lid seals and cuts her voice off, frost covers her face, and a click later the same voice is clear in your ear on the radio: "Stay on the line, okay?" Heavy steps come up through the floor, and your hand goes to the release: the hold is yours.

**11. Seeds (after the hold, 45 seconds).** The white thins into the view from Kayla's pod at dawn. Pods drift out of the dying ship like seeds off a dandelion, one for every person you freed. The ship breaks in total silence, the thunder arrives seconds later, and the walkie in Kayla's hand stops hissing. The sky turns to June and you are Kayla.

**12. We Waited (the epilogue, 75 seconds).** Kayla walks to the gate where they promised to wait. Whoever lived is standing on the other side, and the first of them says, tired and flat, "We waited." She laughs and cries at once, raises her film camera and the flash goes off, the same click that started the game. Song 3 begins on one chord of Mari's old guitar, and you walk through the gate as the credits roll. If no one is there, she takes a picture of the empty gate anyway.

**What stays in your own eyes.** The scariest moments are never cutscenes, because fear only works in your own hands: the hunter's hand on the shelter window, "They're breathing" on the security camera, the Mark, the Mimic in Kayla's jacket, the breach, the lift and the missile, Kayla clipping the parachute to you, the drumstick in Marcus's hand, and the hold. Light and sound pull your eyes to the right place, but nobody ever turns your head for you.
