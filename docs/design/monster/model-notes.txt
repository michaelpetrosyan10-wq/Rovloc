WHAT WAS BUILT
--------------
One skinned character for the website, made entirely by scripts in tools/art/creature/ (README.md there), in the
rig's own rest pose and measures. Its skeleton IS the rig: 41 bones named and placed exactly as CreatureRigBuilder's
parts (HumanoidRootPart, LowerTorso, UpperTorso, Head, Jaw, Hair1_1..Hair7_2, Left/Right UpperArm, LowerArm, Hand,
Index, Middle, Ring, Pinky, Thumb, UpperLeg, LowerLeg, Foot), pivots and rest frames read from web/build/world.json
(the rig's export). The animator (profile.js on animator.js) that drives the block rig drives this mesh unchanged:
finger curls move the finger bones, the head turns on the Neck joint at the skull's base, the Jaw moves the face's
point below the slit and parts it a little. Nothing gameplay reads was touched (height 11 / 9 hunched, hitboxes, eye
heights, reach, speeds, senses, the Luau rig, its config and tests): the website only DRAWS differently.

Pipeline (Blender as a Python module, the scratchpad's bpyenv; tools/art/creature/run.sh runs each step):
  body.py      MakeHuman base mesh (MPFB2, CC0) with macros for an old, very thin man and its game_engine skin
               weights; every MakeHuman bone mapped onto a segment of the rig at rest and the mesh warped onto it by
               linear blend skinning (stretched along the rig's segments, thinned across): arms to below the knees,
               long legs, bare feet; fingers 0.7 of the rig's 1.3-stud finger length with the knuckles spread (a big
               old hand, about 1.45 studs wrist to fingertip, longer than the head); head and neck cut away.
  head.py      the head and neck as an implicit surface in the rig's face frame, lofted from outline / profile /
               depth / cross-section tables read off the favourite picture (B3): the long narrow face tapering to a
               point far below a chin (0.38 wide at the eye holes, about 2.7 x as long as wide), the almond eye holes
               slanted and bored in, the furrowed brow, the faint ridge where a nose would be with hollows beside it,
               the small slit near the point, a small skull narrowing behind, small flat ears low and far back, the
               long thin neck swept up into the skull from below and behind; marching cubes (PyMCubes), smoothed.
  assemble.py  + cloth.py: the remains of a grey hoodie (split seams, sleeves torn at the elbows, hem above the ribs,
               collar, drawstrings, the torn hood hanging behind the neck, never on the head) and faded jeans to a
               frayed mid-shin, burst at the left thigh, as separate skinned shells; the thin matted hair cap and
               strands; skin weights; regions; one UV atlas (face 2.6x texel density, hands 1.7x). The MASTER: every
               piece subdivided twice and displaced by the modelled relief (ribs, collarbones, spine knobs, hip
               points, forearm tendons, knuckles and their creases, nails, veins, brow furrows, cloth folds), then
               decimated to the Max budget; High, Medium, Low and Potato are decimated from that same master (same
               silhouette, same atlas).
  texture.py   the maps from the master, procedural and ours: albedo (Cycles-baked ambient occlusion in it; alpha =
               how thin the flesh is, from a Cycles inside bake, for light bleeding through ears and fingers), normal
               + roughness (vs the smoothed normals, the lower levels) and normalFine (vs the master's own normals,
               Max). Skin: grey-ivory, mottled, age spots, veins as meandering lines of even width (most on hands,
               forearms, neck), pores and fine creases; the face old ivory gone leathery (fine crackle, hollows
               greyer), the fine wavy brow furrows, the crease between the brows, the seam down the ridge, the two dark
               dry streaks from the eye holes, the glossy black eye holes, the slit; hair strands; cotton knit, denim
               twill, dirt, fray.
  export.py    web/art/creature: creature-{max,high,medium,low,potato}.bin.gz (packed skinned geometry), the maps as
               webp at 4096/2048/1024/512 (normalFine 4096), creature.json (bones, levels, maps, face features,
               fingertip smoke anchors). --glb <dir> also writes glTF binaries on an armature of the rig's parts.
  turntable.py, preview.py, sheet.py, shots.sh   the proof pictures (below).
Full rebuild: tools/art/creature/fetch.sh once, then tools/art/creature/build.sh (body -> head -> assemble ->
texture -> export). On this machine: assemble ~2.5 min, head ~3 min, texture (4096) ~13-15 min.

  (a) far: reads as a tall pale thin figure at the end of the corridor; at that distance the face is a pale wedge and
      the hoodie / jeans read as blocks of grey and blue. Fine for its job, nothing to admire.
  (b) lean: the strongest pose we have that is like the favourite, but it is the Lunge's bow (the catch's pose
      without its camera), not the favourite's own moment: in the picture he hangs head-down out of the dark from
      above with ONE hand on the lockers; the animator has no "lean round a door frame, hand on the frame" pose, and
      ReachIn turns the head to the hand's target, so a hand on the frame turned the face away. The corridor stays
      fairly lit round him (the stage's lit probe point), so he does not come "out of the dark" as in the picture.
      His bare pale chest under the hoodie's high hem shows as a pale mass behind the head. The arm veins read a
      little like painted squiggles at this distance.
  (c) hunch and (e) reach: small in frame (the shot tool's standard camera); the body reads, the detail does not.
  (d) vent crawl: the limbs splay up like a spider's; that is the rig's pose; the skin of the long arms is smooth
      tubes between the joints at this distance.
  (f) catch: the closest to the favourite: the face, the eye holes with the pinprick deep in each, the furrows, the
      streaks, the seam, the point. Weaker than the picture: the skin is cleaner and more evenly lit (no leathery
      sheen breaking up in the torch), the furrows are more regular than the picture's, and the face reads a shade
      too white and plastic in the torch's full light.
  Turntable: the neck is very long and thick as the rig dictates (the favourite never shows it); the head is small on
      it; the jeans read clean. Hands are the best part of the body (knuckles, nails, veins, age spots).
  Potato: same silhouette and pose in every stage; flat-lit, the face's detail only in the albedo, the hands paddle-
      like at 4k triangles.

WHAT THE ROBLOX PORT WILL NEED
  - export.py --glb <dir>: each level as a glTF binary on an armature whose bones are the rig's parts (bone heads at
    the Motor6D pivots, the rig's axes), weights up to four bones per vertex. Import as a MeshPart with Bones named
    after the rig's parts, posed from the same Motor6D transforms each frame (Bone.Transform = the Motor6D's
    Transform), or as an R15-style skinned mesh driven by the existing Motor6Ds.
  - Roblox's mesh import caps a mesh at about 20k triangles and SurfaceAppearance maps at 1024: the medium (20k)
    level is the most Roblox can take, low/potato for weaker devices; ColorMap / NormalMap / RoughnessMap at 1024
    from the same atlas (our normal map's blue channel is roughness: split it into the two maps; the alpha in our albedo is thinness, not opacity: strip it or route it to a separate
    map; Roblox would read it as transparency).
  - The eye holes' pinpricks and depth, the light through thin flesh, the catch's marks and the dissolve are shader
    work on the website; on Roblox: a small Neon part or Attachment light in each eye hole, a decal swap for the
    catch's creases, and the per-part dissolve already described in docs/MONSTER-LOOK.md.
  - Keep the hitboxes and the Luau rig as they are: the mesh rides them, it never replaces them.
