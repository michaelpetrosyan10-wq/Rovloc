Dark, underexposed horror film still, real 35mm photograph, night in an American high school corridor: steel lockers along both walls, ceiling tiles, one flickering fluorescent tube far down the corridor behind him, cold moonlight from a classroom door window on the left, haze; the rest is nearly black. Three-quarter shot from his knees to his head, the camera two steps away at the height of his hips, looking up at an impossibly tall, gaunt figure, nearly four metres tall, the tops of the lockers level with his hips, turned three-quarters to the camera, his long neck bent forward so that his small head fits under the ceiling tiles and looks down into the lens. Long evening-shadow proportions: narrow shoulders, a long narrow starved torso, a long thin neck with tendons, a tiny narrow head. He was a young man of 18: living pale grey mottled skin tight over every rib, the collarbones and the hip bones, a concave belly, no fat and little muscle. The remains of his clothes are far too small: a grey hooded sweatshirt with white drawstrings burst at the seams, covering only his shoulders and upper chest, its sleeves ending at the elbows; blue jeans split at the side seams. The porcelain of his face is spreading over him: glossy old ivory glaze, crazed with a net of fine dark cracks like an old china plate, has grown from the mask down the side of his neck, across the near shoulder through the torn sweatshirt and down that whole long arm, which hangs close to the lens, to the tips of its very long thin fingers; the edge of the glaze feathers into the living skin like frost on a window, and islands of it have started on his ribs. The glazed arm shines in the moonlight; the other arm is dull skin in shadow. His face is a slim mask of the same cracked ivory porcelain covering only the front of his face, with thin lank dark hair hanging behind it: only half as wide as it is long, a little too long, slightly lopsided, hollow sunken cheeks under sharp cheekbones, a pointed chin, a sculpted brow ridge with no eyebrows, deep drooping almond eye holes that are pure black with a tiny pale pinprick of light far inside each, the right one slightly larger, a thin nose ridge, a thin closed mouth slit, almost straight, with only its right corner curled slightly up, one long crack from the brow through the left eye hole down to the jaw with hairline cracks off it, a chip missing at the right temple, two dark tear streaks under each eye hole. Expressionless and starved. He is perfectly still. The moonlight grazes the glazed arm and half of the mask; the rest of him is a dark shape with a faint cold edge from the far tube. 28mm lens, film grain, deep black shadows, muted cold colour, photographic realism, real skin, real cloth, real glazed ceramic. No blood, no wounds, clean hands.
