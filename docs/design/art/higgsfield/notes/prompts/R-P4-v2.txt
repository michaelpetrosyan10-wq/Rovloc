Dark, underexposed horror film still, real 35mm photograph, night in an American high school with the power out: the open doorway of a dark classroom, its painted steel door frame, a corridor of lockers fading into black. Camera low, at a crouching person's chest height, an arm's length from the door frame, looking up. An impossibly tall, gaunt figure, nearly four metres tall, is leaning out round the door frame from the black classroom toward the lens, bent down from high above: one huge old veined hand with very long knotted fingers and plain nails curls round the edge of the door frame close to the camera, sharp in the foreground; above it his long bare neck, with tendons, bends round the frame, and his small narrow bare head hangs over the lens, seen from below in three-quarter, cocked to one side. Thin flat dark hair on the narrow skull, nothing covering his head. His face is not a human face: a very long, very narrow, smooth pale face of old ivory gone leathery, with no nose, tapering down to a point far below where a chin should be, a wrinkled brow, two huge black slanted eye holes with one tiny pinprick of pale light deep in each, two dark streaks running down from them, a small slit of a mouth near the tip. A bony bare shoulder and the torn grey cotton of a split hoodie behind the frame; pale grey veined skin. A far emergency light down the corridor behind him gives a thin cold edge of light on the knuckles, the neck and the long point of the face; the rest of him is lost in black. Expressionless, starved, looking straight down into the lens from very near. Perfectly still. 28mm lens, shallow focus on the face, film grain, deep black shadows, muted cold colour, photographic realism, real skin, a real place. Not a puppet, not a costume, not a bird, not an alien. No blood, no wounds.
