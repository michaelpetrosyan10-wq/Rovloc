Macro photograph at ground level, real 35mm film, grey overcast daylight, the wet asphalt of a back lot behind a school, the dented green steel side of a dumpster soft in the background. A small unknown creature, hand-sized, the size of a curled-up kitten, is caught in the middle of a clumsy little frog hop, a hand's width above the asphalt, tilted crooked in the air, its soft body stretched by the jump, its small dark reflection and shadow on the wet ground below it, a few water drops flying. Its body is one simple round ball of pale cream down, soft and slightly damp like a duckling's, with no tail, no ears and no beak. Its face is not an animal's face: set into the front of the down is a tiny human doll's face made of hard glossy glazed porcelain, old ivory, without a single crack, no wider than a jam-jar lid, a little narrow and long, with two big dark oval eyes, a thin nose ridge and a tiny painted smile whose right corner curls up. The little porcelain face is turned to the camera. Under the ball, where legs should be, the down parts over long pale fingers folded together like a closed fist, and it has pushed off with their tips. For a moment it is simply cute, small and clumsy. In front of it, at the edge of the frame and out of focus, the toe of a white sneaker with white laces. 100mm macro lens, very shallow depth of field, a little motion blur on the down, soft flat light, film grain, muted natural colour, a real living thing in a real place, real down, real glazed ceramic. Not a toy, not a plush, no stitching, no plastic. No blood, no wounds.
