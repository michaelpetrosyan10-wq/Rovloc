Dark, underexposed horror film still, real 35mm photograph, night in a long empty American high school corridor with the power out: steel lockers on both walls, a waxed floor, ceiling tiles, haze in the air. One fluorescent tube still burns at the far end of the corridor, directly behind him; nothing lights him from the front. Low camera close to the floor, fifteen steps away, looking up. In the middle of the corridor stands an impossibly tall, gaunt figure, nearly four metres tall, whole from head to bare feet: the lockers reach only to his hips, his narrow bony shoulders are pressed under the ceiling tiles, his upper back is bent and his head hangs forward and down from a neck as long as a forearm and as thick as a wrist, cocked to one side. He is in proportion like a very tall starved man: long narrow torso, long thin legs, and arms hanging straight down so long that his big old veined hands with long knuckled fingers hang below his knees. A few weeks ago he was a high-school senior of 18. His head is narrow and long, with thin dark hair flat on the skull; his face is a very long, very narrow pale face of old ivory gone leathery, tapering down to a point far below where a chin should be, a wrinkled brow, two huge black slanted eye holes, two dark streaks running down from them, a small slit of a mouth near the tip: the only pale thing in the frame, looking straight at the camera. Pale grey veined skin tight over bone; what is left of a grey hoodie split along the seams, sleeves stopped at the elbows; faded jeans ending at mid-shin, burst at the side seams. He is a dark silhouette against the haze with a thin hard cold edge of light along his skull, shoulders, arms and fingers; the tube flares behind his shoulder; his long shadow runs down the floor to the lens. Perfect stillness. 24mm lens, film grain, deep black shadows, muted cold colour, photographic realism, a real location, real skin and cloth. Not a puppet, not a costume, not a skeleton, not a bird. No blood, no wounds, no hat.
