Dark, underexposed horror film still, real 35mm photograph, late dusk, the empty back lot behind an American high school: wet cracked asphalt with puddles, a chain-link fence, two dented green steel dumpsters, a bike rack with one bicycle fallen on its side, a brick wall with steel staff doors, the last deep blue light going out of the sky. One sodium yard lamp on a tall pole burns behind and above the figure; nothing lights him from the front. Wide shot from a low camera, twenty steps away. Standing in the middle of the lot between the camera and the lamp, full body in frame from bare feet to head, is an impossibly tall, gaunt figure: nearly four metres tall, twice the height of the dumpsters and the fence beside him. His proportions are those of a long evening shadow: twelve heads tall, a tiny narrow head on a long thin neck, narrow shoulders, arms so long that the fingertips hang below his knees, very long thin fingers. He was a young man of 18: a real living body with weight on one leg, one shoulder lower than the other, the small head cocked to one side, pale grey mottled skin tight over ribs, collarbones and hip bones, thin ropy muscle and tendons. The remains of his clothes are far too small: a grey hooded sweatshirt with white drawstrings burst at the seams, its sleeves ending at the elbows, its hem at the lower ribs; long blue jeans that now end at mid-shin, split at the side seams. The porcelain of his face is spreading over him: glossy old ivory glaze, crazed with a net of fine dark cracks like an old china plate, has grown from the mask down the side of his neck, across one shoulder through the torn sweatshirt and down that whole arm to the fingertips, so that arm glints like wet cracked china under the lamp while the other arm is dull living skin; islands of the same glaze have started on his ribs and one shin. His face is a small slim mask of the same cracked ivory porcelain, narrow and gaunt, hollow cheeks, a pointed chin, two drooping almond eye holes that are pure black with only a pinprick of pale light deep inside each, a thin straight closed mouth slit, a long crack down through the left eye hole, dark tear streaks, turned to the camera. He is almost a silhouette against the lamp glow and the wet ground, with small hard glints on the glazed arm and the edge of the mask, and his very long thin shadow lies across the asphalt toward the camera. On the asphalt in the foreground lie a red knitted beanie and a pair of empty white sneakers with the laces tied. He is perfectly still. 28mm lens, film grain, deep black shadows, muted colour, photographic realism, a real location, real skin, real cloth, real glazed ceramic. No blood, no wounds, clean hands.
