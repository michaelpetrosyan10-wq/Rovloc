Wildlife macro photograph, real 35mm film, grey overcast daylight, the wet asphalt of a back lot behind a school, the dented green steel side of a dumpster soft in the background, small puddles. On the asphalt sits a small living animal nobody has seen before, hand-sized, the size of a curled-up kitten: one simple round ball of pale cream down, soft and slightly damp like a duckling's, breathing, with no tail and no ears. On its front it has a tiny pretty porcelain face no wider than a jam-jar lid: smooth glossy old ivory glaze without a single crack, a little narrow and long for a doll, two big dark oval eyes, a thin nose ridge, a tiny painted smile whose right corner curls up. It has tipped its face up and to one side like a curious puppy and is looking straight at the camera. It is clumsy: it has just landed from a hop and sits a little crooked. For a moment it is simply cute. On a second look, the bottom of the round body is not a body: under the down are long pale fingers folded tightly over one another like a closed fist, the down only fuzz on the knuckles, and the tips of two pale fingers rest on the wet asphalt like little feet. Its reflection lies in the wet asphalt; tiny water drops sit on the down. Camera at ground level, 100mm macro lens, very shallow depth of field, soft flat light, film grain, muted natural colour, a real animal in a real place, real down, real glazed ceramic, real skin on the fingers. Not a toy, not a doll, no stitching, no plastic. No blood, no wounds.
