Dark, underexposed horror film still, real 35mm photograph. A long American high school corridor at night, rows of steel lockers on both walls, a linoleum floor with a wet shine, ceiling tiles, one flickering fluorescent tube at the far end; everything else is nearly black, haze in the air. The camera sits low near the floor. Fifteen steps away, backlit by the far light, stands a figure, full body in frame: a boy of 18 whose living body has stretched to nearly four metres, so tall that the lockers reach only to his hips and his head is bent a little under the ceiling. He is as thin as a starved man: narrow shoulders, a long thin neck, arms so long that the fingertips hang below his knees, very long thin fingers, long thin legs, bare feet. His head has not grown with him: it is the size of an ordinary human head, small and narrow at the top of that huge thin body, thin dark hair flat on the skull, tilted slightly to one side. His face is a small slim mask of cracked old ivory porcelain: narrow and gaunt with hollow cheeks and a pointed chin, two black drooping almond eye holes with a tiny pinprick of pale light in each, a thin straight mouth slit, one long crack down through the left eye hole, dark tear streaks under the eyes. It is the only pale thing in the frame and it is looking at the camera. He wears the remains of a teenager's clothes that are far too small: a grey hoodie burst at the seams, its sleeves ending at his elbows, its hem at his lower ribs; blue jeans that end at mid-shin, split at the side seams. Pale grey mottled skin tight over ribs and hip bones shows in the gap, a sunken stomach. He is mostly a dark shape: a thin cold rim of light along his shoulders, arms and long fingers, his long shadow reaching along the floor toward the camera. He stands perfectly still. 24mm lens, film grain, deep black shadows, muted cold colour, photographic realism, real skin and cloth. No blood, no wounds.
