Film still, real 35mm photograph, a grey overcast late afternoon in the back lot behind an American high school, flat soft light beginning to fail, a chain-link fence, a bike rack and a green steel dumpster soft in the background. Close shot, chest up, of a high-school senior of 18 crouched by the bike rack, turned three-quarters to the lens: a Latino young man with a light build, light brown skin, dark brown eyes and a wide open grin that uses his whole face, a red knitted beanie pulled low with dark hair curling out under it, a grey hooded sweatshirt with white drawstrings, the straps of a black backpack. He is looking away, off to the side, laughing at something a friend has said, not at what sits on him. On his shoulder nearest the camera sits a small unknown animal the size of a curled-up kitten: one simple round ball of pale cream down like a duckling's, with no visible legs, and on its front a tiny glossy porcelain doll's face no wider than a jam-jar lid, smooth old ivory glaze without a crack, two big dark oval eyes, a thin nose ridge, a tiny painted smile whose right corner curls up. The little face is turned straight to the camera and it is leaning its cheek gently against his cheek, like a pet. He likes it; nothing hurts. On a second look the down at the bottom of the ball parts over pale folded knuckles, like fingers closed into a fist, resting on the grey cloth. Two faces side by side, his alive and warm, the small one glazed and still. 85mm lens, shallow depth of field, film grain, muted natural colour, real skin texture with pores, real down, real glazed ceramic, photographic realism. No blood, no wounds.
