Dark, underexposed horror film still, real 35mm photograph. A long American high school corridor at night seen down its length from a low camera: steel lockers on both walls, ceiling tiles, linoleum with a wet shine, one flickering fluorescent tube at the far end, cold moonlight from a classroom door window on the left, haze in the air; the rest is nearly black. Fifteen steps away, in the middle of the corridor, stands an impossibly tall, gaunt figure, full body in frame from bare feet to head: nearly four metres tall, the tops of the lockers level with his hips, his long neck bent forward so that his head fits under the ceiling tiles. His proportions are those of a long evening shadow: twelve heads tall, a tiny narrow head, narrow shoulders, arms so long that the fingertips hang below his knees, very long thin fingers. He was a young man of 18: a real living body with weight, two long thin legs set a little apart, pale grey mottled skin tight over ribs, collarbones and hip bones, thin ropy muscle and tendons. The remains of his clothes are far too small: a grey hooded sweatshirt with white drawstrings burst at the seams, its sleeves ending at the elbows, its hem at the lower ribs; blue jeans that end at mid-shin, split at the side seams. The porcelain of his face is spreading over him: glossy old ivory glaze, crazed with a net of fine dark cracks like an old china plate, has grown from the mask down the side of his neck, across one shoulder through the torn sweatshirt and down that whole arm to the fingertips, so that arm shines like cracked china in the moonlight while the other arm is still dull living skin; islands of the same glaze have started on his ribs and on one shin. His face is a small slim mask of the same cracked ivory porcelain, narrow and gaunt, hollow cheeks, a pointed chin, two drooping almond eye holes that are pure black with only a pinprick of pale light deep inside each, a thin straight closed mouth slit, a long crack down through the left eye hole, dark tear streaks, turned to the camera. He stands upright and perfectly still, the small head cocked to one side. He is backlit by the far tube, a dark shape with a faint cold edge of light, and the moonlight picks out only the glazed arm and half of the mask. 24mm lens, film grain, deep black shadows, muted cold colour, photographic realism, a real location, real skin, real cloth, real glazed ceramic. No blood, no wounds, clean hands.
