Dark, underexposed horror film still, real 35mm photograph, the last minute of dusk in the empty back lot behind an American high school: wet cracked asphalt, a chain-link fence, two dented green steel dumpsters, a bike rack, a bicycle lying on its side next to him. One sodium yard lamp on a tall pole has just come on directly behind him; nothing lights him from the front. Wide shot from a low camera close to the asphalt, ten steps away, tilted up. A high-school senior of 18 is rising to his feet, but not the way a person stands up: he rises as if lifted by the crown of his head, heels last, arms hanging dead at his sides, and he keeps on rising. He is already far too tall, about three metres, well above the fence and the dumpsters, and too thin, because his body is lengthening: his legs and forearms have grown long, his bare heels have lifted out of his white sneakers, which stay on the asphalt with the laces still tied, his wrists and long forearms stick far out of the cuffs of his grey hooded sweatshirt, the sweatshirt is splitting at the shoulder and side seams and riding up over his lower ribs, his blue jeans stop above his ankles, his fingers hang long and thin and nearly reach his knees, his neck has grown long. He wears a red knitted beanie on a head that now looks small on the long body, and the head is tilting slowly over to one side. His face is turned away into shadow and cannot be seen, only the pale edge of a smooth porcelain cheek. He is a black silhouette against the lamp glow and the dark blue sky, with a thin hard edge of lamplight along his neck, shoulders, arms and fingers; the lamp flares in the haze beside his head; his very long thin shadow slides across the wet asphalt toward the camera. A black backpack lies on the ground behind him. Stillness, no violence. 24mm lens, film grain, deep black shadows, muted colour, photographic realism, a real location, real skin and cloth. No blood, no wounds.
