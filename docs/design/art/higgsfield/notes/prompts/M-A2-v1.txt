Horror film still, real 35mm photograph, dusk, the empty back lot behind an American high school: wet cracked asphalt, a chain-link fence, two dented green steel dumpsters, a bike rack with one bicycle fallen on its side, a brick wall with steel staff doors, the last deep blue light in the sky. One sodium yard lamp on a tall pole burns behind the figure. Wide shot from a low camera, twenty steps away. Standing in the middle of the lot between the camera and the lamp, full body in frame from bare feet to head, is an impossibly tall, impossibly thin figure: nearly four metres tall, almost as tall as the lamp pole, twice the height of the dumpsters and the fence beside him; narrow shoulders, a long thin neck, arms so long that the fingertips hang below his knees, very long thin fingers spread a little, long thin legs. His proportions are those of a long evening shadow: twelve heads tall, with a tiny narrow head at the top. He was a young man of 18 and still wears the remains of his clothes, far too small now: a grey hoodie burst at the seams with sleeves ending at the elbows and the hem at his lower ribs, blue jeans ending at mid-shin; pale grey mottled skin tight over ribs and hip bones shows in the gap. He stands upright, perfectly still, facing the camera, the small head tilted a few degrees to one side. His face is a small slim mask of cracked old ivory porcelain, narrow and gaunt, hollow cheeks, pointed chin, two black drooping eye holes with a tiny pale pinprick of light in each, a thin straight mouth slit: the only pale spot on a dark shape. He is backlit by the lamp: almost a silhouette, an orange rim of light along his shoulders, arms and fingers, his very long thin shadow lying across the wet asphalt toward the camera. On the asphalt in the foreground lie a red knitted beanie and a pair of empty white sneakers with the laces tied. 28mm lens, film grain, deep shadows, muted colour, photographic realism, a real location, real skin and cloth. No blood, no wounds.
