The same being as in the reference image, the same face and the same hands, now seen whole and far away. Dark, underexposed horror film still, real 35mm photograph, night in a long empty American high school corridor with the power out: steel lockers on both walls, a waxed floor, ceiling tiles, one fluorescent tube still lit at the far end behind him, haze in the air; everything else sinks into black. Camera low, near the floor, twenty steps away. Far down the corridor, standing still in the middle of it, a living man grown nearly four metres tall and starved thin: the lockers reach only to his hips, his bony shoulders are pressed under the ceiling tiles so his long neck bends forward and his head hangs just under the ceiling, tilted to one side. A small, very long, very narrow head, no wider than his neck: his face is the long narrow pale face of the reference, old ivory gone leathery, tapering down to a point far below where a chin should be, a wrinkled brow, huge black slanted eye holes with one tiny pinprick of pale light deep in each, two dark streaks running down from the eyes, a small slit of a mouth near the tip. His body is as thin as his face is narrow: pale grey veined skin tight over ribs and collarbones, a long narrow torso, long thin legs. What is left of a grey hoodie, split at the seams, sleeves stopped at the elbows; faded jeans that end at mid-shin, torn at the side seams; bare feet. His arms hang straight down, so long that his big old veined hands with long knuckled fingers hang below his knees. He is backlit by the far tube: a thin cold edge of light on the head, shoulders, arms and fingers, the rest of him nearly a silhouette, only the pale face catching light, looking straight at the camera. Long shadow on the floor toward the lens. 24mm lens, film grain, deep black shadows, muted cold colour, photographic realism, real skin, a real place. Not a costume, not a puppet, not a skeleton, not an alien. No blood, no wounds, no hat.
