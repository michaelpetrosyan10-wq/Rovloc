Horror film still, real 35mm photograph, night inside an American high school corridor. Three-quarter shot from the knees up, from a low camera very close to him, of a boy of 18 whose living body has grown wrong: nearly four metres tall and starved thin, standing upright and far too still, turned three-quarters to the camera, the steel lockers beside him reaching only to his hips. Pale grey mottled living skin stretched tight over ribs and collarbones, thin ropy muscle and tendons, a long thin neck, narrow shoulders, very long thin arms, one huge hand with very long thin fingers hanging in the foreground. A shrunken grey hoodie with white drawstrings, split at the seams, its sleeves ending at the elbows and its hem above the hollow stomach; split blue jeans. His head is small and narrow, no bigger than an ordinary person's head, tiny at the top of such a tall body, carried high on the long neck and tilted slightly to one side, a few lank strands of dark hair hanging beside it. His face is a slim porcelain mask grown into the head: old ivory glaze, narrow, about half as wide as it is long, a little too long and slightly lopsided, hollow sunken cheeks under sharp cheekbones, tapering to a pointed chin; a sculpted brow ridge with no eyebrows; two deep drooping almond eye holes, pure black, each with one tiny pale pinprick of light far inside, the right one slightly larger; a thin nose ridge; a thin closed mouth slit, almost straight, with only its right corner curled slightly up; one long crack from the brow through the left eye hole down to the jaw with hairline cracks branching off it; a chip missing at the right temple; two dark tear streaks under each eye hole. The porcelain has spread: the same old ivory glaze has crept from the mask down the side of his neck, over one shoulder and all the way down that arm to the fingertips, like cracked glaze grown into the skin, crazed with fine cracks, its edge feathering into the living skin like frost on a window. The blank starved face looks down into the lens. The corridor is almost black: cold moonlight through a classroom door window rakes from one side across the glazed arm and half of the mask, the other half is lost in shadow, and a failing fluorescent tube far behind him puts a thin rim on his shoulder. Haze in the air. 28mm lens, natural film grain, deep real shadows, muted cold colour. Photographic realism, a real location, real skin and cloth. No blood, no wounds.
