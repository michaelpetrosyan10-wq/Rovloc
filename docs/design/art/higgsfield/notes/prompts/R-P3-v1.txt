Dark, underexposed horror film still, real 35mm photograph, night in an American high school corridor with the power out, low down by the wall: steel lockers, a scuffed baseboard, and at floor level an open square wall vent, its louvred steel grille lying bent on the floor beside it. The only light is a flashlight beam from the camera, held low, a hard circle with fast falloff; everything outside it is black. Camera at floor height, four steps away. An impossibly tall, gaunt figure, nearly four metres long, is folding himself into the low vent: he is bent double, down on his knees and elbows, his long thin back arched like a bow and pressed under the edge of the lockers, his narrow bony shoulders and one long arm already pushed into the black square of the vent, his other arm bent back on itself along the floor, the big old veined hand with long knuckled fingers spread flat on the floor tiles, joints folded too tightly, wrong. His bony knees are up by his ears. He has stopped, and only his head has turned back on its long neck, over his shoulder, toward the camera, cocked to one side, the body not following. His head is narrow and long, with thin dark hair flat on the skull; his face is not a human face: a very long, very narrow, smooth pale face of old ivory gone leathery, with no nose, tapering down to a point far below where a chin should be, a wrinkled brow, two huge black slanted eye holes with one tiny pinprick of pale light deep in each, two dark streaks running down from them, a small slit of a mouth near the tip. The face is the brightest thing in the beam. Pale grey veined skin tight over the knobs of his spine and his ribs where the cloth has split; what is left of a grey hooded sweatshirt torn open along the back seam; faded jeans burst at the side seams; long bare feet. A few weeks ago he was a high-school senior of 18. Perfect stillness. 35mm lens, film grain, deep black shadows, muted cold colour, photographic realism, a real location, real skin and cloth. Not CG, not a puppet, not a costume, not a skeleton, not a bird, not an insect. No blood, no wounds.
