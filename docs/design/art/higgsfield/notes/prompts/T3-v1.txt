Macro photograph at ground level, real 35mm film, grey overcast daylight, the wet asphalt of a back lot behind a school, the dented green steel side of a dumpster soft in the background. In sharp focus, side by side: a white sneaker with white laces worn by someone standing still, only the shoe and the hem of blue jeans in frame, and, pressed against its toe, a small unknown creature much smaller than the shoe, hand-sized. It has just bumped into the sneaker and now tips its face up toward the person above, head on one side, like a puppy that wants to be picked up. Its body is one simple round ball of pale cream down, soft and slightly damp like a duckling's, with no tail, no ears and no beak. Its face is not an animal's face: set into the front of the down is a tiny pretty human doll's face of hard glossy glazed porcelain, old ivory, without a single crack, no wider than a jam-jar lid, a little narrow and long, with two big dark oval eyes, a thin nose ridge and a tiny thin smile whose right corner curls up. For a moment it is simply cute, small and clumsy. On a second look, under the ball, where legs should be, the down parts over long pale fingers folded tightly together like a closed fist, and two fingertips rest on the wet asphalt like little feet. Tiny water drops on the down; its reflection in the wet asphalt. 100mm macro lens, shallow depth of field, soft flat light, film grain, muted natural colour, a real living thing in a real place, real down, real glazed ceramic, real leather and laces. Not a toy, not a plush, no stitching, no plastic. No blood, no red, no wounds.
